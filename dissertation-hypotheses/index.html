How the original seven map to these statements

The original hypotheses were printed in the dissertation with a mix of directional, predictive, and broad wording. This crosswalk preserves their scope while making the proposed statements specific and non-directional.

H1 Males will consume pornography more frequently than females.
Revised as R1. The displayed sex association is with ever-consumption, not frequency. R1 needs a sex-by-frequency analysis that is not shown in the reported tables.
H2 Higher levels of sexual compulsivity will be positively associated with a higher frequency of pornography consumption.
Revised as R2. The reported ordinal frequency model includes sexual compulsivity, subject to confirming the analysis population, coding, and model assumptions.
H3 Certain personality traits, such as higher conscientiousness, will be associated with greater perceived control over pornography consumption.
Revised as R3 for the specifically named conscientiousness relationship. Other personality-trait associations with control remain exploratory unless separately justified.
H4 Lower levels of neuroticism and agreeableness will be associated with a higher likelihood of consuming pornography.
Revised as R4a and R4b. The two traits are separate statements, and the outcome is ever-consumption, a binary measure, not frequency.
H5 Factors such as sexual compulsivity and personality traits will significantly predict individuals' perceived control over their pornography consumption.
Revised as R3 and R5 for conscientiousness-control and sexual-compulsivity-control. The remaining personality-control questions are exploratory; the model's overall test is not a substitute for each named coefficient.
H6 Higher levels of aggression will be associated with more problematic pornography consumption behaviors.
Proposed as R6a, R6b, and R6c across ever-consumption, frequency, and perceived control. This is an operationalization proposal. The original draft does not define “problematic,” and perceived control is not a clinical measure of problematic use.
H7 Aggression will be positively correlated with sexual compulsivity among individuals who consume pornography.
Revised as R7, retaining the ever-consumer subgroup. The displayed correlation uses the full sample of 214, so it does not establish this subgroup association. A subgroup analysis is needed.

Keep the three outcomes separate

Ever consumed
Screening Q1 is yes/no. R4a, R4b, and R6a refer to this binary outcome. Do not substitute it for frequency.
Consumption frequency
Q3 uses ordered response categories and has no explicit recall window. R1, R2, and R6b refer to frequency. The displayed sex-by-ever-use result does not test R1.
Perceived control
Q8 is a single author-created ordered item with Complete, Some, Little, No, and N/A responses. N/A is not an ordered response category. R3, R5, and R6c refer to this item, not to a validated problematic-use scale.

For R2 through R6c, the existing regression tables list seven predictors. The adjusted version tests the named predictor's coefficient conditional on the other listed predictors, not only whether the whole model is significant. R1 requires a new frequency comparison. R7 requires the specified consumer-only correlation. Neither should be described as already tested by a different outcome or population.

What to confirm before treating this as a protocol

  1. Eligibility and missing answers. The form directs people answering “No” to Q1 to mark related items N/A. A proposed analysis population for frequency and control is Q1=yes with valid applicable responses, pending the codebook and actual output. Keep a “Never” response only if it was validly answered and coded for that group. Do not recode N/A as Never or as complete control.
  2. Scoring and model setup. Verify response order and numeric value labels, reverse-scored BFI-10 items, scale totals or means, missing-item rules, and model-specific N. Check ordinal parallel-lines assumptions, sparse categories and convergence; check binary-model separation and predictor form. The codebook/output is needed to interpret coefficient signs.
  3. Retrospective reporting. These ten statements were drafted after the existing results were available. Keep them separate from the historical hypotheses, retain nonsignificant relationships, report the full tested set, and do not select statements because their p-values are small.
  4. Design limits. The study is cross-sectional, convenience-sampled, and self-reported. It supports association questions, not causal effects or mediation. Wording alone cannot guarantee measurement or design validity, establish power from N=214 alone, or justify generalization to all young adults.
  5. If this is a new study. Justify the hypotheses from independent literature, define outcomes, eligibility, analysis and the test family before collecting new data, and consider prospective registration. A new protocol should not be presented as if it had governed the completed study.
