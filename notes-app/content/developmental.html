Trap

Critical = rigid biological window (lost if missed). Sensitive = optimal but flexible. Lorenz's imprinting = critical. Language learning = sensitive (you can still learn L2 at 30, just harder).

2. Prenatal Development and Teratogens

Three prenatal stages

StageDurationKey event
Germinal0-2 weeksZygote to blastocyst to implantation
Embryonic2-8 weeksOrganogenesis; most vulnerable to teratogens
Fetal8 weeks to birthGrowth and refinement; viability ~24 weeks

Teratogens (high-yield)

  • Alcohol — Fetal Alcohol Spectrum Disorder (FASD); FAS = facial anomalies, growth restriction, CNS damage. Most common preventable cause of intellectual disability.
  • Tobacco — low birth weight, prematurity.
  • Thalidomide — limb malformations (1950s-60s, classic teratogen exam case).
  • Rubella — heart, eye, ear defects in 1st trimester.
  • Lead, mercury — CNS damage.
  • Zika — microcephaly.
Trap

The embryonic period (weeks 2-8) is most vulnerable, not the fetal period. Organs are forming. By the fetal period, organs already exist and growth is the main task.

3. Piaget: Cognitive Development

The 4 stages

StageAgeAchievementsLimits
Sensorimotor0-2Object permanence (~8mo), goal-directed action, deferred imitation (end)No symbolic thought early on
Preoperational2-7Symbolic thought, language, pretend playEgocentrism, animism, centration, no conservation
Concrete Operational7-11Conservation, reversibility, classification, seriation, decentrationTied to concrete reality; abstract is shaky
Formal Operational11+Abstract, hypothetico-deductive, propositional logicAdolescent egocentrism (imaginary audience, personal fable)

Sensorimotor: the 6 substages

  1. Reflexive schemes (0-1mo)
  2. Primary circular reactions (1-4mo, on own body)
  3. Secondary circular reactions (4-8mo, on objects)
  4. Coordination of secondary schemes (8-12mo, intentional, object permanence, A-not-B error)
  5. Tertiary circular reactions (12-18mo, experimentation)
  6. Mental representation (18-24mo, deferred imitation, symbolic thought)

Key concepts

  • Schema — mental framework for understanding the world.
  • Assimilation — fitting new info into an existing schema (a horse = a "doggie").
  • Accommodation — modifying the schema for new info ("ok, that's a horse, not a doggie").
  • Equilibration — the drive to balance assimilation and accommodation; the engine of cognitive change.
  • Egocentrism — preoperational; can't take another's perspective. Three Mountains task.
  • Animism — preoperational; attributing life to objects.
  • Centration — focus on one feature at a time; blocks conservation.
  • Conservation — quantity stays the same despite a change in form (number, mass, volume). Order: number then mass then liquid then length then area then weight then volume (last, ~11).
Trap

Object permanence (sensorimotor, ~8mo) is NOT conservation (concrete operational, ~7yr). Different stages, different concepts. Also: "egocentrism" in dev psych is a cognitive limit, not narcissism or a personality trait.

Critiques of Piaget

  • Underestimated infants (modern habituation studies show object permanence earlier).
  • Stages less discrete than he claimed (horizontal décalage = uneven performance within a stage).
  • Underplayed culture (Vygotsky's main attack).
  • Not everyone reaches formal operational, especially for unfamiliar content.

4. Vygotsky: Sociocultural Theory

  • ZPD (Zone of Proximal Development) — the gap between what a child can do alone vs with help from a more knowledgeable other (MKO).
  • Scaffolding — temporary, tailored support, withdrawn as competence grows. This is Bruner's term, applied to Vygotsky's framework.
  • Private speech — kids talk to themselves while problem-solving. It becomes inner speech and guides cognition. Piaget called this "egocentric speech" and dismissed it; Vygotsky said it is central.
  • Tools of thought — language is the master tool, internalized from social interaction. Higher mental functions are socially mediated.
  • Cultural specificity — cognitive development depends on the cultural tools available (counting systems, writing, schooling).
Trap

"Scaffolding" is Bruner, not Vygotsky. Vygotsky's own terms are ZPD and MKO.

High-yield

Piaget vs Vygotsky in one line: Piaget = child as a lone scientist (development drives learning). Vygotsky = child as an apprentice (learning drives development, mediated by culture).

5. Bronfenbrenner: Ecological Systems / Bioecological Model

Core idea

  • Urie Bronfenbrenner (1917-2005), Russian-born American developmental psychologist.
  • Development is shaped by ongoing interaction between the person and a set of nested environmental systems.
  • Originally Ecological Systems Theory, from "The Ecology of Human Development" (1979).
  • Later renamed the Bioecological Model (1994, refined 2005) to give biology more weight and add the PPCT mechanism.
  • Co-founded the Head Start program (1965) in the US, an applied use of the theory.
High-yield

One-line answer: Development = person × proximal processes × nested context × time.

The 5 systems (innermost to outermost)

5.1 Microsystem

  • Immediate settings with direct face-to-face contact; the most direct effect on development.
  • Examples: family, school, peer group, neighbourhood, daycare, workplace (for adults).
  • Influence is bidirectional: the child shapes the environment, and the environment shapes the child.

5.2 Mesosystem

  • Interactions between two or more microsystems. Not a setting itself; a system of microsystems.
  • Examples: parent-teacher communication, family-peer overlap, home-daycare and home-school links.
  • Strong, supportive mesosystem links predict better developmental outcomes.

5.3 Exosystem

  • Settings the child does not directly participate in, but which still affect them via the microsystem.
  • Examples: parent's workplace, school board decisions, mass media, parent's social network, local government policy.

5.4 Macrosystem

  • The overarching cultural blueprint, the "cultural envelope" around all the other systems.
  • Examples: ideologies, customs, laws, religion, SES, cultural values, the economic system, war/peace.
  • Does not refer to a specific setting; refers to the cultural patterning that shapes all settings.

5.5 Chronosystem

  • The time dimension. Added later, after the original 4 systems.
  • Three flavours of "time" effects:
    • Life transitions: normative (school entry, puberty) and non-normative (parents' divorce, death of a parent).
    • Historical events the person lives through (pandemic, war, recession).
    • Sociohistorical conditions of the cohort (cohort effects, the sociohistorical era).

Quick-reference table

SystemWhat it isExamples

MesosystemInteractions between microsystems (a system of microsystems, not a setting itself)Parent-teacher meeting, home-school link, family-peer overlap

PPCT Model (1994 refinement, often asked)

Bronfenbrenner refined the theory into the Bioecological Model using the PPCT mechanism. This is the engine of the modern version.

Process — proximal processes

  • The engine of development, the most important driver.
  • Regular, reciprocal interactions over extended time between the person and the people, objects, and symbols in the immediate environment.
  • Examples: parent-child play, reading together, sustained peer activity, learning a skill from a mentor.
  • Must be regular and over long stretches of time to count as proximal processes, not one-off events.

Person — individual traits

Three sub-types:

Sub-typeDefinitionExamples
Demand characteristicsVisible traits that pull responses from othersAge, gender, skin colour, physical appearance
Resource characteristicsSkills, abilities, experiencesIntelligence, prior knowledge, social skills
Force characteristicsTemperament, motivation, persistenceCuriosity, drive, distractibility, regulation

Context — the 5 nested systems

Micro / Meso / Exo / Macro / Chrono, as covered above.

Time

  • Micro-time: continuity vs interruption within an activity (e.g. an uninterrupted reading session).
  • Meso-time: consistency of activities across days and weeks.
  • Macro-time: the chronosystem level, sociohistorical change across the lifespan.

High-yield exam pointers

  • Originally 4 systems; the chronosystem was added later.
  • Bidirectionality is a key examiner trigger word, especially in microsystem questions.
  • Two phases of theory: Ecological Systems Theory (1979) then Bioecological Model (1994) with PPCT.
  • Proximal processes are the engine, not the context. Context only modifies how proximal processes operate.
  • The mesosystem is not a setting; it is a system of relationships between settings.

High-yield

If the stem mentions nested environments, cultural context, a parent's workplace affecting the child, or indirect influence, the answer is Bronfenbrenner.

Applications

  • Child welfare policy — interventions at multiple system levels (family + school + community).
  • Inclusive education — coordinating microsystems (home, school, peers).
  • Public health — tobacco control, vaccination uptake (macro + exo levels).
  • Family therapy — recognising that the family microsystem sits inside larger systems.
  • Head Start program (Bronfenbrenner co-founded) — early childhood intervention targeting micro + meso systems.

Critique

  • Hard to operationalise and test empirically; too many simultaneous variables.
  • Originally underplayed biology (fixed in the bioecological revision via the "Person" component).
  • Criticised for being descriptive more than predictive; doesn't say which system dominates when.
  • Cultural specificity is unclear; the macrosystem treats "culture" as a single bucket.
Trap

Exosystem does NOT mean no influence. The child isn't in it, but it shapes them through their microsystem (e.g. dad loses his job, stress rises at home, the child's development is affected).

Trap

Mass media affecting a child = exosystem, not macrosystem. Media is a setting, not a cultural blueprint. By contrast, "culture, religion, ideology, SES" = macrosystem; "parent's workplace" = exosystem; "sibling fight at home" = microsystem; "parent-teacher meeting" = mesosystem.

Trap

The chronosystem was added later. Older textbooks list only 4 systems. If a question says "Bronfenbrenner originally proposed 5 systems," that is false. PPCT belongs to the Bioecological Model (1994), not the original 1979 theory.

Gilligan's critique: Kohlberg's stages reflect a male "justice" orientation; women often reason via an "ethics of care" (relationships, responsibility). Gilligan added a parallel orientation; she did not replace Kohlberg.

Disorganized (D) was added by Main and Solomon, not Ainsworth. Ainsworth's original 3 = A/B/C.

Adult attachment (Main, AAI)

  • Secure-Autonomous (corresponds to B)
  • Dismissing (corresponds to A)
  • Preoccupied (corresponds to C)
  • Unresolved / disorganized (corresponds to D)

Foundational attachment experiments

  • Harlow — wire vs cloth mother monkeys. Contact comfort beats food. Demolished the pure drive-reduction view of attachment.
  • Lorenz — imprinting in geese, the classic critical period example.
  • Bowlby's WHO Report (1951) — the "maternal deprivation hypothesis"; institutional rearing harms development. Influenced post-WWII childcare policy.

11. Baumrind: Parenting Styles

High demandLow demand
High warmthAuthoritative (best outcomes)Permissive / Indulgent
Low warmthAuthoritarianNeglectful / Uninvolved
  • Authoritative — high warmth + high demand, clear limits, explanations. Best child outcomes (competence, self-regulation, school).
  • Authoritarian — high demand, low warmth, "because I said so." Linked to lower self-esteem, more anxiety.
  • Permissive — high warmth, few demands. Linked to impulsivity, lower achievement.
  • Neglectful — low on both (added by Maccoby and Martin as the 4th style). Worst outcomes.
Trap

Authoritative is NOT authoritarian. Authoritative = warm + firm (good). Authoritarian = cold + harsh (bad). The words look similar; the outcomes are opposite.

12. Thomas and Chess: Temperament

  • Easy (~40%) — regular routines, positive mood, adapts to change.
  • Difficult (~10%) — irregular, intense reactions, slow to adapt.
  • Slow-to-warm-up (~15%) — low activity, mild reactions, gradual to adapt.
  • The remaining ~35% don't fit cleanly into one category.
High-yield

Goodness of fit (Thomas and Chess): the match between a child's temperament and environmental demands predicts adjustment, not temperament alone. Outcome depends on temperament × environment.

14. Adolescence

  • Puberty — primary (gonads) and secondary (hair, voice) sex characteristics. Girls typically mature 1-2 years earlier than boys.
  • Adolescent egocentrism (Elkind) — the imaginary audience (everyone is watching me) and the personal fable (I am unique and immune to harm).
  • Identity — the central task (Erikson stage 5); Marcia's 4 statuses.
  • Risk-taking — the limbic system matures before the prefrontal cortex (the dual systems model).
  • Storm and stress (G. Stanley Hall) — an older view; modern data show most adolescents are not in chronic conflict.

15. Aging and Late Adulthood

  • Primary aging — biological (sensory decline, slower processing).
  • Secondary aging — disease and lifestyle (preventable).
  • Crystallized intelligence — preserved or improves with age (vocab, knowledge).
  • Fluid intelligence — declines (processing speed, novel problem-solving). The Cattell-Horn-Carroll model.
  • Socioemotional selectivity theory (Carstensen) — older adults prioritize emotionally meaningful goals as their time horizon shrinks.
  • Kübler-Ross 5 stages of dying — denial, anger, bargaining, depression, acceptance. Often misunderstood as universal/linear; she did not claim that.

17. Top MCQ Traps and 30-Second Recall

  1. Object permanence (sensorimotor) is NOT conservation (concrete operational).
  2. Critical period (rigid, biological) vs sensitive period (flexible).
  3. Authoritative is NOT authoritarian. One word apart, opposite outcomes.
  4. Disorganized attachment was Main and Solomon, not Ainsworth.
  5. "Scaffolding" is Bruner's term, applied to Vygotsky's ZPD.
  6. Bronfenbrenner exosystem = child not in it, but still affected (parent's workplace).
  7. Bronfenbrenner mesosystem = link between microsystems, not a setting itself.
  8. Chronosystem was added later; originally 4 systems.
  9. PPCT = Bioecological Model (1994), not the original 1979 theory.
  10. Gilligan added "ethics of care"; she did not replace Kohlberg.
  11. Overregularization ("goed") is rule-learning, not regression.
  12. The embryonic period (weeks 2-8) is most teratogen-vulnerable, not the fetal period.
  13. FAS / FASD = the most common preventable cause of intellectual disability.
  14. Egocentrism (Piaget) = a cognitive limit, not a personality trait.
  15. Goodness of fit (Thomas and Chess) — the outcome depends on the temperament × environment match.
