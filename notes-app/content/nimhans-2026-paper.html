1. Nagy's Family Systems (Contextual Family Therapy)

Core: Ivan Boszormenyi-Nagy founded Contextual Family Therapy, built on relational ethics and the multigenerational ledger of fairness.

  • Invisible loyalties — unconscious commitments to family of origin that drive present behavior across generations.
  • Ledger of entitlements and indebtedments — a running balance of what each member has given vs. received; injustice ("destructive entitlement") gets passed down.
  • Relational ethics — fairness/trustworthiness is the deepest level of family life (above facts, psychology, and transactions).
  • Four dimensions of relational reality: (1) facts, (2) individual psychology, (3) transactional patterns/systems, (4) relational ethics.
  • Multidirected partiality — the therapist's stance: sequentially siding with each member so everyone is heard (not neutral, not one-sided).

Hook: Nagy = "ledger + invisible loyalties + relational ethics."

Trap: Don't confuse the family-therapy founders — see table.

FounderSchoolSignature concept
Boszormenyi-NagyContextualLedger, invisible loyalties, relational ethics
Murray BowenBowenian / intergenerationalDifferentiation of self, triangles, genogram
Salvador MinuchinStructuralBoundaries, subsystems, enmeshment/disengagement
Jay HaleyStrategicDirectives, paradox, power/hierarchy
Virginia SatirExperiential / communicationCommunication stances, family sculpting, self-esteem

2. CGI Scale (Clinical Global Impression)

Core: A clinician-rated global measure of illness severity and treatment response, scored on simple 7-point scales.

  • CGI-S (Severity): 1 = normal/not ill → 7 = among the most extremely ill. Rated relative to the clinician's experience with that disorder.
  • CGI-I (Improvement): 1 = very much improved, 4 = no change, 7 = very much worse (rated against baseline).
  • CGI-E / Efficacy Index: therapeutic effect weighed against side effects.
  • Developed at the NIMH; widely used as a quick global anchor in clinical trials.

Trap: CGI is clinician-rated and global; contrast with disorder-specific scales (PANSS, HAM-D, Y-BOCS). "4" means no change on CGI-I, not "moderate."

3. AUDIT Scale (Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test)

Core: A WHO-developed 10-item screen for hazardous/harmful alcohol use; total score 0–40.

  • Items 1–3 = consumption; 4–6 = dependence; 7–10 = alcohol-related harm.
  • Cut-off ≥ 8 indicates hazardous/harmful drinking (commonly cited). Higher bands suggest likely dependence (often ≥ 15–20).
  • AUDIT-C = the 3 consumption items only, a brief version.

Trap: AUDIT is WHO, prospective/current use, 10 items. Don't confuse with CAGE (4 questions, lifetime), MAST (longer), or SADQ (severity of dependence).

4. RAVLT — Dimensions / What It Measures

Core: The Rey Auditory Verbal Learning Test assesses verbal learning and memory using a 15-word list across repeated trials.

  • Structure: List A read 5 times (Trials 1–5), then interference List B, then immediate recall of A (Trial 6), then delayed recall (~20–30 min) and recognition.
  • Dimensions captured: immediate memory span (Trial 1), learning curve/acquisition (Trials 1→5), proactive & retroactive interference, retention/forgetting (delayed vs. Trial 5), and recognition memory.
  • Sensitive to medial temporal / hippocampal dysfunction (e.g., Alzheimer's, amnesia).

Trap: RAVLT = auditory/verbal list learning. The visual analogue is the Rey-Osterrieth Complex Figure (visuospatial memory + construction). Don't mix them.

5. Mental Healthcare Act, 2017 (India)

Core: India's MHCA 2017 (in force 2018) replaced the Mental Health Act 1987; rights-based, aligned with the UN CRPD.

  • Decriminalised attempted suicide (Sec 115) — presumed to be under severe stress, not punished.
  • Advance Directive — a person may state how they wish to be treated (and a Nominated Representative).
  • Bans unmodified ECT, ECT for minors (except with board approval), and sterilisation as treatment.
  • Mental Health Review Boards (MHRB); Central & State Mental Health Authorities.
  • Rights to access, confidentiality, community living, and free treatment for the poor/homeless.

Trap: "MHA" = Mental Healthcare Act 2017 in the Indian exam context. Key year traps: passed 2017, effective 2018; repealed the 1987 Act. Capacity-based, not custodial.

6. Tests Notified in the Gazette of India

Core: Likely tests the RCI Act, 1992 framework — only RCI-recognised qualifications and registered professionals may practise; recognition is notified in the Gazette of India.

  • The Rehabilitation Council of India (RCI) maintains the Central Rehabilitation Register and notifies recognised courses (e.g., M.Phil Clinical Psychology) via Gazette notifications.
  • Under the RPwD Act 2016 and assessment rules, the specific tests/tools used for disability certification (intellectual disability, autism, specific learning disability) are notified by Gazette — e.g., for ID certification, IQ + adaptive-behaviour tools standardised on Indian populations.
  • Practical reading: psychometric tests usable for legal/certification purposes in India must be officially notified/standardised, not arbitrary.

Flag (moderate confidence): The exact "tests in the Gazette" depend on the question's framing (RCI recognition of qualifications vs. notified disability-assessment tools). Core link to remember: RCI Act 1992 + Gazette notification = legal recognition.

7. Levodopa (L-DOPA)

Core: A dopamine precursor that crosses the blood-brain barrier (dopamine itself cannot) and is the gold-standard treatment for Parkinson's disease.

  • Converted to dopamine by DOPA decarboxylase (AADC); given with carbidopa/benserazide (peripheral decarboxylase inhibitors) to reduce peripheral side effects and boost central delivery.
  • Pathway it boosts: nigrostriatal dopamine (substantia nigra → striatum), the system depleted in Parkinson's.
  • Long-term problems: motor fluctuations ("wearing-off", "on-off"), dyskinesias; can induce psychosis/hallucinations.
  • Psych link: dopamine excess theory of schizophrenia — too much dopamine activity can produce psychotic symptoms.

Trap: Levodopa ≠ dopamine agonist. Antipsychotics (D2 blockers) do the opposite and can cause drug-induced parkinsonism; levodopa can worsen psychosis.

8. Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS)

Core: Standard scale for level of consciousness, especially after head injury. Score range 3 (deep coma) to 15 (fully alert).

ComponentRangeBest response
Eye opening1–44 = spontaneous
Verbal response1–55 = oriented
Motor response1–66 = obeys commands
  • Severity: Mild 13–15, Moderate 9–12, Severe ≤ 8 (≤ 8 = "intubate").
  • Lowest possible = 3, never 0. Devised by Teasdale & Jennett (Glasgow, 1974).

Trap: Eye opening is max 4, verbal max 5, motor max 6 (4+5+6 = 15). Minimum is 3, not 0.

9. Minuchin — Structural Family Therapy

Core: Salvador Minuchin founded Structural Family Therapy; the family's structure (boundaries, subsystems, hierarchy) maintains symptoms.

  • Subsystems: spousal, parental, sibling.
  • Boundaries: clear (healthy), rigid → disengagement, diffuse → enmeshment.
  • Psychosomatic family features: enmeshment, overprotectiveness, rigidity, conflict avoidance (studied in anorexia, asthma, diabetes).
  • Techniques: joining, enactment, boundary-making, unbalancing, reframing.

Hook: Minuchin = "maps, boundaries, enmeshment."

Trap: Minuchin (structural, boundaries) vs. Bowen (differentiation/triangles) vs. Haley (strategic/directives) vs. Nagy (contextual/ledger). The spelling "Munichin" in recall = Minuchin.

10. Durkheim's Theory of Suicide

Core: Émile Durkheim (1897, Le Suicide) explained suicide sociologically via two axes: social integration and social regulation.

TypeCauseExample
EgoisticToo low integrationIsolated, unmarried, weak social ties
AltruisticToo high integrationSelf-sacrifice for the group (e.g., soldier, sati)
AnomicToo low regulationSudden disruption (economic crash, divorce)
FatalisticToo high regulationOppressive control (prisoners, slaves)

Hook: Integration low/high = Egoistic/Altruistic; Regulation low/high = Anomic/Fatalistic.

Trap: Durkheim is sociological, not psychological. Contrast with Freud (aggression turned inward), Shneidman (psychache), Beck (hopelessness), Joiner (interpersonal theory: thwarted belonging + perceived burdensomeness + acquired capability).

12. National Mental Health Survey (NMHS) — Prevalence

Core: The NMHS of India 2015–16 (led by NIMHANS, Bengaluru) is the landmark Indian epidemiological survey.

  • Current prevalence of mental disorders ~10.6%; lifetime prevalence ~13.7%.
  • Treatment gap very high: ~70–92% depending on disorder (overall ~83%; severe mental disorders ~75%).
  • ~1 in 20 Indians suffers from depression; alcohol use disorder notably high in men.
  • Higher burden in urban metros; large gender, age, and income gradients.

Trap: Lock the headline numbers: current 10.6%, lifetime 13.7%, treatment gap ~83%, year 2015–16, conducted by NIMHANS. Don't quote the WHO global figure.

13. Functional Hallucinations

Core: A hallucination that occurs simultaneously with, and triggered by, a real external stimulus in the same modality — and both are perceived together.

  • Example: hearing a voice only while a tap runs, both the running water and the voice perceived at once.
  • Key distinction from reflex hallucination: in functional, stimulus and hallucination are in the same modality; in reflex, a stimulus in one modality triggers a hallucination in a different modality.
TypeDefinition
FunctionalReal stimulus + hallucination, same modality, perceived together
ReflexStimulus in one modality → hallucination in another
ExtracampineHallucination outside the sensory field (e.g., seeing behind one's head)
AutoscopicSeeing oneself / a double
Hypnagogic / HypnopompicWhile falling asleep / on waking (can be normal)

Trap: Functional = stimulus and hallucination coexist in the same sense; if it stops when the stimulus stops, that's the cue. Reflex = cross-modality.

14. PANSS (Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale)

Core: A 30-item clinician-rated scale for schizophrenia symptom severity (Kay, Fiszbein & Opler, 1987).

  • 3 subscales: Positive (7 items), Negative (7 items), General Psychopathology (16 items).
  • Each item scored 1 (absent) to 7 (extreme); total range 30–210.
  • Requires a semi-structured interview; gold standard in schizophrenia trials.

Trap: 30 items, 7-point items, minimum total 30 (not 0). Contrast with SAPS/SANS (separate positive/negative scales, Andreasen) and BPRS (18–24 items, broader).

15. WASI (Wechsler Abbreviated Scale of Intelligence)

Core: A brief, screening measure of intelligence yielding FSIQ, derived from the Wechsler tradition.

  • WASI / WASI-II has 4 subtests: Vocabulary, Similarities (Verbal Comprehension); Block Design, Matrix Reasoning (Perceptual Reasoning).
  • Can give a 2-subtest FSIQ (Vocabulary + Matrix Reasoning) or a 4-subtest FSIQ.
  • Ages ~6–90; used when a full battery isn't feasible.

Trap: WASI = abbreviated/screening, not the full battery. Don't confuse with WAIS (full adult), WISC (children), WPPSI (preschool), or WMS (memory). "Weschler" in recall = Wechsler.

16. Attunement (Affect Attunement)

Core: Daniel Stern's concept of affect attunement — the caregiver matching/sharing the infant's internal emotional state (often cross-modally), central to intersubjectivity and the developing sense of self.

  • Part of Stern's senses of self (emergent, core, subjective/intersubjective, verbal). Affect attunement appears with the subjective/intersubjective self (~7–9 months).
  • Distinct from simple imitation: attunement matches the quality/intensity/contour of affect, not the literal behavior (e.g., matching a baby's vocal burst with a body movement of the same intensity).
  • Related: mirroring (Kohut/Winnicott), mentalization (Fonagy), marked mirroring, contingent responsiveness — all feed secure attachment.

Flag (low confidence on exact wording): Sihan's recall ("attunement workup and another dimension is a part of what") was garbled. Most likely intent: affect attunement as one component of Stern's intersubjective self / dimensions of the self, or attunement as part of an assessment of the caregiver-infant relationship. Anchor: Stern, affect attunement, cross-modal, intersubjectivity.

17. R² in Multiple Regression

Core: R² (coefficient of determination) = the proportion of variance in the dependent variable explained by the set of predictors. Ranges 0 to 1.

  • R² = 0.40 → the predictors jointly explain 40% of the variance in Y.
  • In multiple regression, R² is the square of the multiple correlation R (correlation between observed and predicted Y).
  • Adjusted R² penalises for the number of predictors — use it when comparing models, because raw R² never decreases when predictors are added.
  • 1 − R² = the coefficient of alienation (unexplained variance).

Trap: R² is variance explained, not correlation strength itself and not causation. Adding predictors always inflates R²; adjusted R² can go down. A high R² with poor predictors can be spurious/overfit.

18. Floortime (DIR Model) as an Intervention

Core: Floortime is the core technique of the DIR (Developmental, Individual-difference, Relationship-based) model by Stanley Greenspan & Serena Wieder, used mainly for Autism Spectrum Disorder and developmental delays.

  • Relationship- and play-based, child-led: the adult joins the child's activity/interest and "opens and closes circles of communication" to expand engagement.
  • Targets functional emotional developmental capacities (regulation, engagement, two-way communication, complex problem-solving, symbolic/abstract thinking).
  • Emphasises affect and relationships as the engine of development, not drilled skills.

Trap: Floortime/DIR is developmental & relationship-based. Contrast with ABA (behavioral, reinforcement-based), TEACCH (structured teaching/environment), PECS (picture exchange communication), and ESDM (blends developmental + ABA). The cut-off "...intervention t" almost certainly = "for autism."

28. Primary, Secondary, Tertiary Prevention (Caplan)

Core: Gerald Caplan's public-health model of prevention, defined by when the intervention occurs relative to disorder onset.

LevelTimingGoalPsych example
PrimaryBefore onsetReduce incidence (new cases)Parenting programs, stress-management workshops, anti-stigma campaigns
SecondaryEarly stageReduce prevalence via early detection & prompt treatmentDepression screening, suicide-risk screening, early psychosis (EIP) services
TertiaryAfter established illnessReduce disability/impairment, prevent relapsePsychosocial rehabilitation, relapse prevention, vocational rehab

Hook: Primary = prevent (incidence) · Secondary = screen (prevalence) · Tertiary = rehab (disability).

Trap (the favourite compare): Don't confuse Caplan's model with Gordon's / IOM framework, which classifies prevention by target population: Universal (everyone), Selective (at-risk groups), Indicated (high-risk individuals with early signs). Caplan = timing; Gordon = who you target. Some add quaternary prevention = protecting patients from over-medicalisation/iatrogenic harm.

29. Primary Auditory Cortex → Heschl's Gyrus

Core / answer: The primary auditory cortex is Heschl's gyrus (transverse temporal gyrus) on the superior surface of the superior temporal gyrus, Brodmann areas 41 and 42.

  • Tonotopic organisation: different frequencies map to different locations (low frequencies anterolateral, high frequencies posteromedial).
  • Receives input from the medial geniculate nucleus of the thalamus.
Option on paperWhat it actually is
Heschl's gyrus ✓Primary auditory cortex (BA 41/42) — correct answer
Supramarginal gyrusInferior parietal (BA 40); phonological processing, part of Wernicke's area / language
Angular gyrusInferior parietal (BA 39); reading, writing, semantic integration (lesion → Gerstmann syndrome)
Fusiform gyrusVentral temporal/occipital (BA 37); face & word-form recognition (lesion → prosopagnosia)

Trap: Distinguish auditory areas: Heschl's gyrus = primary auditory (hearing); Wernicke's area (BA 22, posterior STG) = auditory comprehension; Broca's area (BA 44/45, inferior frontal) = speech production. The "supra-something" decoy is the supramarginal gyrus; the "fusiform" decoy is faces, not sound.
