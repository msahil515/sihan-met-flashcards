1 · Foundations & Approaches to Psychopathology

Different Terms

  • Abnormal: outlining atypicality. Abnormality depends on the intensity and frequency of behaviour.
  • Psychopathology: part of abnormality — the medical angle of the abnormal (involves neuroimaging, aetiology, diagnosis, prognosis).
  • Clinical Psychology: application of psychological principles in the treatment of mental disorders.
The 4 D's of Abnormality: Deviance (social and statistical), Distress (causing unhappiness), Dysfunction (unable to show adaptive behaviour), Danger (to oneself and others).

History of Clinical Psychology

  • Historically the mentally ill were tied, chained and kept in inhumane conditions.
  • 1826 — Philippe Pinel introduced moral therapy.
  • 1802–1887 — Dorothea Dix advocated for humane treatment.
  • APA founded 1892; first president G. Stanley Hall.
  • Lightner Witmer — founder of clinical psychology; opened the first clinic.
  • DSM introduced in 1952; ICD-6 in 1952. Current diagnosis is more spectrum-oriented.
Named figures. Pinel (moral therapy), Dorothea Dix (humane treatment advocacy), G. Stanley Hall (first APA president), Lightner Witmer (founder of clinical psychology, first clinic).

2 · Case History & Mental Status Examination

Case History

Four assessment techniques are used: Observation, Conversation, Exploration, Testing.

Personal information: name, father's/husband's name; sociodemographic profile (age, sex, education, occupation, socio-economic status, marital status, religion, residence, mother tongue, address); source of referral; type of admission; identification marks.

Informant reliability — the 5 C's (SIMS): Contact (between patient and informant), Closeness of relationship, Continuity of the account, Consistency of the verbatim, Corroborativeness between sources.

History of Present Illness — the factors

FactorDefinition
PredisposingFactors operating from early life that determine vulnerability to develop a disorder
PrecipitatingEvents that occur shortly before the onset of a disorder
PerpetuatingFactors due to which the disorder is maintained or aggravated
LimitingFactors which limit the illness from extensive progress
ModifyingFactors which modify the natural or expected course of the illness
  • Mode of onset: abrupt, acute, insidious.
  • Course of illness: continuous, episodic, fluctuating.
  • Progress of illness: improving, deteriorating, static.
  • Compliance: the extent to which the patient's behaviour (taking medications, following diets, other lifestyle changes) coincides with medical recommendations.

Also covered: treatment history, past history (medical & psychiatric), family history (genogram, consanguinity), personal history (birth & early development, childhood disorders, home atmosphere, scholastic/extracurricular, occupational, menstrual, sexual & marital, forensic), premorbid personality (individualized styles of dealing with the environment characteristic of a person prior to onset), and physical examination (by physician).

Mental Status Examination (MSE)

The part of clinical assessment describing the sum total of the examiner's observations and impressions of a psychiatric patient at the time of interview.

Attitude towards the examiner

Frank and Cooperative / Attentive (normal). Defensive & Guarded (Paranoid Schizophrenia, Delusional disorder). Evasive (Organic psychoses, Substance abuse). Hostile (Paranoid Schizophrenia, Antisocial PD). Seductive.

Rapport

A bidirectional empathetic relationship. Easily established — normal persons, Mania; Established with difficulty — Schizophrenia, Dementia, Depression; Not possible — Paranoid Schizophrenia, Dementia, Depression.

Speech

  • Intensity: excessively loud (Mania, Hypomania), abnormally soft (vocal cord palsy, Depression, Paranoid Schizophrenia).
  • Pitch: monotonous (parietal lobe damage, chronic schizophrenia, depression).
  • Reaction time: increased (hypothyroidism, depression, schizophrenia), decreased (Mania).
  • Speed: very slow (depression, dementia, hypothyroidism); rapid with prolixity/flight of ideas (mania, hypomania, hyperthyroidism). Pressure of speech — increased, accelerated, hard to interrupt (Mania, Schizophrenia, cognitive disorders).
  • Ease: hesitant (head injury, anxiety), mutism (stupor, dementia), slurring (cerebellar damage), stuttering/stammering (severe anxiety, schizophrenia), whispering (pseudobulbar palsy), muttering (schizophrenia, dementia).
  • Deviations: rhyming/punning (mania), talking past the point (Ganser's syndrome, schizophrenia), clang association (schizophrenia, mania), stereotypy (catatonic schizophrenia), perseveration.

Cognitive Functions

Orientation (time, place, person), attention, concentration, memory (immediate / recent / remote), abstract ability, intelligence (comprehension, general information, vocabulary, calculation).

Thinking styleDefinition & association
Concrete thinkingThinking bound to actual things/events & immediate experience — Schizophrenia, young children
Functional thinkingAbstraction relating only to functional aspects — Schizophrenia, MR
Over-abstractionExcessive manifestation of a concept beyond relevance — Schizophrenia, philosophical preoccupations

Mood & Affect

Mood = persistent, sustained emotion colouring perception of the world. Affect = present emotional responsiveness inferred from expression.

  • Quality: dysphoric, anxious, irritable, depressed, elevated, euphoric, elated, exalted, ecstatic, euthymic.
  • Intensity: shallow (lack of depth), blunted (greatly diminished, uniform voice), flat (no affect displayed).
  • Appropriateness: Paramimia — lack of unity between modes of emotional expression; Parathymia — expression opposite to what is expected (e.g. laughing at tragic news).
  • Diurnal variation: worse in morning (endogenous depression, headache), worse in evening (anxiety, delirium/sundowning), worse at night (uremia).

Thought

AspectKey terms
StreamAcceleration, pressured speech, flight of ideas (rapid, chance connections still understandable), prolixity (ordered flight, returns to task), retardation/bradyphrenia, poverty of speech (laconic), circumstantiality (reaches point via trivia), tangentiality (oblique, never reaches point), thought blocking (sudden arrest → blank)
FormNegative type (loses ability to form concept) vs positive type (false concept by blending incongruous elements): loosening of associations, derailment, neologism, over-inclusion
PossessionObsession, rumination, compulsion, thought alienation — insertion, withdrawal, broadcasting, thought echo

Perception

  • Sensory distortion — intensity: hyperaesthesia, hyperacusis, hypoacusis.
  • Quality: Xanthopsia (yellow), Chloropsia (green), Erythropsia (red/pink).
  • Spatial form: Micropsia (smaller), Macropsia/megalopsia (larger), Porropsia (retreat into distance without size change).
  • Sensory deception — Hallucination: perception without an adequate external stimulus (auditory, visual, olfactory, gustatory, tactile).
Delusion vs Hallucination. A delusion is a false firm belief (a disorder of thought content). A hallucination is a perception without an adequate external stimulus (a disorder of perception). An illusion is a misperception of a real stimulus. Don't conflate them.
Other psychotic phenomena. Somatic passivity — belief that outside influences play on the body. Made phenomenon — made act (actions under external control), made affect (feelings not one's own), made impulse (impulse admitted as one's own action but not one's own drive).

Judgement & Insight

Judgement: social, personal, test. Insight — degree of awareness/understanding of being ill (intellectual vs true emotional insight), rated on a 6-point scale:

Insight — 6-point scale: Grade 1 complete denial → Grade 2 slight awareness but denying → Grade 3 attributed to external/physical factors → Grade 4 due to something unknown in self → Grade 5 intellectual insight → Grade 6 true emotional insight.

Eliminated: sexual aversion; polysubstance-related disorder.
Criticism of DSM: medicalization (over-diagnosis), pharmaceutical influence, cultural and ethical concerns, comorbidity.

4 · Neurodevelopmental Disorders

Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD)

  • Earlier diagnosable only up to age 7, now up to age 12; stays into adulthood if untreated; many comorbidities.
  • Inattention: distractible, loses interest, keeps shifting tasks, doesn't attend to detail, loses things.
  • Hyperactivity: impulsive, can't wait, aggressive, can't wait for turn, squirms, talks excessively.
  • Any 6 symptoms must be met; duration ≥ 6 months.

Treatment: Ritalin, amphetamine, dextroamphetamine; behavioural modification, applied behavioural analysis, shaping, chaining, token economy, response cost; Modafinil; Clonidine, venlafaxine, bupropion, SNRI.

DDx: oppositional defiant, conduct disorder, aggression, depression, substance use, ASPD; problems with frontal lobe & cerebellum; difficulty copying, rapid alternating movements, right-left discrimination.

Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD / Pervasive Developmental Disorder)

  • Chromosomes 2, 7, 4, 15, 19 involved.
  • Impairment in social interaction (eye contact, social smile, attachment, expression, making friends, routine).
  • Impairment in communication and language (delayed milestones, articulation difficulty, echolalia, verbigeration, saying "me" instead of "you").
  • Restrictive, repetitive, stereotyped behaviour, flapping, echopraxia; impaired theory of mind; high pain tolerance; biting/scratching/headbanging; isles of precocity (high skill in something).

Treatment: Risperidone, Aripiprazole; behaviour modification. DDx: Fragile X, Phenylketonuria, Rubella, Intellectual disability, Tuberous sclerosis, asphyxia.

Rett's Syndrome

Losing fine motor skills; head circumference decreases from 5–48 months; stereotyped movement; problems with walk/talk/eat/breathe; 75% seizures; progressive; mostly in girls; microencephaly. A subtype of pervasive developmental disorder. Normal early development followed by partial/complete loss of speech, locomotion & hand-use skills with deceleration in head growth. Treatment symptomatic.

Heller's Syndrome (Childhood Disintegrative Disorder, CDD)

Rare; late onset (> 3 years) of developmental delays in language, social function, motor skills. Symptoms age 2–10 years: impaired social skills, language skills, bladder control; stereotyped behaviour; microcephaly. Another neurodevelopmental disorder.

Asperger's Syndrome

Mild ASD; problems with social interaction; experience emotion but can't express; restrictive, repetitive, stereotyped behaviour.

Intellectual Disability (ID)

Overlaps with ADHD and Autism before 18 years; IQ below 70; spectrum approach; based on adaptive functioning in conceptual, social and practical domains; onset before 18 years.

Aetiology: genetic abnormality (5%) — chromosomal, cranial anomaly, single-gene disorder, inborn errors of metabolism (Tay-Sachs, Lesch-Nyhan); perinatal (10%) — infections, prematurity, birth trauma, hypoxia, kernicterus, placental abnormality, drugs; acquired physical (infections, cretinism, trauma, lead poisoning, cerebral palsy); sociocultural (15%) — deprivation; psychiatric (1–2%). DDx: deaf/dumb, deprived, speech defect, psychiatric disorder, systematic disorder, epilepsy.

Global Developmental Delay

Delay in development for a patient under 5 years who has not been adequately evaluated; missed developmental milestones.

Specific Learning Disability (SLD)

IQ is normal but poor academic score. Dyslexia — inability to read/comprehend; Dysgraphia — poor handwriting; Dyscalculia — arithmetic impairment.

Tics & Tourette's (Motor Disorder)

  • 1% of young people; family history of tics or OCD.
  • Motor tics, vocal tics, involuntary jerks; stress develops; effort goes into controlling tics.
  • Tourette's: ≥ 2 motor and 1 vocal tic; knee-jerking responses; longer than 1 year.
  • Vocal: barks, clicks, coughs, grunts. Coprolalia — swearing (bad-word) tics.
  • Dopamine antagonists used for treatment.

Communication Disorders

  • Language disorder: problems in spoken/written/sign language; expressive & receptive; vocabulary & grammar; speaks slower & less; academic issues; can co-exist with ADHD.
  • Speech sound (phonological) disorder: substituting/omitting sounds (e.g. "ghaspetti" for "spaghetti"); diagnosed only in first language; 2–3% of preschool children.
  • Childhood-onset fluency (stuttering) disorder: difficulty with beginning of words; momentary panic; anxiety/frustration; occurs with consonants and initial sounds; begins 2–5 years; more in boys; ~3% of children; dopamine antagonists used.
  • Social (pragmatic) communication disorder: don't understand what to say where; poor contextual use of speech; less practical language use, convention, turn-taking, non-verbal/implied communication; may be comorbid.

5 · Anxiety Disorders (F40–48)

Neurosis — subjective distress; ego-dystonic; insight present; personality & behaviour preserved; absence of organicity; reaction to stress & adjustment; somatoform.

Anxiety symptoms. Motor (tremors, restlessness, muscle twitch, fearful face); autonomic/visceral (palpitation, tachycardia, sweating, flushes, hyperventilation, chest constriction, mouth dryness, reduced micturition, mydriasis); cognitive (poor concentration, distractibility, hyperarousal, vigilance); affective (apprehension, fearfulness, inability to relax, irritability, feelings of doom); other (noise sensitivity, insomnia, increased startle response). Trait anxiety ("I often feel anxious") vs state anxiety ("I feel anxious now") — those with trait anxiety also have state anxiety.

Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD)

Most diagnosed anxiety disorder; free-floating anxiety; for 6 months or more; constant low arousal of the sympathetic nervous system. DDx: OCD, PTSD, mood disorder, physical causes, realistic anxiety. Treatment: benzodiazepine, SSRI, barbiturates (old), CBT, relaxation, psychodynamic therapy.

Social Anxiety Disorder

Severe irrational fear of evaluation; dysfunction at work and relationships; ≥ 6 months; sometimes only due to evaluation of performance.

Specific Phobia

Defence mechanism: displacement. Irrational, disproportional fear of certain animals, natural environment, situations, blood/injection, others. Fear out of proportion; insight present; inability to control fear; avoidance of the object.

Agoraphobia

Fear of being in a place where they can't get up and leave; fear of crowded/enclosed places. Treatment: clomipramine, venlafaxine, buspirone.

Panic Disorder

Feelings of impending doom; hippocampus and amygdala involved; periodic, discrete bouts of panic/terror in the absence of any real threat; feels like a heart attack; should be present for more than a month.

Separation Anxiety Disorder

Fear that something will happen to the attachment figure if away; can happen in adults; may have vomiting; ≥ 6 months in adults, ≥ 4 weeks in children.

Selective Mutism

Children remain silent except before close people; normal intelligence; family history of SAD; may develop SAD in adulthood; duration > 1 month; treatment: social skills training.

Other anxiety disorders

Substance-induced anxiety disorder (from exposure/withdrawal to a medication); anxiety disorder due to another medical condition; other specified anxiety disorder (clinician specifies reason for non-fulfilment); unspecified anxiety disorder (clinician does not specify).

6 · Obsessive-Compulsive & Related Disorders

Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD)

  • Young-adulthood onset; most common in males; family history.
  • Obsessions: ego-dystonic, irrational, absurd, distressing, recognised as one's own idea.
  • Compulsions: to neutralize the obsession; not realistic; irrational; insight present; subjective; may diminish the anxiety associated with the obsession.
  • Major patterns: contamination, pathological doubt, intrusive thought, symmetry (tics).
Aetiology. Dysfunction in fronto-striatal brain circuitry (caudate). Defence mechanisms: reaction formation, isolation of affect, undoing.

DSM specifier: with good/fair insight; with poor insight; with absent insight/delusional beliefs; tic-related. Treatment: clomipramine, antipsychotics, lithium, valproate; Exposure and Response Prevention, aversion therapy; caudectomy, cingulotomy, capsulectomy.

Trichotillomania

Recurrent pulling out of one's hair resulting in hair loss and bald patches; attempts to control the behaviour.

Excoriation (Skin-Picking) Disorder

Recurrent skin picking resulting in skin lesions; attempts to control the behaviour. ICD-11: factitious dermatitis.

Hoarding Disorder

Overwhelming urge to accumulate things; distressed when discarding possessions.

Body Dysmorphic Disorder

Preoccupied with minor body imperfections. Specify if: with muscle dysmorphia.

7 · Depressive (Mood) Disorders

Major Depressive Disorder (MDD)

Duration ≥ 2 weeks. Mnemonic SIGECAPS: Sleep disturbance (REM latency, early-morning awakening); low Interest; Guilt; Energy deficit; reduced Concentration; reduced Appetite; Psychomotor agitation/retardation; Suicidal ideation.

Specifiers/features: with psychotic features (schizoaffective); atypical features (hypersomnia, overeating); melancholic features (in old age); catatonic features.

Persistent Depressive Disorder (Dysthymia)

Symptoms persist for 2 years; no suicidal ideation; no hospitalization; symptoms never absent for longer than 2 months.

MDD vs Persistent Depressive Disorder. MDD = ≥ 2 weeks, may include suicidal ideation and hospitalization. Persistent depressive disorder (dysthymia) = ≥ 2 years, no suicidal ideation, no hospitalization, symptoms never absent > 2 months. Double depression = MDD superimposed on dysthymia (a poor-prognosis feature).

Disruptive Mood Dysregulation Disorder (DMDD)

For children and adolescents; a precursor to bipolar; sudden anger, throwing fits, tantrums; persistent negative moods; sadness translated into complete outbursts. DDx: oppositional defiant disorder.

Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder (PMDD)

Mood shifts or other dysphoria in the week or two before the menstrual period.

Physical signs. Veraguth's fold — triangular fold in the nasal corner of the upper eyelid. Omega sign on the forehead. Beck's cognitive triad — negative view of self, world, future. Also: learned helplessness (Seligman).

Aetiology: unipolar depression concordance rate 46%; abnormality in monoamine, acetylcholine and GABA neurotransmitters; hormonal disturbances; neuroanatomical — reduced dorsolateral prefrontal cortex and increased HPA axis.

Treatment: pharmacotherapy takes 3–4 weeks; maintained for 6 months (prophylactic). Tricyclic antidepressants (block reuptake of serotonin & norepinephrine, the "-pramines"; anticholinergic side effects — constipation, urinary retention, blurred vision, dry mouth). SSRIs / NaSSA / SNRI (side effects: nausea, anxiety, diarrhoea). MAOIs. Antipsychotics. Therapies: CBT, mindfulness, IPT, ECT, TMS, vagus nerve stimulation, deep brain stimulation, sleep deprivation therapy, phototherapy (1500–10,000 lux).

8 · Bipolar & Related Disorders (Mania)

Manic episode criteria — mnemonic DIG FAST: Distractibility; Impulsivity; Grandiosity; Flight of ideas; Activity (goal-directed) increase; Sleep deficit; Talkativeness.

Bipolar I

Requires a manic episode. Major depressive episodes common but not required for diagnosis.

Bipolar II

A hypomanic episode and at least one major depressive episode; there has never been a full manic episode.

Bipolar I vs Bipolar II — and Mania vs Hypomania. Mania (Bipolar I): lasts 1 week, accompanied by less intense depression, may have psychotic features, requires hospitalization. Hypomania (Bipolar II): lasts 4 days, accompanied by major depressive episodes, may not require hospitalization, never a full manic episode. Bipolar I needs a manic episode; Bipolar II needs hypomania + ≥ 1 MDE and no full mania.

Cyclothymic Disorder

Periods with hypomanic symptoms (not meeting full criteria) and depressive symptoms (not meeting full criteria) for at least 2 years, during which symptoms have not been absent for more than 2 months.

Specifiers

Mood-congruent psychotic features; mood-incongruent psychotic features; catatonia; peripartum onset; seasonal pattern; hypomania; mixed episodes (cycle in 7 days); anxious distress; rapid cycling; melancholic features; atypical features.

Aetiology & prevalence. Bipolar disorder is the most genetically influenced disorder (74% if both parents affected). Neurotransmitters: increased dopamine activity. Genetic factors: chromosomes 18q, 22q, 21q. Mania more common in men; depression more common in women; onset 30 years and above; more in divorced/single persons.

Treatment: Lithium (side effects: postural tremors [treated with beta-blockers], memory disturbance, intracranial tension, peripheral neuropathy, hypothyroidism, polyuria, diabetes; lithium toxicity — tremors, renal impairment, ataxia, delirium, increased reflexes, convulsions, vomiting, kidney issues), Valproate (acute mania), Lamotrigine (depressive), Carbamazepine, Oxcarbazepine, antipsychotics, benzodiazepines, antidepressants; ECT; therapy. Treatment continued for 2 years.

9 · Schizophrenia Spectrum & Other Psychotic Disorders

History. Emil Kraepelin gave the term dementia praecox (1896); his classification: simple dementia, paranoia, catatonia, hebephrenia. Eugen Bleuler coined the term schizophrenia and gave the 4 A's: Ambivalence, Autism, Association (disturbance), Affect disturbance. Karl Kahlbaum — catatonia.

Symptoms

Negative symptoms: affective flattening, avolition, apathy, anhedonia, asociality, alogia.

Schneider's First-Rank Symptoms (positive). Audible thoughts (1st person); voices heard arguing (3rd person); voices commenting on one's actions; thought withdrawal; thought diffusion/broadcasting; made feeling; made impulse; made actions; somatic passivity.

Also: hallucinations; delusions (erotomania, grandeur, guilt, jealousy, passivity, persecution, poverty, reference, somatic, thought control). Speech disturbances: poverty of speech, echolalia, verbigeration (continual repetition of stereotyped phrases), perseveration.

Catatonia (Karl Kahlbaum): agitation, cataplexy (sudden muscle weakness/paralysis), echolalia/echopraxia, exaggerated compliance, grimace, mannerism, mutism, stereotypy, posturing, waxy flexibility.

Types (subtypes)

Paranoid; Catatonic; Disorganized/Hebephrenic; Undifferentiated; Residual; Simple (worst prognosis). Also pfropfschizophrenie — schizophrenia and mental retardation coexisting.

Schizophreniform vs Schizophrenia (the classic duration trap). Schizophreniform = 1–6 months; Schizophrenia = 6+ months — same full symptom set, only the duration cutoff differs. Brief psychotic disorder is even shorter (1 day–1 month), and schizoaffective requires both schizophrenia and a mood disorder.

Etiology, epidemiology & prognosis

Positive symptoms — excess dopamine in the mesolimbic tract (VTA → limbic region). Negative symptoms — dopamine dysregulation in the mesocortical pathway (VTA → prefrontal lobe). Genetic loci: 1q, 5q, 6p, 6q, 8p, 10p, 13q, 15q, 22q. Biochemical: dopamine, serotonin, glutamate, GABA. Epidemiology: 1% lifetime; 0.15–0.25% per 1000; good prognosis in females; bad prognosis with low SES.

Treatment. Typical antipsychotics (dopamine receptor antagonists; side effect: Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome, NMS) — Chlorpromazine (1950), Haloperidol, Phenothiazines, Thioxanthene, Butyrophenones. Atypical antipsychotics (target 5-HT receptors, partially block D2) — Clozapine (treatment-resistant schizophrenia), Olanzapine, Quetiapine; do not cause tardive dyskinesia.

10 · Trauma & Stressor-Related Disorders

Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)

  • Occurs after rape, floods, abduction, plane crash, soldiers of war; symptoms don't develop immediately.
  • Was classified under anxiety disorders until DSM-IV-TR.
  • Symptoms: avoidance of situations/places/memories; intrusive flashbacks, re-experiencing, nightmares; arousal (hypervigilance, exaggerated startle); depersonalization & derealization.
  • Diagnosed if symptoms appear within 6 months after trauma; symptoms present for more than a month. Brain regions: hippocampus, amygdala.

Acute Stress Disorder

All PTSD symptoms for less than 1 month; also experience dissociation; 9/14 symptoms of PTSD must be present.

Acute Stress Disorder vs PTSD. ASD = PTSD symptoms lasting < 1 month (with prominent dissociation). PTSD = symptoms > 1 month, appearing within 6 months of trauma. Note ICD-11 eliminated acute stress disorder as a diagnosis.

Complex PTSD (ICD-11)

Prolonged exposure to trauma. Disturbances in self-organization — the person must experience 2/3 of: (1) emotional dysregulation, (2) interpersonal disturbances, (3) [disturbed self-perception]. Clinical features: re-experiencing, avoidance, hyperarousal, impaired self-perception, difficulty maintaining relationships. Treatment: prevention/disaster management, practical help, SSRIs, benzodiazepines, EMDR.

Prolonged Grief Disorder (DSM-5-TR & ICD-11)

Intense, persistent grief; difficulty accepting loss; intrusive thoughts/memories; avoidance; emotional numbness; identity disturbance (feeling like we have died with the other person); unable to function for more than 6 months. Treatment: grief counselling, group therapy, SSRIs.

Adjustment Disorder

Not being able to adjust to a new situation; psychological and physical symptoms (depression, anxiety) for a span of 3–6 months. Specifiers: anxiety, depression, behaviour, conduct.

Reactive Attachment Disorder

Unable to form reciprocal relationships; faced rejection in childhood; periods of irritable sadness; issues develop before age 5; persistent if symptoms last longer than a year. DDx: ASD, depression.

Disinhibited Social Engagement Disorder

Outcome of abuse, insufficient caregiving, neglect, frequent change; shyness; don't check in with regular caregiver; may become excessively familiar with strangers.

11 · Dissociative Disorders

A group of normally integrated mental processes become separated from the rest. Clinical features: disturbance in integrated consciousness; sudden onset, temporary course; precipitatory stress; secondary gain; no physical abnormality; affects memory, identity, perception; produced unconsciously by the psyche.

Types of gain. Primary — internal psychological motivation to reduce guilt. Secondary — external psychological motivation for assurance/social support. Tertiary — parent's/close person's benefit.

Dissociative Amnesia (Psychogenic Amnesia)

Types: circumscribed (personal event-specific), selective (forgetting some things), generalized (forgetting everything), continuous (from a stressful event till present), systematized (everything about an event).

Dissociative Fugue

Wandering/running off; losing old identity and picking up a new one; termination is abrupt.

Depersonalization/Derealization Disorder

Depersonalization — cutting off/detachment from oneself. Derealization — experience of what is real and not real may be lost. Occurs without overlap with other disorders.

Dissociative Identity Disorder (DID)

At least 2 distinct identities; personality states called alter egos.

Other dissociative presentations (ICD-10)

Dissociative trance or possession disorder; dissociative stupor; dissociative disorder of movement and sensation (la belle indifférence) — physical symptoms due to psychological causes.

12 · Somatic Symptom & Related Disorders

Somatic Symptom (Somatoform) Disorder

Chronic pain without any physical cause; conviction persists despite negative test results; duration 6+ months; onset may be due to stress. Clinical features: 4 pain symptoms, 2 GI symptoms, 1 sexual symptom, 1 pseudo-neurological symptom. Treatment: psychotherapy.

Illness Anxiety Disorder (Hypochondriasis)

Over-concern with normal body function; terribly worried about having a serious illness; symptoms don't remain constant; unconsciously start mimicking symptoms (not psychotic); addicted to internet searches. Subtypes: care-seeking, care-avoidant. Duration 6+ months.

Conversion Disorder (Hysteria / Functional Neurological Symptom Disorder)

Body function is lost due to a psychological issue; primary and secondary gain; psychogenic non-epileptic seizures (PNES); disturbance of consciousness; la belle indifférence; duration 6+ months.

Factitious Disorder

Person wants to stay sick for attention; maintains the sick role; simulates symptoms. Types: for self; by proxy (Munchausen syndrome) — keeping a child sick. Pseudologia fantastica — telling stories, pathological lying. Person pretends to have non-measurable diseases; "professionals as patients".

13 · Feeding & Eating Disorders

Anorexia Nervosa

Restricted food intake to markedly reduced body weight; obsessing about obesity/weight gain; distorted self-perception of being overweight. Characteristic symptoms: disturbance of body image; fear of fatness & excessive emphasis on thinness; restriction of energy intake (significantly less weight than normal); amenorrhea (compulsory until DSM-IV), hypothermia, edema, bradycardia, reduced sexual activity; cuts food into small pieces and rearranges it; cooks for other people. Types: restricting type, purging type. Treatment: hospitalization, reinforcement for healthy eating, CBT, SSRIs.

Bulimia Nervosa

Normal body weight; binge eating followed by purging; gobbles food in large quantities; no distorted view of being overweight; morbid fear of weight gain; normal sexual functioning; not secretive. Duration 3+ months.

Severity (episodes/week): Mild 1–3; Moderate 4–7; Severe 8–13; Extreme 14 or more.
Anorexia vs Bulimia. Anorexia → markedly reduced body weight and a distorted body image. Bulimia → normal body weight, binge-purge cycles, and no distorted view of being overweight; not secretive.

Binge Eating Disorder

Heavy episodes of eating; episodes often occur in private; craving for calorie-dense food with high speed of consumption; duration 3+ months; severity measured like bulimia.

Rumination Disorder

Repeated regurgitation of food — chewed, swallowed, brought back to the mouth and chewed again; may lead to nutritional deficiency; may happen in infants and intellectually disabled children (usually males); malnutrition and vulnerability to disease; may increase gastro-oesophageal reflux; duration 1+ months.

Pica

Consuming non-nutritive, non-food substances; duration 1+ months; not eating normal food; nutritional deficiency; behaviour often recognized only when bowel-obstruction surgery becomes necessary; may be found in children with ID and ASD; usually low socioeconomic status.

Avoidant/Restrictive Food Intake Disorder (Food Fad Disorder)

Child becomes addicted to one kind of food and refuses anything else; may commence due to parent-child conflict. 3 categories: don't care about eating; restrict diet due to sensory issues; don't eat because of an unpleasant experience. DDx: anxiety.

14 · Sleep-Wake Disorders

Why we sleep & sleep stages

Adaptive theory: organisms sleep for self-preservation. Restorative theory: to restore depleted chemical resources.

Parasomnias

NREM: confusional arousal; sleepwalking (somnambulism); sleep/night terrors (usually during deep sleep). REM: REM sleep behaviour disorder (acting out dreams); recurrent isolated sleep paralysis (awake but unable to move); nightmare disorder. (Additional behaviours with or during sleep.)

Dyssomnias

Intrinsic: obstructive sleep apnea (due to obesity); central sleep apnea (reduced oxygen to brain due to cardiovascular problems); psychophysiological insomnia; idiopathic insomnia; narcolepsy (excessive daytime sleepiness; cataplexy — sudden loss of muscle tone with intense emotions; hypnopompic & hypnagogic hallucinations).

Extrinsic: circadian rhythm disorder (night shifts / pineal gland problem; suprachiasmatic nucleus disturbed); restless legs syndrome (leg sensations, impulse to move).

Treatment: medication, melatonin. Measurement: polysomnography (EEG/EMG/EOG/ECG/GSR).

15 · Sexual Dysfunctions, Gender Dysphoria & Paraphilic Disorders

Sexual Dysfunctions

Delayed ejaculation; erectile disorder; female orgasmic disorder; female sexual interest/arousal disorder; genito-pelvic pain/penetration disorder; male hypoactive sexual desire disorder; premature ejaculation.

Gender Dysphoria

A marked incongruence between one's experienced/expressed gender and assigned gender; duration 6 months.

Paraphilic Disorders

Anomalous activity preferences — Courtship disorders (resemble distorted components of human courtship): Voyeuristic (spying on others in private), Exhibitionist (exposing the genitals), Frotteuristic (touching/rubbing against a non-consenting person).

Algolagnic disorders (pain & suffering): Sexual masochism (arousal from undergoing humiliation/bondage/suffering), Sexual sadism (arousal from inflicting humiliation/bondage/suffering).

Anomalous target preferences — directed at others: Paedophilic disorder (sexual focus on children). Directed elsewhere: Fetishistic disorder (nonliving objects or highly specific non-genital body-part focus); Transvestic disorder (sexually arousing cross-dressing); duration 6 months.

16 · Personality Disorders

General features: ego-syntonic; don't seek psychiatric help unless other symptoms co-occur; deviate from social norm — unhappy and impaired; abnormal behaviour and subjective experience causing significant impairment; disharmonious attitude; enduring, maladaptive pattern; appear during adolescence; personal/social distress. Prevalence: 10–20% of the general population, onset adolescence/early adulthood; symptoms fizzle out after 40. More common in men and minority groups.

Cluster A — Odd & Eccentric

Paranoid Personality Disorder

Obsessive rumination; excessive suspiciousness; heightened self-importance; persistently bears grudges; conspiracy theories; suspicious of partner's fidelity. Treatment: supportive psychotherapy, benzodiazepines, antipsychotics. DDx: delusional disorder.

Schizoid Personality Disorder

Emotional bluntness; lack of pleasure from activities; limited capacity to express feelings; indifference to praise/criticism; little interest in sexual experience; prefers solitary activities; preoccupation with fantasy and introspection.

Schizotypal Personality Disorder

Ideas of reference; odd beliefs/magical thinking; eccentric behaviour; odd thinking; suspiciousness/paranoid ideas; inappropriate/constricted affect; poor rapport; lack of close friends; unusual perceptual experience; stereotypical thinking; quasi-psychotic episodes; belief in telepathy and thought broadcast; inner world like a child; may experience psychosis under severe stress; 3/9 symptoms needed. ICD-10 puts it under schizophrenia. Treatment: antidepressants, antipsychotics, benzodiazepines; psychoanalysis, individual therapy.

Cluster B — Dramatic & Emotional

Antisocial Personality Disorder

Callous unconcern for others' feelings; disregard for social norms; can't maintain relationships; low frustration tolerance; can't experience guilt; proneness to blame others; history of conduct disorder; EEG abnormality; ADHD comorbidity; genetic predisposition; comes to psychiatry from legal authorities; 3/6 symptoms to be met. DSM-5 also includes impulse-control disorder. Treatment: carbamazepine, beta-blockers.

Histrionic Personality Disorder

Extreme attention-seeking; emotionalism seeps into all areas; self-dramatization; suggestibility; shallow and labile affect; continual attention-seeking; inappropriately seductive; over-concern with physical attractiveness; flamboyant. Defence mechanisms: acting out, dissociation. Treatment: antidepressants, psychotherapy.

Borderline Personality Disorder

Impulsive; labile; self-harm clearly visible to others; unstable relationships; fragile self-esteem; unclear preferences; devaluation or idealisation; fear of abandonment; chronic feelings of emptiness; accident proneness. Primary defence: splitting; comorbidity with depression; also called emotionally unstable personality disorder. Treatment: Dialectical Behaviour Therapy (DBT).

Narcissistic Personality Disorder

Sense of grandiosity; fantasy of unlimited success; attention/praise-seeking; lack of empathy; shaky self-esteem; thirst for admiration; unable to face criticism; tries to impress new people; believes they are special/talented; develops depression if faced with rejection.

Cluster C — Anxious & Fearful

Avoidant Personality Disorder

Feelings of inadequacy; belief that they are socially inept, inferior, unappealing; preoccupation with being rejected; unwillingness to involve socially; restricted lifestyle; 3/6 traits should be present; depression may develop. Treatment: beta-blockers, SSRI, CBT, social skills training.

Dependent Personality Disorder

Need for others to take care of them and make decisions; subordination of one's own needs; unwillingness to make demands; fear of being abandoned; can't make decisions without excessive reassurance; may develop passive-aggressive personality.

Obsessive-Compulsive Personality Disorder (OCPD)

Excessive doubt; need to exert interpersonal control; preoccupation with details; excessive conscientiousness; adherence to social convention; others should submit to their ways; unwelcome thoughts. Psychodynamic view: fixation in the anal stage. ICD calls it anankastic personality disorder. Treatment: psychotherapy.

OCD vs OCPD. OCD (an anxiety-related disorder) involves ego-dystonic obsessions and compulsions with insight. OCPD (a personality disorder) is an ego-syntonic, pervasive pattern of perfectionism, control and orderliness — the person sees nothing wrong with it. ICD calls OCPD "anankastic."

17 · Substance-Related & Addictive Disorders

ICD-10: "mental and behavioural disorders due to psychoactive substance use" — more focus on consequence. Initial denial, then tolerance and withdrawal.

Etiology. Brain areas: striatum, nucleus accumbens (addiction behaviour). Neurotransmitter systems: dopamine, opioid, catecholamines, GABA. Hereditary factors involved.

Clinical syndromes (ICD)

(1) Acute intoxication; (2) withdrawal state; (3) dependence syndrome; (4) harmful use — causing harm to the individual but dependence criteria not met; damage to physical/mental health; issues in daily functioning.

Dependence: behavioural, physical & psychological craving; difficulty controlling use; withdrawal symptoms; tolerance; neglect of alternative pleasures; persistence with substance use.

Classes of substances

CNS stimulants: cocaine, amphetamine, caffeine, nicotine. Depressants: alcohol, barbiturates, benzodiazepines, inhalants. Opioids: narcotics, heroin, morphine. Cannabis. Hallucinogen & phencyclidine.

Cocaine

Blocks D1, D2 dopamine receptors; increases dopamine & norepinephrine in the synapse; sympathomimetic; vasoconstriction; once used as an anaesthetic; crack = freebase of cocaine (high craving). Intoxication: euphoria, pupil dilation, tachycardia, sweating. Miscellaneous: nasal perforation, tongue turns black. Withdrawal: low energy/exhaustion, hunger, suicidal ideation. Formication (Magnan's phenomenon / cocaine bugs) — sensation of insects crawling on/under the skin (tactile hallucination). Cocaine-induced psychotic disorder — similar to paranoid schizophrenia.

Amphetamine

Euphoria; potential for psychotic disorder; used for performance enhancement. Substances: methamphetamines, dextroamphetamines, methylphenidate. Acute intoxication: paranoid ideation, auditory hallucination, visual illusion. Withdrawal: fatigue; vivid/unpleasant dreams; insomnia or hypersomnia; increased appetite; psychomotor agitation or retardation.

Nicotine

Enhances attention, learning, reaction time, problem-solving. Most common in India as beedi; nicotine and carbon monoxide responsible for cardiovascular disorder. Withdrawal: craving, irritability, anxiety, difficulty concentrating, bradycardia, drowsiness, decreased REM sleep, increased appetite. Treatment: nicotine replacement therapy; Bupropion; Clonidine; Nortriptyline; Varenicline (new); behaviour therapy, CBT.

Caffeine

Effects: anxiety, jittery, restless, insomnia, flushed face. Withdrawal: headaches, fatigue, irritability, difficulty concentrating.

Opioids

Opiates (natural) — poppy; opioids (synthetic) — heroin (quick absorption). Effects: euphoria, warmth, high dependence potential, facial flushing, sedation. Overdose: respiratory depression, coma, pupil constriction, cyanosis. Withdrawal: muscle cramps, body aches, insomnia, anxiety, hypertension, tachycardia, nausea, vomiting, anorexia. Treatment: long-acting opioid — methadone, buprenorphine for 2–3 weeks, slowly tapered. Withdrawal duration: 6–8 hrs; peak 2nd–3rd day, reduces by 8–10 days.

Hallucinogens & Phencyclidine

LSD, psilocybin mushroom, MDMA (ecstasy), angel dust (PCP). Effects: depersonalization, derealization, synesthesia, hallucinations, illusions, autonomic hyperactivity, altered perception; may cause a "bad trip" (benzodiazepines given to reduce). No physical dependence; tolerance; withdrawal rarely seen; LSD associated with flashback phenomena. Treatment: psychotherapy. PCP: behavioural changes, dizziness, nystagmus, slurred speech, unsteady gait, lethargy, tremors, muscle weakness, blurred vision, stupor, euphoria; rapid reach to lungs/brain/bloodstream. Overdose: lesions in the brain, cognitive deficits.

Cannabis

Bhang, charas, ganja, weed, hash. Effects: behavioural changes, impaired motor coordination, anxiety, slowed time, red eyes, increased appetite, dry mouth, rapid heartbeat, impaired judgement. Withdrawal: irritability, anger, aggression, nervousness, sleep difficulties, decreased appetite, restlessness, depressed mood. Treatment: rehabilitation, behaviour therapy, psychotherapy.

Inhalants (Depressants)

Gasoline, spray paint, thinner. Effects like a depressant: lethargy, depressed reflexes, psychomotor retardation, tremors, generalized muscle weakness, blurred vision, stupor, euphoria. Withdrawal: agitation/irritability, powerful headaches, nausea, excessive sweating, tremors and convulsions, abdominal cramping, intense cravings.

Alcohol

Jellinek's typology of alcoholism: (1) Alpha — psychological dependence, early stage, relieving emotional issues, can leave alcohol if desired; (2) Beta — physical consequences (liver sclerosis) started, no psychological/physical dependence, no addiction; (3) Gamma — addiction with loss of control, tolerance, physical dependence; (4) Delta — maintenance drinking, can't abstain, loss of control; (5) Epsilon — dipsomania, periodic/binge drinking, most advanced.
Cloninger's typology: Type 1 — later onset, more psychological dependence, guilt, no sense of control. Type 2 — starts early, socially disruptive, more physical dependence, sensation seeking, better sense of control.

Legal limit: 30 mg/dl. 10% discarded, 90% absorbed; peak absorption 30–90 minutes. Reverse tolerance: body degraded so much that even a lesser amount has effects.

Withdrawal timeline (seizures): 6–8 hrs — coarse tremors; 12–24 hrs — alcoholic hallucinosis; 24–48 hrs — alcohol withdrawal seizures (tonic-clonic); 48–72 hrs — delirium tremens.

Evaluation: CAGE questionnaire — Cut down, Annoyed, Guilty, Eye-opener; AUDIT; SADQ. Treatment: benzodiazepines, thiamine, carbamazepine, disulfiram, anti-craving agents; CBT, Alcoholics Anonymous, family/group therapy.

18 · Neurocognitive Disorders

ICD-11: organic mental disorder. Episodic memory is first to go; semantic memory is last to go. Defies Occam's razor: no single explanation. Marked by anxiety, sleep problems, psychosis.

Cognitive impairment — the 4 A's: Aphasia, Agnosia, Amnesia, Apraxia. Plus disturbance of consciousness (delirium), visual hallucinations, transient delusions.

Dementia (Major Neurocognitive Disorder)

Amnesia, aphasia, apraxia, agnosia; personality changes (frontotemporal lobe); hallucinations, delusions, mood symptoms; progressive; reduced intellectual functioning; impaired memory & self-care; perseveration; disorientation in time/place/person.

TypeExamples & features
Cortical dementia (fronto-, temporo-, parieto-)Hallucination, delusion; cognitive functioning more compromised. E.g. Alzheimer's, Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (mad cow disease)
Subcortical dementiaMotor issues, depressive. E.g. Huntington's chorea, Parkinson's disease, progressive supranuclear palsy, Wilson's disease

Alzheimer's Disease

Most common dementia; 5% of people by age 65 (pre-senile); 2–30% by age 85 (senile); females outnumber males 3:1; linked with chromosomes 1, 14, 21 (autosomal). Marked by neurofibrillary tangles (barrier in the cholinergic pathway) and beta-amyloid plaques; hippocampal degeneration. Symptoms: confusion with time/location, difficulty completing familiar tasks, misplacing items, memory loss, difficulty problem-solving, social withdrawal, poor judgement, unfounded emotions, difficulty with words, trouble with images; anterograde then retrograde amnesia.

Delirium

Metabolic encephalopathy; disturbance of consciousness; impaired attention; impaired time/place/person orientation; impaired immediate memory; perceptual disturbances (hallucinations); hypo/hyperactivity; disturbed sleep-wake cycle; autonomic disturbances. Sundowning — increased problems in the evening. Floccillation — patient picks at bedclothes. Occupational delirium. Acetylcholine and ARAS implicated. Predisposing factors: pre-existing dementia, extreme old age, history of delirium, drug dependence, cerebral lesions, surgery, severe psychological symptoms, psychotropic medicines. Treatment: treat underlying cause, antipsychotics, benzodiazepines.

Delirium vs Dementia (and Pseudodementia). Delirium = acute, fluctuating disturbance of consciousness/attention with visual hallucinations; reversible. Dementia = chronic, progressive decline in cognition with clear consciousness. Pseudodementia (depressive) — complains about memory impairment, emphasizes disability/distress, says "don't know," recent-memory impairment, variable test performance, past history of mood episodes.

Dementia due to Traumatic Brain Injury

Loss of consciousness/delirium; male-predominant (bike accidents), elderly (falls); consciousness (4 A's) affected; diagnosable through scans; amnesia seen first; hemiparesis. Heavy instance of repeated head trauma; hyper- and hypokinesia; procedural memory affected.

Vascular Dementia

Multi-infarction; small vascular strokes (heart issue); insufficient oxygen supplied to the brain.

Huntington's

100% genetic — caused by the HTT gene (dominant) on the 12th chromosome; chorea; hyperkinesia; cognitive + motor deficit; glutamate excitotoxicity.

Lewy Body Disease

New in DSM-5. Lewy bodies = bits of alpha-synuclein protein (in cytoplasm) of brain cells, located in brainstem, substantia nigra, locus coeruleus. Cognitive symptoms start first, followed by motor symptoms; fluctuating attention; hallucinations (can't prescribe antipsychotics); late onset of Parkinson-like symptoms. Duration: chronic.

Parkinson's

Pill-rolling tremors, bradykinesia, rigidity, slow movements, stooped posture, postural instability, akinesia, freezing of gait, micrographia.

Korsakoff's

Thiamine (vitamin B1) deficiency caused by chronic alcoholism and lack of nutrition. Less severe — Wernicke's encephalopathy: global amnesia, confusion, ataxia, ophthalmoplegia; acute symptoms; sudden onset; reversible. Korsakoff's: irreversible, confabulation observed, chronic onset; found in older adults.

Wernicke's vs Korsakoff's. Both from thiamine (B1) deficiency. Wernicke's encephalopathy = acute, sudden onset, reversible (confusion, ataxia, ophthalmoplegia). Korsakoff's = chronic, irreversible, with confabulation.

Frontotemporal Dementia

Accounts for 5% of all dementia; cortical dementia (hallucination and delusion); executive functions impacted. Behavioural variant — socially inappropriate, apathy, fixed mindset. Language variant — inappropriate words, grammar. Types: frontal variant; semantic dementia (temporal); progressive non-fluent aphasia.

Mild Neurocognitive Disorder

Doesn't meet criteria for major dementia but marked dysfunction; person scores below 1 standard deviation on tests; minor loss of cognitive functioning.

19 · Named / Eponymous Syndromes

Delusional misidentification syndromes.
  • Capgras syndrome — belief that a loved one or pet has been replaced by an identical imposter.
  • Fregoli syndrome — belief that familiar people are in disguise or have changed their appearance.
  • Syndrome of intermetamorphosis — delusion that people have swapped identities while maintaining the same appearance (total psychological + physical transformation, not just disguise).
  • Syndrome of subjective doubles — delusion that others have swapped identities while keeping the same appearance.
  • Reduplicative paramnesia — delusional belief that identical places and events exist.
Other classic named syndromes.
  • Cotard's syndrome (Dr Jules Cotard, 1882) — delusions that one is dead or does not exist; a nihilistic delusion; treatment: ECT.
  • Othello syndrome — delusional belief/overvalued idea that one's spouse/partner is unfaithful.
  • De Clérambault's syndrome (erotomania) — the patient, often a single woman, believes an exalted person is in love with her.
  • Charles-Bonnet syndrome — visual hallucinations without any other psychotic features or psychiatric disorder.
  • Couvade syndrome — a spouse complains of obstetric symptoms during his partner's pregnancy and parturition.
  • Diogenes syndrome — gross self-neglect, especially among elderly reclusive persons; often wealthy and intelligent; ~50% have no psychiatric illness (may have paranoid PD).
  • Ganser syndrome — giving approximate answers to simple questions; a dissociative disorder; can understand but can't give pointed answers; happens in prisoners; clouding of consciousness, hallucinations, delusions.
  • Kleine-Levin syndrome (KLS, "sleeping beauty disorder") — hypersomnolence, hypersexuality, hyperorality; early description by Alexandre Jacques François Brière de Boismont (1862); mostly adolescent males; spontaneous disappearance of symptoms.
  • Blocq's disease (astasia-abasia) — inability to walk/stand in a normal manner; bizarre gait not suggestive of an organic lesion; a conversion symptom (dissociative motor disorder in ICD-10 / conversion disorder in DSM-5).
  • Briquet's syndrome (St Louis hysteria) — multiple physical complaints in several systems with no physical cause; begins before age 30; chronic; frequent medical contact.
  • Ekbom's syndrome — unpleasant sensations predominantly in the legs (rarely arms); occur only at rest; worse in the evening/night.
  • Chronic fatigue (Myalgic encephalomyelitis) — fatigue, malaise, headache, pharyngitis, low-grade fever, cognitive complaints.
  • Restless legs syndrome — uncomfortable leg sensation (like insects crawling under skin); urge to move/walk; difficulty initiating sleep; medication: Ropinirole.
