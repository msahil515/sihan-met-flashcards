1. Defining abnormality (the 4 Ds)

  • Deviance — statistical or social norm violation.
  • Distress — subjective suffering.
  • Dysfunction — impairs daily life / role functioning.
  • Danger — risk to self or others.
Trap

Deviance alone is not abnormal. A genius IQ is statistically deviant but not pathological. You always need at least distress or dysfunction alongside it.

High-yield

Some textbooks list a 5th D, Duration, used for chronicity criteria (the recurring duration thresholds like ≥ 6 months, ≥ 2 years, etc. that define many disorders below).

4. The ICD: WHO's classification (full breakdown)

Definition

The ICD (International Statistical Classification of Diseases and Related Health Problems) is the WHO's global classification of all diseases, not just mental ones. Mental disorders occupy a single chapter of it. DSM (American Psychiatric Association) covers mental disorders only. So: ICD = whole-body, global, free, used for official health statistics; DSM = mental-only, US, paywalled, research-heavy. NIMHANS / India officially codes on ICD.

Trap

In ICD-10, OCD and PTSD sit inside F40–F48 (neurotic/stress-related), not as standalone chapters. That is the opposite of DSM-5, which pulled OCD and trauma out into their own chapters. Always know which manual the question is about.

4.5 ICD-11 — what actually changed

  • Coding format changed. Mental disorders moved from F-codes to alphanumeric 6-codes (Chapter 06). E.g. schizophrenia = 6A20, bipolar type I = 6A60.
  • Complex PTSD added. ICD-11 has PTSD AND Complex PTSD (PTSD core + the three "disturbances in self-organization" / DSO: affect dysregulation, negative self-concept, relationship disturbance). DSM-5 does not have Complex PTSD. High-yield difference.
  • Gaming disorder added as a formal diagnosis.
  • "Mental retardation" → "Disorders of intellectual development."
  • Gender incongruence moved out of the mental-disorders chapter into a new sexual-health chapter (depathologised).
  • Schizophrenia subtypes removed (paranoid, catatonic, etc. dropped), matching DSM-5; rated by symptom dimensions instead.
  • Bereavement → Prolonged grief disorder recognised.
  • Neurodevelopmental added to the chapter title; autism + ADHD grouped here.
  • Dimensional personality model: ICD-11 scrapped categorical PD types and uses severity + five trait domains (negative affectivity, detachment, dissociality, disinhibition, anankastia) with one retained pattern qualifier, borderline.
High-yield

NIMHANS angle: India officially codes on ICD. Expect "which body publishes ICD" (WHO), "ICD chapter for mental disorders" (Chapter V / F-codes in ICD-10), and an ICD-vs-DSM difference (Complex PTSD, free-vs-paid, global-vs-US, guidelines-vs-criteria).

6. Mental status examination (MSE)

Order matters — ABS-MTPCIJ: Appearance, Behavior, Speech, Mood/affect, Thought (form + content), Perception, Cognition, Insight, Judgment.

  • Mood = subjective ("how do you feel?"). Affect = observed (range, congruence, intensity).
  • Thought form = how thoughts flow (loose associations, tangential, flight of ideas, circumstantial, blocking, perseveration).
  • Thought content = what is thought (delusions, suicidal/homicidal ideation, obsessions, phobias).
  • Perception = hallucinations (auditory, visual, etc.), illusions, depersonalization.
  • Cognition = orientation, attention, memory, abstract thinking.
  • Insight = awareness of being ill. Judgment = decision-making in real situations.

7. Neurodevelopmental disorders

  • IDD (Intellectual Developmental Disorder) — onset in the developmental period; deficits in intellectual + adaptive functioning. Severity rated by adaptive function, not just IQ. (Formerly "Mental Retardation"; ICD-11: Disorders of Intellectual Development.)
  • Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) — social-communication deficits + restricted/repetitive behaviors. DSM-5 merged Asperger's, PDD-NOS, and autistic disorder into one spectrum.
  • ADHD — inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity ≥ 6 months; onset before 12; present in ≥ 2 settings.
  • Specific Learning Disorder — dyslexia (reading), dysgraphia (writing), dyscalculia (math).
  • Tic Disorders — Tourette's = motor + vocal tics ≥ 1 year, onset before 18.
  • Communication Disorders — language disorder, speech sound disorder, childhood-onset fluency disorder (stuttering), social (pragmatic) communication disorder.
Trap

ADHD onset cutoff is before 12 (DSM-5), changed from "before 7" (DSM-IV). Asperger's is no longer a separate diagnosis (folded into ASD).

8. Schizophrenia & other psychotic disorders

8.1 Conceptual history

  • Kraepelin — coined dementia praecox, emphasised early onset + deteriorating course.
  • Bleuler — renamed it schizophrenia, emphasised cognitive fragmentation. 4 As: Associations (loose), Affect (flat), Ambivalence, Autism (withdrawal — here "autism" = social withdrawal, NOT autism spectrum).
  • Schneider — First-Rank Symptoms (FRS): thought insertion/withdrawal/broadcasting, audible thoughts, voices commenting/arguing, made impulses/actions, somatic passivity, delusional perception. Suggestive but not diagnostic alone in DSM-5.

8.2 Symptom domains

  • Positive (added) — hallucinations, delusions, disorganized speech, grossly disorganized/catatonic behavior.
  • Negative (taken away) — flat affect, alogia (poverty of speech), avolition (low motivation), anhedonia, asociality.
  • Cognitive — attention, working memory, executive function deficits.

8.3 DSM-5 criteria for schizophrenia

  • ≥ 2 of (delusions, hallucinations, disorganized speech, grossly disorganized/catatonic behavior, negative symptoms), with at least one from the first three.
  • Active phase ≥ 1 month; total disturbance ≥ 6 months (including prodrome / residual).
  • Significant social or occupational dysfunction.
  • Not better explained by mood disorder, substance, or medical condition.

Trap

Schizophrenia ≠ DID. Schizophrenia = "split mind" (fragmented thought). DID = multiple identities. Different chapters, different mechanisms.

Trap

DSM-5 removed schizophrenia subtypes (paranoid, catatonic, etc.). They belong to DSM-IV. If subtypes appear in the answer choices, the question is testing DSM-IV vocabulary.

11. OCD & related disorders

  • OCD — obsessions (intrusive thoughts) + compulsions (repetitive acts to neutralize). Ego-dystonic. Time-consuming (≥ 1 hr/day) or distressing.
  • Body Dysmorphic Disorder (BDD) — preoccupation with a perceived flaw in appearance.
  • Hoarding Disorder — persistent difficulty discarding possessions. New in DSM-5.
  • Trichotillomania — recurrent hair-pulling.
  • Excoriation — recurrent skin-picking. New in DSM-5.
Trap

OCPD ≠ OCD. OCPD = perfectionist, rigid personality style, ego-syntonic, in the personality-disorders chapter. OCD = ego-dystonic obsessions/compulsions, in the OCD-related chapter. First-line therapy for OCD is ERP, not CBT alone (see §20).

12. Trauma- & stressor-related disorders

  • PTSD — exposure to actual/threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence + intrusion symptoms (flashbacks, nightmares) + avoidance + negative alterations in cognition/mood + arousal/reactivity. Duration > 1 month.
  • Acute Stress Disorder — same symptom clusters but duration 3 days to 1 month.
  • Adjustment Disorders — emotional/behavioral symptoms within 3 months of a stressor; does not meet criteria for another disorder.
  • Reactive Attachment Disorder (RAD) — withdrawn, emotionally inhibited toward caregivers; from severe early neglect.
  • Disinhibited Social Engagement Disorder (DSED) — overly familiar with strangers; from severe early neglect.
  • ICD-11 adds Complex PTSD — PTSD core + disturbances in self-organization (DSO): emotion dysregulation, negative self-concept, relational disturbance. DSM-5 does not have Complex PTSD.

13. Dissociative disorders

  • Dissociative Amnesia — gaps in autobiographical memory, often after trauma. Specifier: with dissociative fugue (sudden travel + identity confusion).
  • Dissociative Identity Disorder (DID) — ≥ 2 distinct personality states, recurrent gaps in recall. Strongly linked to severe early trauma. Renamed from Multiple Personality Disorder.
  • Depersonalization / Derealization Disorder — feeling unreal/detached from self or surroundings, reality testing intact (key differentiator from psychosis).

14. Somatic symptom & related disorders

DSM-5 renamed this chapter from "somatoform disorders." Key entries:

  • Somatic Symptom Disorder (SSD) — distressing somatic symptoms + excessive thoughts/behaviors about them. Symptoms may have a medical basis (the focus is on the response, not whether a cause exists).
  • Illness Anxiety Disorder (IAD) — preoccupation with having an illness, with few/no somatic symptoms. Replaces hypochondriasis.
  • Conversion Disorder (Functional Neurological Symptom) — neurological symptoms (paralysis, blindness, seizures) without medical explanation. La belle indifférence (oddly calm despite the symptom) is a soft sign.
  • Factitious Disorder — intentional faking for sick-role gain (no external incentive). Munchausen = on self; Munchausen by proxy = on another (often a child).
  • Malingering — conscious faking for external gain (money, avoiding work/court, drugs). NOT a mental disorder; coded as a V-code / Z-code.
Trap

The three are constantly confused. Conversion = unconscious, no gain (symptoms are real to the person). Factitious = conscious symptom production, internal sick-role gain. Malingering = conscious, external gain (money, court). La belle indifférence is a conversion soft sign.

16. Sleep-wake disorders

  • Insomnia Disorder — difficulty falling/staying asleep, ≥ 3 nights/wk, ≥ 3 months.
  • Hypersomnolence — excessive sleepiness despite ≥ 7 hr sleep.
  • Narcolepsy — recurrent sleep attacks; cataplexy (sudden muscle-tone loss) often present; low CSF hypocretin/orexin.
  • Sleep apnea — obstructive (most common) vs central.
  • Parasomnias — sleepwalking, sleep terrors (NREM); nightmare disorder, REM Sleep Behavior Disorder (REM, often pre-Parkinson's).
  • Circadian rhythm disorders — delayed/advanced sleep phase, shift-work type.

17. Substance-related & addictive disorders

  • DSM-5 collapsed "abuse" + "dependence" into a single Substance Use Disorder (SUD) spectrum (no more "abuse vs dependence").
  • Criteria: ≥ 2 of 11 in a 12-month period.
  • Severity: 2–3 mild; 4–5 moderate; ≥ 6 severe.
  • The 11 criteria cluster into 4 groups: impaired control, social impairment, risky use, pharmacological (tolerance, withdrawal).
  • Tolerance + withdrawal are pharmacological criteria but are not required for diagnosis.
  • Gambling Disorder is the one behavioral (non-substance) addiction formally in DSM-5.
Trap

Tolerance/withdrawal due to prescribed medications taken as directed do NOT count toward SUD criteria.

18.1 One-line distinguishers (Cluster B is the most exam-tested)

  • Antisocial — disregard for others' rights; age ≥ 18 + Conduct Disorder before 15. Higher in males.
  • Borderline — instability of relationships, self-image, and affect; impulsivity; fear of abandonment; chronic emptiness; self-harm common. Higher in females. First-line: DBT (Linehan).
  • Histrionic — attention-seeking, theatrical, sexually provocative.
  • Narcissistic — grandiosity, need for admiration, lack of empathy.
Trap

Schizoid vs Schizotypal. Schizoid = detached, indifferent to relationships, no eccentric beliefs. Schizotypal = detached + odd beliefs, magical thinking, perceptual oddities. Schizotypal lies on the schizophrenia spectrum; schizoid does not (in DSM-5).

Trap

OCPD ≠ OCD. OCPD = ego-syntonic perfectionism (personality chapter). OCD = ego-dystonic obsessions/compulsions (OCD-related chapter).

High-yield

ICD-11 abandoned these categorical clusters entirely, replacing them with a dimensional model (severity + five trait domains, plus a retained borderline pattern qualifier — see §4.5). DSM-5 keeps the 10 categorical disorders in 3 clusters.

19. Suicide risk assessment

SAD PERSONS: Sex (male), Age (< 19 or > 45), Depression, Previous attempt, Ethanol/drugs, Rational thinking loss, Social supports lacking, Organized plan, No spouse, Sickness.

Trap

Strongest single predictor of completed suicide = previous attempt. Most common method globally = hanging. Highest-risk diagnosis = MDD with comorbid substance use.

22. 30-second recall script
