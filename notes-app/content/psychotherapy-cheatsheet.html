1. Psychoanalytic / Psychodynamic

Freud, the scaffolding

  • Topographic model: conscious, preconscious, unconscious.
  • Structural model: id (pleasure principle, primary process), ego (reality principle, secondary process), superego (conscience + ego ideal).
  • Psychosexual stages: oral (0-1.5), anal (1.5-3), phallic (3-6) — Oedipus / Electra, latency (6-puberty), genital (puberty-adulthood). Fixation → adult character traits.
  • Defenses: Vaillant hierarchy: psychotic (denial, distortion) → immature (projection, acting out, regression, somatization) → neurotic (intellectualisation, displacement, reaction formation, repression, isolation of affect) → mature (humour, sublimation, suppression, altruism, anticipation).

Classical technique

  • Free association — the fundamental rule.
  • Interpretation of resistances and transference.
  • Dream analysis — manifest vs latent content.
  • Working through — repeated re-examination across contexts until insight becomes integrated and behavioural change emerges. Distinguishes intellectual from emotional/behavioural insight.
  • Frequency: classical psychoanalysis 3-5×/week, couch, years. Psychoanalytic / psychodynamic therapy: 1-2×/week, face-to-face, months-years.

MCQ trap: Working through is NOT free association nor a single interpretation; it is repeated re-encounter with the same conflict/insight in shifting contexts until consolidated. Common distractor in MET / NIMHANS.

2. Behaviour Therapy

Founders: Joseph Wolpe, Hans Eysenck, B. F. Skinner, Albert Bandura. Mechanism: directly manipulate antecedents, behaviours, consequences via classical + operant conditioning + social learning.

MCQ trap: ERP is the first-line behavioural treatment for OCD (SSRIs first-line pharmacotherapy). Systematic desensitisation is for phobias, not OCD.

3. Cognitive and Cognitive-Behavioural Therapy

Beck's Cognitive Therapy

  • Cognitive triad of depression: negative views of (1) self, (2) world/experience, (3) future.
  • Schemas → automatic thoughts → emotion/behaviour.
  • Common cognitive distortions: all-or-nothing thinking, overgeneralisation, mental filter, disqualifying the positive, jumping to conclusions (mind reading / fortune telling), magnification or minimisation, emotional reasoning, "should" statements, labelling, personalisation.
  • Techniques: Socratic questioning (guided discovery), thought records, downward arrow, behavioural experiments, activity scheduling, graded task assignment.
  • Indications: depression (originator), anxiety, OCD, PTSD, eating disorders, psychosis (CBTp).

Ellis's REBT (Rational Emotive Behaviour Therapy)

  • ABC model: A activating event → B beliefs (rational or irrational) → C consequences (emotional + behavioural). Then D dispute, E new effect.
  • Three core irrational demands: I must do well; others must treat me well; the world must be easy/fair.
  • Distinct from Beck: Ellis confronts irrational beliefs more directly; Beck uses Socratic discovery. Both target cognition-emotion-behaviour loop.

4. Humanistic / Existential

Rogers — person-centred therapy (PCT)

  • Three core conditions (1957, necessary + sufficient): empathy, unconditional positive regard (UPR), congruence (genuineness).
  • Actualising tendency as the engine. Therapist's job: provide the conditions; the client does the work.
  • Non-directive. Reflection of feeling. No interpretation.
  • Self vs ideal-self congruence as outcome marker (Q-sort).

Gestalt — Fritz Perls

  • Focus on the here-and-now, awareness of present experience, "figure-ground" of attention.
  • Techniques: empty chair (dialogue with absent person / disowned part of self), top-dog / underdog dialogue, exaggeration, "I" statements, attention to non-verbals, integration of polarities.
  • Goal: integrate disowned parts → wholeness.

Existential — Frankl, May, Yalom

  • Viktor Frankl — Logotherapy. Search for meaning. Three sources of meaning: creative (work / contribution), experiential (love / beauty), attitudinal (response to unavoidable suffering). Techniques: paradoxical intention, dereflection.
  • Rollo May. Existential anxiety vs neurotic anxiety. Daimonic. Freedom and destiny.
  • Irvin Yalom — 4 "ultimate concerns": death, freedom (responsibility), isolation, meaninglessness. Group therapy specialist (next section).

Glasser — Reality / Choice Theory

  • WDEP framework: Wants, Doing, Evaluation, Planning. Focus on present, responsibility, total behaviour. Used in school + corrections settings.

5. Group Therapy — Yalom

The group is a social microcosm; relationship patterns from outside life replay in the group. Therapist's job is to surface the here-and-now process.

Yalom's 11 therapeutic factors

  1. Instillation of hope
  2. Universality ("I'm not the only one")
  3. Imparting information
  4. Altruism (helping others is itself therapeutic)
  5. Corrective recapitulation of the primary family group
  6. Development of socialising techniques
  7. Imitative behaviour
  8. Interpersonal learning (the engine, paired with here-and-now)
  9. Group cohesiveness (the group analogue of the therapeutic alliance)
  10. Catharsis
  11. Existential factors (responsibility, isolation, meaning, mortality)

Process group format: 6-10 members + therapist(s). Bion's group dynamics (basic assumption groups: dependency, fight-flight, pairing) influential. MCQ: "social microcosm" + "here-and-now" + "interpersonal learning" → Yalom.

7. Third-Wave / Integrative

DBT — Dialectical Behaviour Therapy (Linehan, 1993)

  • Synthesis of behavioural change + Zen acceptance ("dialectics").
  • Primary target: BPD with chronic suicidality.
  • 4 skill modules: mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation, interpersonal effectiveness.
  • Standard programme: weekly individual therapy + weekly group skills training + phone coaching + therapist consultation team. ~1 year.
  • Hierarchy of targets: life-threatening behaviours → therapy-interfering behaviours → quality-of-life-interfering behaviours.

ACT — Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (Hayes)

  • Goal: psychological flexibility, not symptom reduction.
  • Hexaflex (6 processes): Acceptance, Cognitive Defusion, Self-as-Context, Present-Moment Contact, Values, Committed Action.
  • Each opposed by an inflexibility process (experiential avoidance, fusion, attachment to conceptualised self, etc.).
  • Underpinned by Relational Frame Theory (RFT).
  • Techniques: defusion ("I'm having the thought that…"), values clarification, metaphors (passengers on the bus, tug-of-war with a monster).

MBCT — Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy (Segal, Williams, Teasdale)

  • 8-week group programme combining MBSR (Kabat-Zinn) + cognitive therapy.
  • Indication: relapse prevention in recurrent depression (≥3 episodes).

MBSR — Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (Kabat-Zinn 1979)

  • 8-week group programme. Body scan, sitting meditation, mindful movement (yoga). Chronic pain, stress, anxiety.

IPT — Interpersonal Therapy (Klerman & Weissman, 1984)

  • Time-limited (12-16 sessions). Originally for MDD.
  • Four problem areas: grief, role disputes, role transitions, interpersonal deficits. Choose one as the focus.
  • Evidence base extends to bulimia, postpartum depression, adolescent depression.

MI — Motivational Interviewing (Miller & Rollnick, 1991, 3rd ed. 2013)

  • Spirit (PACE): Partnership, Acceptance, Compassion, Evocation.
  • Processes: Engaging → Focusing → Evoking → Planning.
  • Core skills (OARS): Open questions, Affirmations, Reflections, Summaries.
  • Listen for and amplify change talk (DARN-CAT: Desire, Ability, Reason, Need → Commitment, Activation, Taking steps).
  • Indication: ambivalence, especially in SUDs; also weight loss, medication adherence, HIV risk.

EMDR (Francine Shapiro, 1987)

  • 8-phase protocol pairing brief recall of trauma memory with bilateral stimulation (eye movements / taps / tones).
  • Indication: PTSD (APA, NICE, WHO endorsed).
  • Mechanism debated (working-memory taxation, REM-like processing, dual attention). Comparable to trauma-focused CBT in head-to-head trials.

CPT — Cognitive Processing Therapy (Resick & Schnicke)

  • 12-session manualised trauma-focused CBT for PTSD. Stuck points → impact statement → Socratic dialogue + worksheets.

Prolonged Exposure (Foa)

  • Imaginal + in-vivo exposure to trauma memories and avoided stimuli. Manualised. Equally evidence-based for PTSD.

TFP / MBT for BPD

  • TFP — Transference-Focused Psychotherapy (Kernberg): twice-weekly psychodynamic, here-and-now transference interpretation, "object relations dyads."
  • MBT — Mentalization-Based Treatment (Bateman & Fonagy): builds capacity to understand own and others' mental states. Day-hospital and outpatient versions.

9. Common-Factors Framework

Research (Lambert, Wampold) finds outcome variance roughly: ~40% client + extra-therapeutic factors, ~30% relationship (alliance), ~15% expectancy / placebo, ~15% specific technique. Implication: most schools work, mostly via common factors (therapeutic alliance, empathy, expectancy, mobilisation of client resources). Specific techniques matter most for specific disorders with strong protocols (ERP for OCD, exposure for PTSD, behavioural activation for depression).

Bordin's working alliance: goals + tasks + bond.

11. Ethics + Frame (MET-style MCQ traps)

  • Boundaries: dual relationships generally avoided; rural and minority-community practice acknowledged exceptions.
  • Confidentiality limits: imminent harm to self / others (Tarasoff duty to warn / protect), child abuse, court order.
  • Informed consent: nature of therapy, alternatives, risks, fees, limits of confidentiality.
  • Multicultural competence: awareness, knowledge, skills (Sue & Sue).
  • Termination: planned, gradual, address relapse-prevention, leave the door open.

12. 12 MCQ Traps to Lock

  1. Working through ≠ free association. It is repeated re-encounter with the conflict/interpretation across contexts.
  2. ERP is for OCD; systematic desensitisation is for phobias.
  3. Beck = cognitive triad + Socratic; Ellis = ABC + dispute. Don't swap.
  4. Rogers' three core conditions are empathy, UPR, congruence. No "interpretation."
  5. Empty chair = Gestalt, not psychoanalysis.
  6. DBT was developed for BPD (Linehan), not for depression. 4 modules: mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation, interpersonal effectiveness.
  7. ACT hexaflex: 6 processes (acceptance, defusion, self-as-context, present moment, values, committed action). Not 4, not 8.
  8. IPT 4 problem areas: grief, role disputes, role transitions, interpersonal deficits. Not "thoughts / feelings / behaviours / body."
  9. Motivational Interviewing spirit = PACE (Partnership, Acceptance, Compassion, Evocation); skills = OARS.
  10. Yalom's "here-and-now" + "social microcosm" are group-therapy markers.
  11. Minuchin = structural family therapy; enmeshed vs disengaged boundaries; joining + restructuring.
  12. EMDR = Shapiro, 1987, 8 phases, for PTSD. Mechanism debated; efficacy comparable to TF-CBT.
