7 · Your dissertation, compressed

The full defence sheet is at /dissertation/ and you should read it tonight, every number there comes off your own Results tables. This is the tomorrow-morning compression. If they open the dissertation thread, lead with the pitch, then stop and let them pick where to dig.

Tell us about your dissertation.

"We looked at how pornography consumption relates to personality, aggression and sexual compulsivity in young adults in India. Quantitative, cross-sectional survey, 214 participants aged 18 to 28, run through a Google Form, using the BFI-10 for the Big Five, the Buss-Perry Aggression Questionnaire Short Form, and the Sexual Compulsivity Scale, plus a short screening section on consumption habits. Analysed in SPSS with Spearman's rank correlation and logistic regression. The headline findings: men were about 2.75 times more likely to consume, sexual compulsivity was the strongest predictor of both whether and how often, higher neuroticism predicted lower consumption, and aggression and sexual compulsivity were positively correlated. So impulse control and personality, not aggression on its own, drove the consumption pattern."

Why Spearman and not Pearson?
Ordinal data and non-normal distributions. Spearman works on ranks, does not assume normality or linearity, only a monotonic relationship.
Why logistic regression?
The outcome was categorical. Binary logistic for "ever consumed or not", ordinal logistic for frequency. Linear regression would have been wrong for a categorical outcome.
What does Exp(B) = 2.752 mean?
The odds ratio. Being male multiplied the odds of consumption by about 2.75, holding the other predictors constant. It is odds, not probability, and say it that way.
Biggest limitation?
Convenience sampling through social media, so it is not representative, plus self-report on a taboo topic, so under-reporting is likely, and cross-sectional design, so no causal claim. The BFI-10 has only two items per trait, which is low reliability.
Can you claim causation?
No. Cross-sectional and correlational. Say "predicted" and "associated with", never "caused". This is the single most common trip-up.
What was your role, on a three-person dissertation?
Name it concretely and honestly: your part in design, data collection, the SPSS analysis and the write-up. Do not claim all of it, and do not shrink into "we".
The ethics of studying pornography?
Informed consent, adults 18+ only, full anonymity with no identifying data collected, a written debrief with contact details, and the option to withdraw. Volunteer this before they ask it.
What would you do differently?
Full-length personality measure, a probability or at least stratified sample, and a longitudinal or at minimum two-time-point design to say anything directional.

8 · Your clinical experience

What is your therapeutic approach?

"I would call myself integrative with a cognitive-behavioural spine and a person-centred relational base. Practically, I am state-driven rather than modality-driven: I read where the client is before I pick a technique. If they are flooded, I go to containment and grounding and I do not try to restructure anything. If they are stuck on a thought, that is where CBT or ACT-style work goes. If they are ambivalent about changing something, motivational interviewing, roll with the resistance rather than argue. If they are functionally stuck, behavioural activation and problem-solving. If they are self-critical, compassion-focused work. Validate first, stabilise before exploring."

This is your real style, documented off your own sessions, not a school you are performing. Panels can smell a borrowed answer in one follow-up question, and they always ask the follow-up.

Tell us about a difficult case.

Pick one from your YourDOST work with a clear arc and keep it de-identified: no name, no identifying detail. Structure it as: presentation, what you formulated, what you did and why, what happened, and what you would do differently now. End on the limitation, that is where the marks are: "In hindsight I would have screened for risk more formally at intake rather than relying on how they presented in session."

A client tells you they are thinking of ending their life. What do you do?

"First, I stay with it rather than moving off it, and I ask directly. Asking about suicide does not plant the idea. I assess risk properly: are there thoughts, is there a plan, is there means and access, is there intent and a timeframe, have there been past attempts, what is the level of hopelessness, what support is around them right now. Then I act on that. I do not hold a serious risk case alone in confidence, this is one of the recognised limits of confidentiality. I would involve my supervisor immediately, work on safety planning with the client, restrict access to means where possible, involve a support person with as much of the client's agreement as I can get, and arrange psychiatric assessment if there is intent or a plan. And I would say clearly that under the Mental Healthcare Act 2017 a person who attempts suicide is presumed to be under severe stress and is not to be punished, they get care, which is exactly the framing I would use with the family too."

Risk questions are pass or fail. They are not testing empathy, they are testing whether you assess, whether you escalate, and whether you know you are not the last line alone.

How do you handle a client you cannot help?

"Recognise it early and refer, that is competence, not failure. Practising outside your competence is an ethical breach. I would name it with the client honestly, make a warm handover rather than just giving a number, and stay available through the transition."

9 · Psychopathology drill

Answer these in two sentences each, out loud. If they are testing content on a viva, this is where most of it will come from.

They askYou say
Schizophrenia, core featuresDelusions, hallucinations, disorganised speech, grossly disorganised or catatonic behaviour, negative symptoms. DSM-5-TR needs two or more, at least one from the first three, for a significant portion of one month, with continuous disturbance for six months.
Positive vs negative symptomsPositive are additions to normal experience: hallucinations, delusions, thought disorder. Negative are losses: avolition, alogia, anhedonia, affective flattening, asociality. Negative symptoms respond less well to antipsychotics and drive long-term disability.
Schizophrenia vs schizoaffective vs mood disorder with psychotic featuresSchizoaffective needs a mood episode concurrent with the psychosis plus at least two weeks of delusions or hallucinations in the absence of a mood episode. If psychosis only ever occurs during mood episodes, it is a mood disorder with psychotic features.
Bipolar I vs IIBipolar I needs one manic episode, at least a week or any duration if hospitalised, depression is common but not required. Bipolar II needs a hypomanic episode, at least four days, plus a major depressive episode, and no full mania ever.
Major depressive episodeFive or more symptoms over two weeks including depressed mood or anhedonia, plus appetite or weight change, sleep disturbance, psychomotor change, fatigue, worthlessness or guilt, poor concentration, thoughts of death.
DysthymiaPersistent depressive disorder. Depressed mood most of the day, more days than not, for at least two years in adults, one year in children and adolescents.
GAD vs panic disorderGAD is excessive, uncontrollable worry across multiple domains, more days than not for six months, with restlessness, fatigue, poor concentration, irritability, muscle tension, sleep problems. Panic disorder is recurrent unexpected panic attacks, peaking within minutes, plus a month of worry about further attacks or a behaviour change because of them.
OCDObsessions are intrusive, unwanted, ego-dystonic thoughts, images or urges. Compulsions are repetitive acts or mental acts done to reduce the resulting distress. Time-consuming, over an hour a day, or causing marked impairment. Treatment of choice is ERP, exposure and response prevention.
PTSDExposure to actual or threatened death, serious injury or sexual violence, then intrusion symptoms, avoidance, negative alterations in cognition and mood, and altered arousal and reactivity, for over a month. Under a month it is acute stress disorder.
Personality disorder clustersCluster A odd or eccentric: paranoid, schizoid, schizotypal. Cluster B dramatic, emotional, erratic: antisocial, borderline, histrionic, narcissistic. Cluster C anxious or fearful: avoidant, dependent, obsessive-compulsive personality disorder.
OCD vs OCPDOCD is ego-dystonic, the person does not want the obsessions. OCPD is ego-syntonic, a pervasive pattern of perfectionism, orderliness and control that the person sees as correct.
Delirium vs dementiaDelirium is acute onset, fluctuating course, impaired attention and consciousness, usually reversible, medically driven. Dementia, major neurocognitive disorder, is insidious in onset, progressive, memory and other cognitive domains, consciousness clear until late.
Illusion vs hallucination vs delusionIllusion is a misperception of a real stimulus. Hallucination is a perception without any external stimulus. Delusion is a fixed false belief held against evidence and not explained by culture.
Neurosis vs psychosis, if asked the old wayNeurotic conditions preserve reality testing and insight, psychotic conditions do not. The terms are dropped from DSM-5-TR but still asked in Indian vivas.
DSM-5-TR vs ICD-11DSM is the American Psychiatric Association's manual, criteria-based, used heavily in research. ICD is the WHO's, used for official morbidity coding and it is what India works with. ICD-11 is more clinically descriptive and less rigidly criterion-counting, it added a dimensional model for personality disorders and it includes complex PTSD and gaming disorder.

What is a mental status examination?

"The systematic cross-sectional description of a patient's current mental functioning: appearance and behaviour, speech, mood and affect, thought form and thought content, perception, cognition covering orientation, attention, memory and intelligence, then judgement and insight. It is to psychiatry what a physical examination is to medicine, a snapshot of now, not a history."

What makes a test a good test?

"Reliability, validity, standardisation and norms. Reliability is consistency: test-retest, split-half, inter-rater, internal consistency measured by Cronbach's alpha. Validity is whether it measures what it claims: content, criterion which splits into concurrent and predictive, and construct which splits into convergent and discriminant. A test can be reliable without being valid, but it cannot be valid without being reliable."

12 · Ethics and law, the section that separates people

What are the limits of confidentiality?

"Confidentiality is the default and I would say so to the client at the start, as part of informed consent, along with its limits. It breaks when there is serious risk of harm to the client, risk of harm to an identifiable other, in the case of child abuse where reporting is mandatory under POCSO, and where a court orders disclosure. Even then I disclose the minimum necessary to the right person, and wherever possible I tell the client what I am doing and why, rather than going behind them."

The structure matters more than the list: default, stated up front, specific exceptions, minimum necessary, told to the client. That is what a trained clinician sounds like.

What is duty to warn?

"It comes from the Tarasoff case in the United States, where the ruling was that a therapist has a duty to protect an identifiable third party from a serious threat made by a client. In practice it means confidentiality does not cover a credible, specific threat to a specific person. Indian practice does not have a Tarasoff statute, but the same ethical principle applies: harm prevention outweighs confidentiality when the risk is serious and identifiable, and you act through your supervisor."

Tell us about the Mental Healthcare Act 2017.

"It replaced the Mental Health Act 1987 and it is rights-based rather than custody-based, aligned with the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities. The key pieces: it gives every person the right to access mental healthcare, it introduces the advance directive so a person can state in advance how they wish to be treated, and the nominated representative to act for them. It sets up the Central and State Mental Health Authorities and Mental Health Review Boards. It presumes capacity, and it restricts admission without consent to defined supported-admission situations with review. It bans cruel or degrading treatment and restricts ECT, no ECT without anaesthesia and muscle relaxants and not for minors except with specific safeguards. And Section 115 says a person who attempts suicide is presumed to be under severe stress and is not to be prosecuted, which effectively decriminalised attempted suicide."

This is the single most likely law question for a clinical psychology panel in India, and most candidates can only say "it decriminalised suicide". Advance directive, nominated representative, review boards and presumption of capacity is what a prepared answer sounds like.

Law or codeWhat to be able to say

RPwD Act 2016Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, replaced the 1995 Act, expanded to 21 conditions including mental illness, specific learning disabilities, autism spectrum disorder and intellectual disability. Reservation, accessibility, non-discrimination, certification of disability.

13 · Stats and research methods

You put a quantitative dissertation in front of them, so this is fair game and you should want it. Two-sentence answers.

TermAnswer
Null vs alternative hypothesisThe null states no effect or no difference, the alternative states there is one. You test the null and either reject it or fail to reject it, you never "accept" it.
p valueThe probability of getting a result at least this extreme if the null hypothesis were true. It is not the probability that the hypothesis is true, and say that, because they often check.
Type I and Type II errorType I is rejecting a true null, a false positive, its rate is alpha. Type II is failing to reject a false null, a false negative, its rate is beta. Power is 1 minus beta.
Parametric vs non-parametricParametric tests assume normality, homogeneity of variance and interval data, non-parametric tests do not and work on ranks. Your Spearman was non-parametric for exactly this reason.
Test pairs to knowt-test to Mann-Whitney U, paired t-test to Wilcoxon signed-rank, one-way ANOVA to Kruskal-Wallis, repeated-measures ANOVA to Friedman, Pearson to Spearman.
Correlation vs regressionCorrelation gives the strength and direction of association, regression predicts an outcome from one or more predictors and gives you the equation.
Reliability vs validityReliability is consistency, validity is accuracy. Reliable but invalid is possible, valid but unreliable is not.
Internal vs external validityInternal validity is whether the effect is really due to the independent variable, external validity is whether it generalises. Lab experiments are strong on the first, weak on the second.
SamplingProbability: simple random, stratified, systematic, cluster. Non-probability: convenience, purposive, quota, snowball. Yours was convenience, and it is a stated limitation.
Cross-sectional vs longitudinalCross-sectional is one time point, cheap and fast, no causal or developmental inference. Longitudinal follows the same people over time, gives direction, costs attrition.
Confounding variableA third variable related to both the predictor and the outcome, which creates or hides an apparent relationship. Controlled by randomisation, matching, or statistically in the model.
Standard deviation vs standard errorSD describes spread in the sample, SE describes the precision of the sample mean as an estimate of the population mean.

15 · What to ask them

They will ask if you have questions. "No sir" is a wasted opportunity. Ask two, maximum, and make them about training, never about fees or hostels.

  • "Could you tell me how the clinical postings are structured across the two years, which settings we rotate through?"
  • "How does supervision work, is it individual or group, and how early do we start seeing patients?"
  • "Is there scope to continue research alongside the coursework? My dissertation was on behavioural addiction and impulse control and I would like to keep working in that area."
  • "How does this MA sit with respect to RCI registration and what does it qualify us for on completion?" (Only if the site did not answer it clearly. Asking is fine, assuming is not.)

16 · Traps

Do not say "it caused". Not about your dissertation, not about anything correlational. Say "was associated with" or "predicted". It is the fastest way for a research-trained panel to mark you down.
Do not diagnose from a vignette in one line. If they read you a case, say what you would want to rule out, what else you would ask, and what you would assess, then give your provisional impression. "It is depression" is a weaker answer than "my first impression is a depressive episode, but I would want to rule out a bipolar history, thyroid dysfunction and substance use before I settled on it."
Do not oversell the online counselling. You have real hours and a real number, use it. But if you imply it is equivalent to hospital-based clinical work, someone on that panel will take it apart. The honest framing, that you have volume and relational skill but not ward-based severe-illness exposure, is stronger and it is true.
Do not invent Manipal facts. No faculty names, no centre names, no accreditation claims you did not verify tonight. You are talking to the people who run it.
Do not bluff a blank. Say "I am not sure sir, but my understanding is..." and give your reasoning, or say "I do not know that one, I will read it up." Panels forgive a gap, they do not forgive being fed a confident wrong answer, and on a recorded session it sits there permanently.
Do not badmouth anything. Not your M.Sc, not your college, not YourDOST, not another institute. Ever. It is the single most reliable way to fail a viva on personality grounds.

17 · If it turns out to be a written or MCQ test

  • Descriptive: definition first, then theorist and year if you know it, then two or three points, then one line of clinical relevance. Panels mark for structure. Headings and short paragraphs beat a wall of text.
  • MCQ: if the marking is negative, the site standard for your mocks is +1 and −0.25, so anything you can cut to two options is still worth attempting, and a total blank is not. Eliminate first, guess second.
  • Time: one pass answering everything you know cold, one pass on the rest. Do not stall on question 3 for four minutes.
  • If it is oral rapid-fire: short answers. Two sentences. Stop talking. Rambling invites a harder follow-up and burns the goodwill you built in the first minute.

Most people in that lobby tomorrow have one of those three. Go in and be accurate rather than impressive, because accurate is impressive to this particular panel.
