Therapy Components — what belongs to what

Recognition drill for "which of these is a component of ___" items · Built 2026-05-21

Why this page exists. Sihan flagged that the real MET paper asks "which one of these is a component of DBT?" style questions across many therapies. Those are recognition items: they give you 4 things and you pick the one that genuinely belongs. The trap is always a real concept from a different therapy. So for each school below you get two lists: the components that belong and the distractors that don't (and where they actually come from). Learn both sides and the question becomes free.

This is the companion to the full psychotherapy cheatsheet, which covers founders + mechanism. This page is purely "what are the named parts."

DBT — Dialectical Behaviour Therapy (Linehan)

4 skill modules: Mindfulness · Distress Tolerance · Emotion Regulation · Interpersonal Effectiveness.

Mnemonic: "My Dog Eats Iguanas" (Mindfulness, Distress tolerance, Emotion reg, Interpersonal).

4 modes of treatment: individual therapy · group skills training · phone/between-session coaching · therapist consultation team.

NOT a DBT module: "cognitive restructuring" / "cognitive triad" (that's Beck) · "free association" (Freud) · "hexaflex" or "cognitive defusion" (ACT) · "unconditional positive regard" (Rogers). DBT uses acceptance + change ("dialectics") and is built for BPD with chronic suicidality.

ACT — Acceptance & Commitment Therapy (Hayes)

Hexaflex, 6 processes: Acceptance · Cognitive Defusion · Self-as-Context · Present-Moment Contact · Values · Committed Action. Goal = psychological flexibility (not symptom reduction).

NOT ACT: the 4 DBT modules · "ABC model" (Ellis) · "miracle question" (SFBT). If you see "hexaflex," "defusion," "values," or "I'm having the thought that…" it is ACT.

CBT — Beck's Cognitive Therapy

Components: cognitive triad (negative view of self / world / future) · schemas → automatic thoughts · cognitive distortions (all-or-nothing, overgeneralisation, catastrophising, etc.).

Techniques: Socratic questioning (guided discovery) · thought records · downward arrow · behavioural experiments · activity scheduling.

NOT Beck: "ABC model / irrational beliefs / dispute" → that's Ellis (REBT). "Reciprocal inhibition" → Wolpe. The cognitive triad is Beck; the ABC is Ellis. Examiners swap these constantly.

REBT — Rational Emotive Behaviour Therapy (Ellis)

ABCDE model: Activating event → Belief (rational/irrational) → Consequence → Dispute → Effect (new).

Core: three irrational demands ("I must do well; others must treat me well; the world must be fair"), aka "musturbation."

NOT REBT: "cognitive triad," "automatic thoughts," "Socratic questioning" → Beck. Ellis is more directly confrontational; Beck uses gentle guided discovery.

Person-Centred Therapy (Rogers)

3 core conditions (necessary & sufficient): Empathy · Unconditional Positive Regard (UPR) · Congruence (genuineness).

Mnemonic: "EUC" / "the 3 C's if you count empathic-understanding, unconditional-regard, congruence."

NOT Rogers: "interpretation," "transference" (Freud) · "empty chair" (Gestalt) · "directive advice" (Rogers is non-directive). Driver = the client's actualising tendency.

Motivational Interviewing (Miller & Rollnick)

Spirit — PACE: Partnership · Acceptance · Compassion · Evocation.

4 processes: Engaging → Focusing → Evoking → Planning.

Core skills — OARS: Open questions · Affirmations · Reflections · Summaries.

Change talk — DARN-CAT: Desire, Ability, Reason, Need → Commitment, Activation, Taking steps.

NOT MI: "confrontation / arguing the client out of it" (the opposite of MI) · "OARS" is MI, not a family-therapy term. Used most for ambivalence in substance use.

IPT — Interpersonal Therapy (Klerman & Weissman)

4 problem areas (pick one focus): Grief · Role disputes · Role transitions · Interpersonal deficits. Time-limited (12–16 sessions), originally for depression.

NOT IPT: "transference" (it deliberately stays in the here-and-now of relationships, not the past) · "cognitive distortions" (Beck). If you see "role dispute / role transition" it's IPT.

Group Therapy — Yalom's 11 therapeutic factors

The 11: Instillation of hope · Universality · Imparting information · Altruism · Corrective recapitulation of the primary family group · Development of socialising techniques · Imitative behaviour · Interpersonal learning (the engine) · Group cohesiveness (group analogue of the alliance) · Catharsis · Existential factors.

NOT a Yalom factor: "transference interpretation," "free association," "the miracle question." Tag words: social microcosm + here-and-now + interpersonal learning → Yalom.

Gestalt Therapy (Perls)

Components/techniques: empty chair · top-dog / underdog dialogue · here-and-now awareness · figure-ground · exaggeration · "I" statements · integration of polarities.

NOT Gestalt: "free association / dream interpretation" (Freud) · "core conditions" (Rogers). Goal = integrate disowned parts into wholeness.

Psychoanalysis / Psychodynamic (Freud)

Components/techniques: free association (the fundamental rule) · interpretation of resistance and transference · dream analysis (manifest vs latent) · working through.

NOT psychoanalysis: "behavioural experiment," "thought record" (CBT) · "miracle question" (SFBT). Working through = repeated re-encounter with a conflict until integrated, NOT a single interpretation or free association — classic NIMHANS trap.

Reality / Choice Therapy (Glasser)

WDEP framework: Wants · Doing · Evaluation · Planning. Present-focused, responsibility, "total behaviour."

NOT Glasser: "WDEP" is uniquely his — don't confuse with MI's "PACE/OARS" or DBT's modules.

Mindfulness-based programmes

MBCT (Segal, Williams, Teasdale): 8-week group, MBSR + cognitive therapy. Indication = relapse prevention in recurrent depression (≥3 episodes).

MBSR (Kabat-Zinn): 8-week group, body scan + sitting meditation + mindful movement. Stress, chronic pain.

Trap: MBCT is for preventing relapse, not treating an acute episode. MBSR came first (1979); MBCT added the cognitive layer.

EMDR (Shapiro)

8-phase protocol pairing brief recall of the traumatic memory with bilateral stimulation (eye movements). Indication = PTSD / trauma.

NOT EMDR: "exposure + response prevention" (that's ERP for OCD). EMDR = trauma + bilateral stimulation + 8 phases.

Techniques: paradoxical intention · dereflection. 3 sources of meaning: creative (work), experiential (love/beauty), attitudinal (stance toward unavoidable suffering).

The 6 traps that catch everyone

  1. Triad vs ABC. Cognitive triad = Beck. ABC = Ellis. Both are CBT-family, easy to swap.
  2. DBT modules vs ACT hexaflex. 4 modules (mindfulness/distress tolerance/emotion reg/interpersonal) = DBT. 6 hexaflex processes = ACT. Both use "mindfulness," so don't decide on that word alone.
  3. Working through. It is the repeated re-examination of a conflict until integrated, NOT free association and NOT one interpretation.
  4. Empty chair = Gestalt, not psychoanalysis. People assume "chair → couch → Freud." Wrong.
  5. ERP vs EMDR. ERP (exposure + response prevention) = OCD. EMDR (8 phases + eye movements) = trauma. Different conditions.
  6. Paradoxical intention shows up in BOTH Frankl and strategic family therapy. Read the stem for context.
