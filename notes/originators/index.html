Originators — "name the thing + who gave it"

The "who proposed what" layer NIMHANS bolts on top of the clinical stuff. Named effects, complexes, personality concepts, social-psych theories, motivation theories and psychometrics terms, each pinned to the person who coined it. Built 2026-05-24 after your prof flagged Icarus complex, McCall's T and reactance as the kind of curveball they ask.

Why this page exists. Your professor said the exam asked: the Icarus complex and who gave it, McCall's T, and reactance. None are core clinical. The tell: NIMHANS tests a layer of "who coined this named effect / complex / concept." So learn the pairing, not just the idea. The three he named are at the top, in the red box, first.

0. The three your prof named

Lock these cold.
TermWhoWhat it is
Icarus complexHenry MurrayA personality pattern of soaring ambition + craving for attention/admiration that ends in a fall (fascination with fire, ascent, falling, narcissism). Same Murray who gave the TAT, needs/press theory and personology.
McCall's T scoreWilliam McCallA standardised score with mean 50, SD 10. Psychometrics, not clinical. Lives next to z-scores, stanines and percentiles.
(Psychological) reactanceJack Brehm (1966)The motivational push-back when you feel a freedom is being threatened or removed: you want the forbidden thing more. Social psych / motivation.
Pattern to take away: personality theorists and their named complexes, social-psych theories with their author, and test/measurement jargon with the mean+SD. Everything below is that same layer, widened.

1. Named effects & complexes

The classic "____ effect / ____ complex, who gave it" format. If a question names an effect, it usually wants the author.

Effect / complexOriginatorWhat it is
Jonah complexAbraham MaslowFear of your own greatness; avoiding self-actualisation. (Opposite flavour to Icarus.)
Inferiority / superiority complexAlfred AdlerCore of Adlerian theory; overcompensation for felt inferiority.
Zeigarnik effectBluma ZeigarnikWe remember interrupted / incomplete tasks better than completed ones.
Halo effectEdward ThorndikeOne good trait (looks) bleeds into global positive judgement. (Reverse = horns effect.)
Barnum / Forer effectBertram ForerAccepting vague, general personality descriptions as uniquely true of you (horoscopes).
Pygmalion effectRosenthal & JacobsonTeacher's high expectations → student performs better. (Self-fulfilling prophecy in class.) Low expectation → Golem effect.
Hawthorne effectElton Mayo (Western Electric studies)People change behaviour because they know they're being observed.
Mere-exposure effectRobert ZajoncLiking something more simply from repeated exposure.
Garcia effect (taste aversion)John GarciaOne-trial conditioned aversion to a taste; biological preparedness; defies classic CC rules.
Flynn effectJames FlynnPopulation IQ scores have risen generation on generation.
Matthew effectRobert Merton"Rich get richer" — early advantage compounds (used in reading/education).
Stroop effectJ. Ridley StroopSlower to name ink colour when it spells a conflicting colour word (attention/interference).
Cocktail-party effectColin CherrySelective attention: tuning one voice out of many, but still catching your name.
Von Restorff (isolation) effectHedwig von RestorffThe odd-one-out item in a list is remembered best.
Bystander effectLatané & DarleyMore onlookers → less likely any one helps (diffusion of responsibility). After Kitty Genovese.
Ringelmann effectMax RingelmannPer-person effort drops as group size grows (precursor to social loafing).
Dunning–Kruger effectDunning & KrugerLow-skill people overestimate their competence.
Werther effectDavid PhillipsSpike in copycat suicides after a publicised one.

6. The standard-score family (so McCall's T sits in context)

ScoreMeanSDNote
z-score01The base; everything else is a rescaled z
T score (McCall)5010No negatives, no decimals needed; MMPI uses T
Stanine5~21–9 scale ("standard nine")
Sten5.521–10 ("standard ten"); used in 16PF
Deviation IQ10015Wechsler (Stanford-Binet SD 16)
Conversion you can be asked: T = 50 + 10z. So z = +1 → T = 60; z = −2 → T = 30. MMPI clinical-range cut is usually T ≥ 65.
