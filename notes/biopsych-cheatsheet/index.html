Biopsychology, Full Section Cheatsheet

Built 2026-05-05 to cover the full biopsych syllabus and every mock-1 wrong-answer cluster (action potential, refractory periods, motor pathways, hypothalamus, basal ganglia, retina, audition, neurotransmitters, LTP, posterior pituitary, REM, parietal lesions, Huntington, Yerkes-Dodson). 1. The Neuron and Glia

Neuron parts

  • Dendrites — receive input. Surface area is multiplied by spines.
  • Soma (cell body) — integrates input. Contains nucleus, ribosomes, mitochondria.
  • Axon hillock — trigger zone. Decides if threshold is reached.
  • Axon — conducts AP. May be myelinated.
  • Axon terminals — release neurotransmitter at the synapse.

Neuron types by function

  • Sensory (afferent) — periphery → CNS. Pseudounipolar.
  • Motor (efferent) — CNS → muscles/glands. Multipolar.
  • Interneurons — local processing inside CNS. Most common.

2. Action Potential, Quick Recap

Full primer: action potential page.

  • Resting membrane potential: ~ −70 mV. Maintained by Na/K pump (3 Na+ out, 2 K+ in) plus leaky K+ channels.
  • Threshold: ~ −55 mV.
  • Depolarisation: voltage-gated Na+ channels open → Na+ in → spike to ~ +30 mV.
  • Repolarisation: Na+ channels inactivate, voltage-gated K+ channels open → K+ out.
  • Hyperpolarisation: K+ channels close slowly → undershoot below −70 mV.

All-or-none law (mock-1 Q1): once threshold is reached the AP fires at constant amplitude. Stimulus intensity is coded by frequency, not amplitude.

Refractory periods (mock-1 Q2):

  • Absolute — Na+ channels inactivated. No stimulus, however strong, can fire another AP.
  • Relative — membrane is hyperpolarised. A stronger stimulus can fire one.

Saltatory conduction: AP jumps between Nodes of Ranvier. Faster + energy efficient.

3. Synapse + Neurotransmitters

Synaptic transmission

  1. AP arrives at terminal.
  2. Voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open. Ca²⁺ enters.
  3. Vesicles fuse with presynaptic membrane (SNARE proteins).
  4. NT released into cleft, binds postsynaptic receptors.
  5. EPSP (depolarising) or IPSP (hyperpolarising) — summation at axon hillock decides if a new AP fires.
  6. NT cleared by reuptake, enzymatic breakdown, or diffusion.

Subcortical structures

  • Thalamus — sensory relay (all senses except smell). LGN (vision), MGN (hearing), VPL (touch), VPM (face).
  • Hypothalamus — homeostasis, hormones, "4 Fs" (feeding, fighting, fleeing, mating). Lesion of VMH = hyperphagia + obesity (mock-1 Q5). Lesion of LH = aphagia. Suprachiasmatic nucleus = circadian. Supraoptic nucleus → ADH (mock-1 Q11). Paraventricular → oxytocin + CRH.
  • Basal ganglia — caudate, putamen, globus pallidus, subthalamic nucleus, substantia nigra. Red nucleus is NOT basal ganglia (mock-1 Q6) — it's midbrain tegmentum. Function: motor initiation/inhibition. Lesions: Parkinson's (SN), Huntington's (caudate/putamen), hemiballismus (subthalamic).
  • Limbic system — hippocampus (memory), amygdala (fear), cingulate (emotion/attention), fornix, mammillary bodies, septum.
  • Cerebellum — coordination, timing, motor learning. Lesions: ataxia, dysmetria, intention tremor.

Hemisphere specialisation

  • Left: language (Broca, Wernicke), logic, sequential, math.
  • Right: spatial, music, faces, emotion processing, holistic.
  • Corpus callosum — major commissure. Severance = split-brain (Sperry, Gazzaniga).

7. Sensory Systems

Vision

  • Retina: rods (peripheral, low light, no colour) and cones (foveal, colour, acuity). Cones are densest at the fovea centralis (mock-1 Q7). Optic disc = blind spot (no photoreceptors).
  • Pathway: retina → optic nerve → optic chiasm (nasal fibres cross) → optic tract → LGN of thalamus → V1 (occipital).
  • Theories of colour vision: trichromatic (Young-Helmholtz, retinal level) + opponent-process (Hering, post-retinal).
  • Dorsal stream "where/how" (parietal). Ventral stream "what" (temporal, object/face recognition).

Audition

  • Outer → middle (ossicles: malleus, incus, stapes) → inner ear (cochlea).
  • Hair cells on basilar membrane → CN VIII → cochlear nuclei → MGN → primary auditory cortex (Heschl's gyrus, temporal).
  • Place theory (Békésy) — high frequencies stimulate the base of basilar membrane, low frequencies stimulate the apex. Best for high frequencies (mock-1 Q8).
  • Frequency theory — firing rate matches frequency. Works for low frequencies (<500 Hz).
  • Volley principle bridges the two for mid-range.

Somatosensation, taste, smell

  • Somatosensory: dorsal column-medial lemniscus (touch, vibration, proprioception) vs spinothalamic (pain, temp). Both decussate but at different levels (DCML at medulla, spinothalamic at the spinal level of entry).
  • Taste: 5 basic — sweet, salty, sour, bitter, umami. CN VII (anterior 2/3), IX (posterior 1/3), X (epiglottis). Insula = primary gustatory cortex.
  • Smell: olfactory epithelium → bulb → piriform cortex (no thalamus relay). Closest to limbic system, hence emotional memory ties.

8. Motor Systems

Pyramidal tract (corticospinal)

  • Origin: primary motor cortex (M1, Brodmann 4) and premotor.
  • Path: internal capsule → cerebral peduncle → pons → medullary pyramids.
  • Decussates at the pyramids of the medulla (~85–90%) → lateral corticospinal tract (mock-1 Q3). Remaining 10–15% become anterior corticospinal, decussate at level of the spinal segment.

Extrapyramidal pathways

  • Rubrospinal — from red nucleus. Flexor bias.
  • Vestibulospinal — postural extensor.
  • Reticulospinal — locomotion, posture.
  • Tectospinal — head/neck orienting.

9. Sleep, Quick Recap

Full primer: sleep page.

  • NREM 1 — theta, easy to wake, hypnic jerks.
  • NREM 2 — sleep spindles + K-complexes. ~50% of total sleep.
  • NREM 3 — delta (slow-wave). Deep sleep, growth hormone, somnambulism.
  • REM — desynchronised low-voltage EEG (similar to wake), rapid eye movements, atonia, vivid dreams, penile/clitoral tumescence. NOT delta-dominant (mock-1 Q12). Delta = NREM 3.
  • Cycle — ~90 min. REM gets longer through the night, NREM 3 dominates early.

Sleep disorders trap: narcolepsy = orexin/hypocretin deficiency. Sleep paralysis = REM atonia spilling into wake.

10. Hunger, Thirst, Temperature

  • Hunger: lateral hypothalamus (LH) = "go". VMH = satiety = "stop". Leptin (adipose) suppresses appetite via arcuate nucleus. Ghrelin (stomach) increases it. Insulin → ↑satiety. Hypothalamic dual-centre theory of feeding.
  • Thirst: osmotic (hypertonic blood, OVLT detects) vs hypovolemic (low BP, kidneys → renin → angiotensin II). ADH from supraoptic nucleus → posterior pituitary → renal water reabsorption (mock-1 Q11).
  • Temperature: preoptic area of hypothalamus is the thermostat. Cold defence: shivering, vasoconstriction. Heat defence: sweating, vasodilation.

12. Memory, Learning, LTP

  • Hippocampus — declarative (explicit) memory consolidation. Bilateral lesion = anterograde amnesia (HM case).
  • Amygdala — emotional memory, fear conditioning.
  • Striatum — procedural / habit memory.
  • Cerebellum — motor learning, classical conditioning of motor responses.
  • LTP (mock-1 Q10) — long-term potentiation. Strengthens synapse after repeated firing. NMDA receptor is critical (Ca²⁺ entry, AMPA insertion). Hebb: "neurons that fire together wire together".
  • LTD — long-term depression. Weakens synapses. Both LTP and LTD enable plasticity.

13. Emotion + Arousal

  • Limbic loop — Papez circuit: hippocampus → fornix → mammillary → anterior thalamus → cingulate → entorhinal → hippocampus.
  • Amygdala — fear, threat detection. Klüver-Bucy syndrome (bilateral lesion) = hyperorality, hypersexuality, placidity.
  • Theories of emotion:
    • James-Lange — body first, then emotion ("we feel sad because we cry").
    • Cannon-Bard — body and emotion simultaneously (thalamic theory).
    • Schachter-Singer — two-factor: arousal + cognitive label.
    • Lazarus — appraisal precedes emotion.
  • Yerkes-Dodson law (mock-1 Q15) — inverted-U relationship between arousal and performance. Optimum is lower for complex tasks, higher for simple/well-learned tasks.

17. Mock-1 Trap Recap (15 questions)

  1. All-or-none = constant amplitude, frequency codes intensity.
  2. Absolute refractory = Na+ inactivated, no spike possible.
  3. Corticospinal decussation = pyramids of medulla.
  4. CN III = oculomotor → most extraocular muscles + pupil.
  5. VMH lesion → hyperphagia + obesity.
  6. Red nucleus is NOT basal ganglia (it's midbrain tegmentum).
  7. Cones densest at fovea centralis.
  8. Place theory (Békésy) explains high-frequency hearing.
  9. Mesolimbic dopamine → positive symptoms of schizophrenia.
  10. NMDA receptor critical for hippocampal LTP.
  11. ADH (vasopressin) = posterior pituitary → water reabsorption.
  12. REM = mixed/low-voltage EEG (NOT delta).
  13. Right parietal lesion → left hemispatial neglect.
  14. Huntington's = AD, CAG repeat, chromosome 4.
  15. Yerkes-Dodson = inverted U; optimum lower for complex tasks.
