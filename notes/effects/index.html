How to use it. If the stem names "the ___ effect," the answer is usually a person (who coined it). If the stem describes a scene with no name, the answer is usually the effect. This page drills both directions. The trap is always a same-family effect (halo↔horn, Pygmalion↔Golem, primacy↔recency), so learn each in its pair.
Jump to: 1. Memory & learning 2. Attention & perception 3. Social & expectancy 4. Judgment & decision 5. Persuasion & media 6. Biological & clinical

1. Memory & learning effects

EffectWhoWhat it is
Zeigarnik effectBluma ZeigarnikInterrupted / unfinished tasks are remembered better than completed ones.
Von Restorff (isolation) effectHedwig von RestorffThe odd-one-out item in a list stands out and is recalled best.
Serial position effectEbbinghaus (named by Murdock)First + last items in a list recalled best (= primacy + recency together).
Primacy effect—First items recalled well (rehearsed into long-term store).
Recency effect—Last items recalled well (still in short-term store). A delay/distractor kills recency, not primacy.
Spacing (distributed practice) effectEbbinghausSpread-out study beats cramming for long-term retention.
Testing effect—Retrieving (self-testing) cements memory better than re-reading.
Generation effectSlamecka & GrafInfo you generate yourself is remembered better than info you just read.
Self-reference effectRogers, Kuiper & KirkerMaterial related to yourself is encoded and recalled better.
Misinformation effectElizabeth LoftusPost-event misleading info distorts the original memory (eyewitness work).
Picture superiority effectPaivio (dual-coding)Pictures are remembered better than words.
Encoding-specificity / context effectTulving & ThomsonRecall is best when retrieval cues match the encoding context (state/context-dependent memory).
Levels-of-processing effectCraik & LockhartDeeper (semantic) processing → better memory than shallow (visual/acoustic).
Swap-trap: remove the delay/distractor and it's the recency end that vanishes (STM decays), primacy survives. Zeigarnik = unfinished remembered; von Restorff = distinctive remembered. Don't merge them.

2. Attention & perception effects

EffectWhoWhat it is
Stroop effectJ. Ridley StroopSlower to name ink colour when the word spells a different colour (automatic reading interferes).
Cocktail-party effectColin CherrySelective attention to one voice in a crowd, yet you still catch your own name.
McGurk effectMcGurk & MacDonaldWhat you see the mouth do changes what you hear (audio-visual speech fusion).
Ventriloquist effect—Sound is mislocated toward a plausible visual source.
Troxler effect (fading)TroxlerFixate steadily and unchanging peripheral stimuli fade from awareness.
Motion aftereffect (waterfall illusion)—After staring at motion, a static scene appears to drift the opposite way.
Bouba/kiki effectKöhler (later Ramachandran)People map "bouba" to round shapes, "kiki" to spiky ones (sound symbolism).
Tetris effect—Heavy repeated activity intrudes into imagery/dreams.
NIMHANS angle: the phi phenomenon and autokinetic phenomenon are on the Phenomena page (they're called "phenomena," not "effects") — don't go looking for them here.

3. Social & expectancy effects

EffectWhoWhat it is
Halo effectEdward ThorndikeOne good trait (e.g. looks) bleeds into an overall positive judgement.
Horn(s) effect—The negative mirror of halo: one bad trait darkens the whole impression.
Pygmalion (Rosenthal) effectRosenthal & JacobsonHigh teacher/leader expectations → the person actually performs better.
Golem effect—Low expectations → worse performance (the dark twin of Pygmalion).
Hawthorne effectElton Mayo (Western Electric)People change behaviour because they know they're being observed.
John Henry effectSaretskyA control group tries extra hard because it knows it's the comparison (a research artefact).
Mere-exposure effectRobert ZajoncRepeated exposure alone increases liking.
Bystander effectLatané & DarleyMore onlookers → less likely any one helps (diffusion of responsibility). After Kitty Genovese.
Ringelmann effectMax RingelmannPer-person effort drops as group size grows (precursor to social loafing).
Pratfall effectElliot AronsonA competent person becomes more likeable after a small blunder.
Spotlight effectThomas GilovichOverestimating how much others notice you.
False-consensus effectLee RossOverestimating how many people share your views.
Lake Wobegon / above-average effect—Most people rate themselves above average (illusory superiority).
Cheerleader effect—Faces look more attractive in a group than alone.
Overjustification effectLepper, Greene & NisbettPaying for an already-enjoyed activity kills the intrinsic motivation.
Boomerang effectHovland traditionPersuasion backfires, shifting the attitude the opposite way (cousin of reactance).
Same-study, different name: Pygmalion = Rosenthal & Jacobson (high expectation lifts), Golem = low expectation drops, Hawthorne = Mayo (being watched changes behaviour). Examiners swap these three constantly.

4. Judgment & decision effects

EffectWhoWhat it is
Barnum / Forer effectBertram ForerAccepting vague, general descriptions as uniquely true of you (horoscopes, cold reading).
Dunning–Kruger effectDunning & KrugerLow-skill people overestimate their own competence.
Anchoring effectTversky & KahnemanFirst number seen drags later estimates toward it.
Framing effectTversky & KahnemanSame facts, different wording (gain vs loss) flips the choice.
Endowment effectRichard ThalerYou value a thing more just because you own it.
IKEA effectNorton, Mochon & ArielyYou over-value things you partly built yourself.
Decoy (asymmetric dominance) effectHuber et al.A third inferior option nudges choice between the other two.
Bandwagon effect—Adopting a belief because many others hold it.
Actor–observer effectJones & NisbettYour own acts blamed on the situation, others' on their character.
Recency / availability in judgmentTversky & KahnemanEasily recalled examples seem more frequent than they are (availability heuristic).
Pollyanna effectMatlin & StangPositive items are processed and recalled more readily than negative.
NIMHANS angle: anchoring, framing and availability are also written up as cognitive biases — if the stem says "bias," check the Phenomena page; if it says "effect," it's here. Same content, two labels.

5. Persuasion, media & population effects

EffectWhoWhat it is
Sleeper effectCarl Hovland (Yale)A message from a low-credibility source persuades more over time once you forget the source.
Mere-exposure effectZajonc(Also a persuasion lever: familiarity breeds liking.)
Werther effectDavid PhillipsSpike in copycat suicides after a publicised one.
Papageno effectNiederkrotenthalerResponsible media coverage of coping can reduce suicide (the protective opposite of Werther).
Mozart effectRauscher et al.A brief, small spatial-reasoning bump after Mozart (popularly overstated).
Flynn effectJames FlynnPopulation IQ scores have risen generation on generation (forces test re-norming).
Matthew effectRobert Merton"Rich get richer": early advantage compounds (reading, science credit).
Kuleshov effectLev KuleshovViewers read emotion into a neutral face based on the adjacent shot (film editing).

6. Biological & clinical effects

EffectWhoWhat it is
Placebo effect—Improvement from an inert treatment driven by expectation.
Nocebo effect—Negative symptoms from an inert treatment (the dark twin of placebo).
Garcia effect (taste aversion)John GarciaOne-trial conditioned aversion to a taste; biological preparedness; defies classic CC timing rules.
Coolidge effectFrank Beach (named it)Renewed sexual interest when a novel partner is introduced.
Westermarck effectEdvard WestermarckReverse sexual imprinting: people raised together in early childhood feel no attraction (incest avoidance).
Kindling effectGoddard (seizure model)Repeated sub-threshold stimulation lowers the threshold for episodes (used for mood/seizure recurrence).
Tolerance / sensitisation—Same drug dose gives less effect (tolerance) or more (sensitisation) with repetition.
Placebo vs nocebo, Werther vs Papageno, Pygmalion vs Golem, halo vs horn, primacy vs recency, tolerance vs sensitisation — six "good twin / bad twin" pairs. If you only learn one of each, the distractor will be the other.
