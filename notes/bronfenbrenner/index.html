Bronfenbrenner, Ecological Systems Theory

Developmental Psychology · Exam-grade · Built 2026-05-05

1. Core Idea

  • Urie Bronfenbrenner (1917-2005), Russian-born American developmental psychologist.
  • Development is shaped by ongoing interaction between the person and a set of nested environmental systems.
  • Originally Ecological Systems Theory, "The Ecology of Human Development" (1979).
  • Later renamed Bioecological Model (1994, refined 2005) to give biology more weight and add the PPCT mechanism.
  • Co-founded the Head Start program (1965) in the US, applied use of the theory.
One-line answer: Development = person × proximal processes × nested context × time.

2. The 5 Systems (innermost to outermost)

2.1 Microsystem

  • Immediate settings with direct face-to-face contact.
  • Examples: family, school, peer group, neighbourhood, daycare, workplace (for adults).
  • Influence is bidirectional: child shapes the environment, environment shapes the child.
  • Most direct effect on development.

2.2 Mesosystem

  • Interactions between two or more microsystems. Not a setting itself, a system of microsystems.
  • Examples: parent-teacher communication, family-peer overlap, home-daycare links.
  • Strong, supportive mesosystem links = better developmental outcomes.

2.3 Exosystem

  • Settings the child does not directly participate in, but which still affect them via the microsystem.
  • Examples: parent's workplace, school board decisions, mass media, parent's social network, local government policy.
TRAP: Exosystem ≠ no influence. Child isn't in it, but it shapes them through their microsystem (e.g. dad loses job → stress at home → child's development affected).

2.4 Macrosystem

  • The overarching cultural blueprint, the "cultural envelope" around all the other systems.
  • Examples: ideologies, customs, laws, religion, SES, cultural values, economic system, war/peace.
  • Doesn't refer to a specific setting, refers to the cultural patterning that shapes all settings.

2.5 Chronosystem

  • The time dimension. Added later (after the original 4 systems).
  • Three flavours of "time" effects:
    • Life transitions, normative (school entry, puberty) and non-normative (parents' divorce, death of parent).
    • Historical events the person lives through (pandemic, war, recession).
    • Sociohistorical conditions of the cohort (cohort effects).
TRAP: Chronosystem was added later. Older textbooks list only 4 systems. If a question says "Bronfenbrenner originally proposed 5 systems" — false. Originally 4, chronosystem added later.

4. PPCT Model (1994 refinement, often asked)

Bronfenbrenner refined the theory into the Bioecological Model using the PPCT mechanism. This is the engine of the modern version.

4.1 Process — proximal processes

  • The engine of development. Most important driver.
  • Regular, reciprocal interactions over extended time between the person and people/objects/symbols in the immediate environment.
  • Examples: parent-child play, reading together, sustained peer activity, learning a skill from a mentor.
  • Must be regular and over long stretches of time to count as proximal processes (not one-off events).

4.2 Person — individual traits

Three sub-types:

Sub-typeDefinitionExamples
Demand characteristicsVisible traits that pull responses from othersAge, gender, skin colour, physical appearance
Resource characteristicsSkills, abilities, experiencesIntelligence, prior knowledge, social skills
Force characteristicsTemperament, motivation, persistenceCuriosity, drive, distractibility, regulation

4.3 Context — the 5 nested systems

Micro / Meso / Exo / Macro / Chrono. Already covered above.

4.4 Time

  • Micro-time: continuity vs interruption within an activity (e.g. uninterrupted reading session).
  • Meso-time: consistency of activities across days and weeks.
  • Macro-time: chronosystem level, sociohistorical change across the lifespan.

5. High-Yield Exam Pointers

  • Originally 4 systems, chronosystem added later.
  • Bidirectionality is a key examiner trigger word, especially in microsystem questions.
  • Theory has two phases: Ecological Systems Theory (1979) → Bioecological Model (1994) with PPCT.
  • Proximal processes are the engine, not the context. Context modifies how proximal processes operate.
  • Mesosystem is not a setting, it's a system of relationships between settings.

7. Applications

  • Child welfare policy — interventions at multiple system levels (family + school + community).
  • Inclusive education — coordinating microsystems (home, school, peers).
  • Public health — tobacco control, vaccination uptake (macro + exo levels).
  • Family therapy — recognising that family microsystem sits inside larger systems.
  • Head Start program (Bronfenbrenner co-founded) — early childhood intervention targeting micro + meso systems.

8. Critique

  • Hard to operationalise and test empirically — too many simultaneous variables.
  • Originally underplayed biology (fixed in the bioecological revision via "Person" component).
  • Criticised for being descriptive more than predictive, doesn't say which system dominates when.
  • Cultural specificity is unclear — macrosystem treats "culture" as a single bucket.

9. One-line MCQ Traps

  1. Exosystem ≠ no influence. Child isn't in it, but it still affects them via the microsystem (e.g. parent loses job).
  2. Mesosystem is not a setting, it's the link between two settings the child is in.
  3. Chronosystem was added later, originally 4 systems.
  4. Bidirectionality is a microsystem feature (child also shapes parent), not a one-way arrow.
  5. Proximal processes are the engine of development, not the context. Context only shapes their effect.
  6. PPCT belongs to the Bioecological Model (1994), not the original 1979 theory.
  7. Mass media affecting a child = exosystem, not macrosystem (media is a setting, not a cultural blueprint).
  8. "Culture, religion, ideology, SES" = macrosystem.
  9. Parent's workplace = classic exosystem example.
  10. Sibling fight at home = microsystem. Parent-teacher meeting = mesosystem.
