1. Freud & the psychodynamic core

Structural model of the mind

  • Id — present at birth, entirely unconscious, runs on the pleasure principle (immediate gratification). Source of libido. Uses primary process thinking.
  • Ego — the reality principle; mediates between id, superego and the real world. Mostly conscious/preconscious. Uses secondary process thinking. Deploys the defence mechanisms.
  • Superego — the moral arm: conscience (punishes with guilt) + ego-ideal (rewards with pride). Internalised parental/social standards, forms ~age 5 after the Oedipus complex resolves.

Topographic model

Conscious (current awareness) → preconscious (retrievable) → unconscious (repressed, inaccessible, but driving behaviour). The iceberg: most of the mind is below the surface. Drives: Eros (life/libido) vs Thanatos (death/aggression).

Psychosexual stages

Defence mechanisms

Unconscious ego strategies that reduce anxiety by distorting reality. Anna Freud systematised them.

DefenceWhat it is
RepressionThe master defence: push threatening material out of awareness.
DenialRefuse to accept external reality.
ProjectionAttribute your own unacceptable impulse to someone else.
DisplacementRedirect the impulse onto a safer target (yell at the dog, not the boss).
Reaction formationBehave as the opposite of the true impulse (excessive niceness masking hatred).
RationalisationInvent a logical-sounding excuse for the real (unacceptable) reason.
SublimationChannel the impulse into a socially valued activity (aggression → surgery/sport). The healthiest, most mature defence.
RegressionRetreat to an earlier developmental stage (an adult sulking).
IntellectualisationStrip the emotion out, deal only with the abstract facts.
IdentificationTake on the qualities of an admired/feared other.
UndoingRitual act to "cancel out" an unacceptable thought/deed (OCD-linked).
Isolation (of affect)Keep the memory but detach the feeling attached to it.
SplittingSee self/others as all-good or all-bad, can't integrate (borderline-linked).
TRAP: Displacement = redirect to a substitute target. Sublimation = redirect into a socially acceptable outlet. Projection = put your impulse onto someone else. They love to swap these in the options.
NIMHANS extra — Vaillant's hierarchy of defences. George Vaillant ranked defences by maturity, and NIMHANS asks which tier a defence falls in:
  • Psychotic (most primitive): denial of external reality, delusional projection, distortion.
  • Immature: projection, acting out, passive aggression, fantasy, splitting, somatisation.
  • Neurotic: repression, displacement, reaction formation, intellectualisation, rationalisation, undoing.
  • Mature: sublimation, humour, altruism, suppression (the only conscious one), anticipation.

2. Neo-Freudians & ego psychology

They kept the unconscious but rejected Freud's heavy emphasis on sexuality, stressing social/cultural drivers instead.

TheoristSignature concept(s)
Carl Jung (analytical psychology)Collective unconscious + archetypes (persona, shadow, anima/animus, self); personal unconscious; introversion vs extraversion; individuation. Word-association test.
Alfred Adler (individual psychology)Inferiority complex & compensation; striving for superiority; style of life; birth order; social interest (gemeinschaftsgefühl); fictional finalism.
Karen HorneyBasic anxiety (child feeling isolated/helpless); three neurotic trends: moving toward / against / away from people; "womb envy" rebuttal to Freud's penis envy; the tyranny of the should / idealised self; feminine psychology.
Erik Erikson (ego psychology)8 psychosocial stages across the whole lifespan; identity vs role confusion; the term "identity crisis." (Stages live under Development.)
Anna FreudCatalogued the defence mechanisms; founder of child psychoanalysis (vs Klein).
Erich FrommHumanistic psychoanalysis; "escape from freedom"; character orientations (receptive, exploitative, hoarding, marketing, productive).
Harry Stack SullivanInterpersonal theory; the "self-system" built from anxiety-reducing relationships; good-me / bad-me / not-me.
Heinz HartmannEgo psychology; the "conflict-free ego sphere"; adaptation. (Father of ego psychology.)
TRAP: Collective unconscious = Jung. Inferiority complex & birth order = Adler. Basic anxiety / womb envy = Horney. Escape from freedom = Fromm. These get rotated as distractors constantly.
Freud vs Adler vs Jung, one line each: Freud — sex/libido. Adler — power/inferiority. Jung — meaning/the collective unconscious.

3. Object relations & self psychology

NIMHANS-heavy. MET barely touched this; NIMHANS (clinical) asks these by name constantly. "Object" = an internalised mental representation of a person (usually the mother), not a thing.
TheoristSignature concept(s)
Melanie KleinFounder of object relations + child play therapy. Paranoid-schizoid position (splitting, part-objects, ~first 6 months) → depressive position (integration, whole objects, guilt/reparation). Projective identification.
Donald Winnicott"Good-enough mother"; transitional object (blanket/teddy) & transitional space; the true self vs false self; holding environment.
W.R.D. FairbairnLibido is object-seeking, not pleasure-seeking (the key break from Freud); endopsychic structure.
Margaret MahlerSeparation-individuation stages: normal autism → symbiosis → differentiation → practising → rapprochement → object constancy.
Otto KernbergBorderline personality organisation; splitting; integrated object relations theory with drive theory; Transference-Focused Therapy (TFP).
Heinz KohutSelf psychology; narcissism; the selfobject; empathic mirroring; idealising/grandiose self; vertical vs horizontal split.
John BowlbyAttachment theory; the internal working model; maternal deprivation; secure base. (Ainsworth's Strange Situation operationalised it.)
TRAP: Good-enough mother / transitional object = Winnicott. Separation-individuation = Mahler. Self / selfobject / narcissism = Kohut. Borderline organisation = Kernberg. Paranoid-schizoid & depressive positions = Klein (note: positions, not stages).

4. Trait theories

Personality = a set of stable, measurable dispositions. Built bottom-up, often via factor analysis.

TheoristModel
Gordon AllportCardinal traits (dominate the whole personality, rare), central traits (5–10 core descriptors), secondary traits (situational preferences). Also: functional autonomy (motives become independent of their origin) and the proprium (the self). Nomothetic vs idiographic. Did NOT use factor analysis.
Raymond CattellFactor-analysed traits into 16 source traits → the 16PF questionnaire. Surface traits vs source traits. Fluid vs crystallised intelligence (also his).
Hans EysenckBiologically-based PEN model: Psychoticism, Extraversion, Neuroticism. Originally just E and N (the EPI), added P later (EPQ). Extraversion = low cortical arousal (ARAS); neuroticism = limbic/autonomic reactivity.
Jeffrey GrayReinforcement Sensitivity Theory (RST): BIS (behavioural inhibition, sensitivity to punishment → anxiety) vs BAS (behavioural activation, sensitivity to reward → impulsivity); + fight-flight-freeze system. A biological reframe of Eysenck.
Costa & McCraeBig Five / Five-Factor Model (see next section). Measured by the NEO-PI-R.
TRAP: Allport = 3 trait levels (cardinal/central/secondary). Cattell = 16 factors. Eysenck = 3 dimensions (PEN). The numbers 3 / 16 / 3 are the giveaway, but note Allport's "3" is levels while Eysenck's "3" is dimensions.
NIMHANS extra: BIS/BAS = Gray. If a stem mentions sensitivity to reward vs punishment, or a biological basis for anxiety vs impulsivity, it is Gray's RST, not Eysenck's arousal model.

5. The Big Five (OCEAN) & HEXACO

The dominant modern trait model. Costa & McCrae; emerged partly from the lexical hypothesis (Galton → Allport & Odbert → Goldberg coined "Big Five"). Each factor is a continuum, not a type.

FactorHigh scorerLow scorer
OpennessCurious, imaginative, novelty-seekingConventional, practical, routine-preferring
ConscientiousnessOrganised, disciplined, dependableCareless, spontaneous, disorganised
ExtraversionSociable, assertive, energeticReserved, quiet, solitary
AgreeablenessTrusting, cooperative, warmCompetitive, sceptical, antagonistic
NeuroticismAnxious, moody, emotionally reactiveCalm, secure, stable (= emotional stability)
Mnemonic: OCEAN (or CANOE). Neuroticism's healthy pole is sometimes labelled emotional stability — same axis, flipped.
NIMHANS extra — HEXACO. A 6-factor extension (Ashton & Lee) adding H = Honesty-Humility (sincerity, fairness, modesty, greed-avoidance) to a reworked Big Five. The "dark triad" (narcissism, Machiavellianism, psychopathy) maps onto low Honesty-Humility. Know that HEXACO = 6 factors, with H the new one.
TRAP: The Big Five is descriptive and atheoretical (it says what traits exist, not why). Don't confuse it with Eysenck's biologically-causal PEN, even though Extraversion and Neuroticism appear in both.

6. Humanistic theories ("third force")

Reaction against the determinism of psychoanalysis and behaviourism. Emphasis on free will, the self, and growth.

Carl Rogers — person-centred

  • Self-concept: the real self vs the ideal self. Congruence = the two align (well-adjusted); incongruence drives distress.
  • Unconditional positive regard vs conditions of worth imposed by others.
  • The organismic valuing process; the goal is the fully functioning person (open to experience, trusts self, lives existentially).
  • Therapy: client-centred, the three core conditions — empathy, congruence/genuineness, UPR.

Abraham Maslow — self-actualisation

  • Hierarchy of needs: physiological → safety → love/belonging → esteem → self-actualisation (added self-transcendence at the top late in life).
  • Lower (deficiency / D-needs) must be met before higher (being / B-needs).
  • Studied healthy exemplars; peak experiences.
TRAP: Rogers = self-concept, congruence, UPR. Maslow = the hierarchy / self-actualisation. Both are humanistic; the paper pairs them to make you slip.

7. Existential theories

NIMHANS extra. Existential thinkers overlap with humanism but focus on meaning, death, freedom and anxiety rather than growth. Frequently asked by name at NIMHANS.
TheoristSignature concept(s)
Viktor FranklLogotherapy — the "will to meaning" as the primary drive; existential vacuum; Man's Search for Meaning; techniques: paradoxical intention, dereflection.
Rollo MayBrought existentialism to US psychology; the daimonic; existential anxiety; The Meaning of Anxiety; freedom + destiny.
Irvin YalomFour "ultimate concerns": death, freedom, isolation, meaninglessness. Existential group/individual therapy.
Ludwig BinswangerDaseinsanalysis (being-in-the-world); applied Heidegger to psychiatry.
TRAP: Will to meaning / logotherapy = Frankl (vs Adler's "will to power" and Freud's "will to pleasure"). The three "wills" are a classic single-best-answer item.

8. Social-cognitive & learning approaches

TheoristConcept
Albert BanduraReciprocal determinism (behaviour ↔ personal/cognitive factors ↔ environment, all interacting); self-efficacy; observational learning (Bobo doll).
Julian RotterLocus of control (internal vs external); expectancy × reinforcement value; social learning theory.
Walter MischelThe person–situation debate: behaviour is less consistent across situations than trait theory assumes (the marshmallow / delay-of-gratification studies; CAPS model — cognitive-affective processing system).
George KellyPersonal construct theory; the person as a "scientist" testing constructs; the Rep Grid / Role Construct Repertory Test.
Henry MurrayPersonology; system of needs & press (need for achievement, affiliation, power, etc.); coined "personology"; built the TAT (with Morgan).
Dollard & MillerTranslated psychoanalysis into learning theory; drive–cue–response–reinforcement; approach-avoidance conflict; frustration-aggression hypothesis.
B. F. SkinnerRadical behaviourist position: "personality" is just learned operant response patterns; no inner self needed.
TRAP: Self-efficacy = Bandura. Locus of control = Rotter. Both sound like "belief about control," but efficacy = "can I do this task?", locus = "do I or outside forces control outcomes?"
NIMHANS extra: Needs & press + TAT = Murray. Personal constructs + Rep Grid = Kelly. Approach-avoidance conflict = Dollard & Miller. These three lesser-known names show up in NIMHANS distractors.

9. Type theories (the older / body-based ones)

TheoristTypology
Hippocrates / GalenFour humours → temperaments: sanguine (blood), choleric (yellow bile), melancholic (black bile), phlegmatic (phlegm).
William SheldonSomatotypes: endomorph (round → viscerotonic, sociable/relaxed), mesomorph (muscular → somatotonic, assertive/energetic), ectomorph (thin → cerebrotonic, restrained/introverted).
Ernst KretschmerBody build → temperament: pyknic (round → manic-depressive/cyclothymic), asthenic/leptosomic (thin → schizophrenic/schizothymic), athletic, dysplastic.
Friedman & RosenmanType A (competitive, hostile, time-urgent → heart disease) vs Type B (relaxed). Later Type C (cancer-prone, suppressed emotion) and Type D (distressed, negative affect + social inhibition).
Carl Jung → Myers & BriggsIntroversion/extraversion + 4 functions (thinking/feeling, sensing/intuiting) → the basis the MBTI was later built on.
TRAP: Type vs trait. Types = discrete categories (you ARE an endomorph). Traits = continuous dimensions (you score 7/10 on extraversion). Examiners like asking which is which. Sheldon = somatotypes; Kretschmer = body build → psychosis link.

10. Indian psychology — Triguna & allied concepts

NIMHANS-specific. NIMHANS (being India-rooted) periodically asks indigenous/Indian personality concepts that MET never touched. Know at least the Triguna model.
ConceptWhat it is
Triguna (Samkhya / Bhagavad Gita)Three fundamental qualities of nature (prakriti) that constitute personality: Sattva (purity, clarity, balance, wisdom), Rajas (activity, passion, restlessness, ambition), Tamas (inertia, dullness, ignorance, lethargy). Everyone is a mix; the dominant guna defines temperament. Measured by the Vedic Personality Inventory (VPI) and GITA inventories.
Ayurvedic TridoshaBody-mind constitution: Vata (air), Pitta (fire), Kapha (earth/water) — a body-typology parallel to Sheldon.
PanchakoshaFive "sheaths" of the self from the Taittiriya Upanishad (annamaya → pranamaya → manomaya → vijnanamaya → anandamaya): a layered model of the person.
Lock this one line: Sattva = goodness/balance, Rajas = activity/passion, Tamas = inertia/darkness. If a NIMHANS stem names three "gunas," that is the Triguna model.

11. Personality assessment

TypeTestsLogic
ProjectiveRorschach (inkblots, Exner scoring), TAT (Murray — ambiguous picture stories), CAT (children), Sentence Completion (RISB), Draw-A-Person, Rosenzweig P-F, Word Association (Jung)Ambiguous stimulus → person projects unconscious material. Rich but low reliability/validity.
Objective / self-reportMMPI-2 (clinical, validity scales L, F, K), 16PF (Cattell), NEO-PI-R (Big Five), EPQ (Eysenck), CPI, MCMI (Millon), Big Five InventoryStandardised items, norm-referenced, scored objectively. Higher reliability; vulnerable to faking/social desirability.
Pairings to lock: 16PF → Cattell · NEO-PI-R → Costa & McCrae · EPQ → Eysenck · TAT → Murray · Rorschach → Hermann Rorschach (Exner scoring) · MMPI → Hathaway & McKinley · MCMI → Millon · Rep Grid → Kelly · VPI → Triguna.

13. The MCQ traps, collected

  • Collective unconscious = Jung, NOT Freud (Freud = personal unconscious).
  • Inferiority complex / birth order = Adler. Basic anxiety / womb envy = Horney. Escape from freedom = Fromm.
  • 16 factors = Cattell · 3 dimensions (PEN) = Eysenck · cardinal/central/secondary = Allport · BIS/BAS = Gray · 6 factors (incl. Honesty-Humility) = HEXACO.
  • Self-efficacy = Bandura · locus of control = Rotter · personal constructs = Kelly · needs & press / TAT = Murray.
  • Will to meaning = Frankl · will to power = Adler · will to pleasure = Freud.
  • Good-enough mother / transitional object = Winnicott · separation-individuation = Mahler · self / narcissism = Kohut · borderline organisation = Kernberg · positions (paranoid-schizoid / depressive) = Klein.
  • Sublimation is the most mature defence; repression is the most basic neurotic one; suppression is the only conscious one (Vaillant).
  • Superego forms in the phallic stage (after the Oedipus complex), not at birth.
  • Real vs ideal self & congruence = Rogers; hierarchy of needs = Maslow.
  • Type = category; trait = continuum. Sheldon = somatotypes; Kretschmer = body build → psychosis.
  • Big Five describes (atheoretical); Eysenck's PEN explains via biology — both share E and N.
  • Sattva / Rajas / Tamas = the Triguna model (Indian psychology), measured by the VPI.
  • Projective tests (Rorschach, TAT) tap the unconscious but are low in reliability; objective inventories (MMPI, 16PF, NEO) are reliable but fakeable.
