1. Freud & the psychodynamic core

Structural model of the mind

  • Id — present at birth, entirely unconscious, runs on the pleasure principle (immediate gratification). Source of libido.
  • Ego — the reality principle; mediates between id, superego and the real world. Mostly conscious/preconscious. Deploys the defence mechanisms.
  • Superego — the moral arm: conscience (punishes with guilt) + ego-ideal (rewards with pride). Internalised parental/social standards, forms ~age 5 after the Oedipus complex resolves.

Topographic model

Conscious (current awareness) → preconscious (retrievable) → unconscious (repressed, inaccessible, but driving behaviour). The iceberg: most of the mind is below the surface.

Defence mechanisms

Unconscious ego strategies that reduce anxiety by distorting reality. Anna Freud systematised them.

DefenceWhat it is
RepressionThe master defence: push threatening material out of awareness.
DenialRefuse to accept external reality.
ProjectionAttribute your own unacceptable impulse to someone else.
DisplacementRedirect the impulse onto a safer target (yell at the dog, not the boss).
Reaction formationBehave as the opposite of the true impulse (excessive niceness masking hatred).
RationalisationInvent a logical-sounding excuse for the real (unacceptable) reason.
SublimationChannel the impulse into a socially valued activity (aggression → surgery/sport). The healthiest, most mature defence.
RegressionRetreat to an earlier developmental stage (an adult sulking).
IntellectualisationStrip the emotion out, deal only with the abstract facts.
IdentificationTake on the qualities of an admired/feared other.
TRAP: Displacement = redirect to a substitute target. Sublimation = redirect into a socially acceptable outlet. Projection = put your impulse onto someone else. They love to swap these in the options.

2. Neo-Freudians & ego psychology

They kept the unconscious but rejected Freud's heavy emphasis on sexuality, stressing social/cultural drivers instead.

TheoristSignature concept(s)
Carl Jung (analytical psychology)Collective unconscious + archetypes (persona, shadow, anima/animus, self); personal unconscious; introversion vs extraversion; individuation. Word-association test.
Alfred Adler (individual psychology)Inferiority complex & compensation; striving for superiority; style of life; birth order; social interest (gemeinschaftsgefühl).
Karen HorneyBasic anxiety (child feeling isolated/helpless); three neurotic trends: moving toward / against / away from people; "womb envy" rebuttal to Freud's penis envy; feminine psychology.
Erik Erikson (ego psychology)8 psychosocial stages across the whole lifespan; identity vs role confusion; the term "identity crisis." (Stages live under Development.)
Anna FreudCatalogued the defence mechanisms; founder of child psychoanalysis.
Melanie KleinObject relations; play therapy; paranoid-schizoid & depressive positions.
Erich FrommHumanistic psychoanalysis; "escape from freedom"; character orientations.
Harry Stack SullivanInterpersonal theory; the "self-system" built from anxiety-reducing relationships.
TRAP: Collective unconscious = Jung. Inferiority complex & birth order = Adler. Basic anxiety / womb envy = Horney. These three get rotated as distractors constantly.
Adler vs Jung vs Freud, one line each: Freud — sex/libido. Adler — power/inferiority. Jung — meaning/the collective unconscious.

3. Trait theories

Personality = a set of stable, measurable dispositions. Built bottom-up, often via factor analysis.

TheoristModel
Gordon AllportCardinal traits (dominate the whole personality, rare), central traits (5–10 core descriptors), secondary traits (situational preferences). Distinguished nomothetic vs idiographic study. Did NOT use factor analysis.
Raymond CattellFactor-analysed traits into 16 source traits → the 16PF questionnaire. Surface traits vs source traits. Fluid vs crystallised intelligence (also his).
Hans EysenckBiologically-based PEN model: Psychoticism, Extraversion, Neuroticism. Originally just E and N (the EPI), added P later (EPQ). Linked extraversion to cortical arousal.
Costa & McCraeBig Five / Five-Factor Model (see next section). Measured by the NEO-PI-R.
TRAP: Allport = 3 trait levels (cardinal/central/secondary). Cattell = 16 factors. Eysenck = 3 dimensions (PEN). The numbers 3 / 16 / 3 are the giveaway, but note Allport's "3" is levels while Eysenck's "3" is dimensions.

4. The Big Five (OCEAN / Five-Factor Model)

The dominant modern trait model. Costa & McCrae; emerged partly from the lexical hypothesis (Goldberg). Each factor is a continuum, not a type.

FactorHigh scorerLow scorer
OpennessCurious, imaginative, novelty-seekingConventional, practical, routine-preferring
ConscientiousnessOrganised, disciplined, dependableCareless, spontaneous, disorganised
ExtraversionSociable, assertive, energeticReserved, quiet, solitary
AgreeablenessTrusting, cooperative, warmCompetitive, sceptical, antagonistic
NeuroticismAnxious, moody, emotionally reactiveCalm, secure, stable (= emotional stability)
Mnemonic: OCEAN (or CANOE). Neuroticism's healthy pole is sometimes labelled emotional stability — same axis, flipped.
TRAP: The Big Five is descriptive and atheoretical (it says what traits exist, not why). Don't confuse it with Eysenck's biologically-causal PEN, even though Extraversion and Neuroticism appear in both.

5. Humanistic theories ("third force")

Reaction against the determinism of psychoanalysis and behaviourism. Emphasis on free will, the self, and growth.

Carl Rogers — person-centred

  • Self-concept: the real self vs the ideal self. Congruence = the two align (well-adjusted); incongruence drives distress.
  • Unconditional positive regard vs conditions of worth imposed by others.
  • The goal: the fully functioning person (open to experience, trusts self, lives existentially).
  • Therapy: client-centred, the three core conditions — empathy, congruence/genuineness, UPR.

Abraham Maslow — self-actualisation

  • Hierarchy of needs: physiological → safety → love/belonging → esteem → self-actualisation (added self-transcendence at the top late in life).
  • Lower (deficiency / D-needs) must be met before higher (being / B-needs).
  • Studied healthy exemplars; peak experiences.
TRAP: Rogers = self-concept, congruence, UPR. Maslow = the hierarchy / self-actualisation. Both are humanistic; the paper pairs them to make you slip.

6. Social-cognitive & learning approaches

TheoristConcept
Albert BanduraReciprocal determinism (behaviour ↔ personal/cognitive factors ↔ environment, all interacting); self-efficacy; observational learning (Bobo doll).
Julian RotterLocus of control (internal vs external); expectancy × reinforcement value.
Walter MischelThe person–situation debate: behaviour is less consistent across situations than trait theory assumes (the marshmallow / delay-of-gratification studies; CAPS model).
George KellyPersonal construct theory; the person as a "scientist" testing constructs; the Rep Grid / Role Construct Repertory Test.
B. F. SkinnerRadical behaviourist position: "personality" is just learned operant response patterns; no inner self needed.
TRAP: Self-efficacy = Bandura. Locus of control = Rotter. Both sound like "belief about control," but efficacy = "can I do this task?", locus = "do I or outside forces control outcomes?"

7. Type theories (the older / body-based ones)

TheoristTypology
Hippocrates / GalenFour humours → temperaments: sanguine (blood), choleric (yellow bile), melancholic (black bile), phlegmatic (phlegm).
William SheldonSomatotypes: endomorph (round, sociable/relaxed), mesomorph (muscular, assertive/energetic), ectomorph (thin, restrained/introverted).
Ernst KretschmerBody build → temperament: pyknic (round → manic-depressive), asthenic/leptosomic (thin → schizophrenic), athletic, dysplastic.
Friedman & RosenmanType A (competitive, hostile, time-urgent → heart disease) vs Type B (relaxed). Later Type C (cancer-prone, suppressed emotion) and Type D (distressed, negative affect + social inhibition).
Carl JungIntroversion/extraversion + 4 functions → the basis the MBTI was later built on (Myers & Briggs).
TRAP: Type vs trait. Types = discrete categories (you ARE an endomorph). Traits = continuous dimensions (you score 7/10 on extraversion). MET likes asking which is which.

8. Personality assessment

TypeTestsLogic
ProjectiveRorschach (inkblots), TAT (Murray — ambiguous picture stories), Sentence Completion, Draw-A-Person, Rosenzweig P-FAmbiguous stimulus → person projects unconscious material. Rich but low reliability/validity.
Objective / self-reportMMPI-2 (clinical, with validity scales: L, F, K), 16PF (Cattell), NEO-PI-R (Big Five), EPQ (Eysenck), CPIStandardised items, norm-referenced, scored objectively. Higher reliability; vulnerable to faking/social desirability.
Pairings to lock: 16PF → Cattell · NEO-PI-R → Costa & McCrae · EPQ → Eysenck · TAT → Murray · MMPI → Hathaway & McKinley.

10. The MCQ traps, collected

  • Collective unconscious is Jung, NOT Freud (Freud = personal unconscious).
  • Inferiority complex / birth order = Adler. Basic anxiety / womb envy = Horney.
  • 16 factors = Cattell · 3 dimensions (PEN) = Eysenck · cardinal/central/secondary = Allport.
  • Self-efficacy = Bandura · locus of control = Rotter.
  • Sublimation is the most mature defence; repression is the most basic one.
  • Superego forms in the phallic stage (after the Oedipus complex), not at birth.
  • Real vs ideal self and congruence = Rogers; hierarchy of needs = Maslow.
  • Type = category; trait = continuum.
  • Big Five describes (atheoretical); Eysenck's PEN explains via biology — both share E and N.
  • Projective tests (Rorschach, TAT) tap the unconscious but are low in reliability; objective inventories (MMPI, 16PF, NEO) are reliable but fakeable.
