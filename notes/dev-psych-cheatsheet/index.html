TRAP: Critical = rigid biological window (lost if missed). Sensitive = optimal but flexible. Lorenz's imprinting = critical. Language learning = sensitive (you can still learn L2 at 30, just harder).

2. Prenatal Development & Teratogens

Three prenatal stages

StageDurationKey event
Germinal0-2 weeksZygote → blastocyst → implantation
Embryonic2-8 weeksOrganogenesis, most vulnerable to teratogens
Fetal8 weeks-birthGrowth + refinement, viability ~24 weeks

Teratogens (high-yield)

  • Alcohol — Fetal Alcohol Spectrum Disorder (FASD); FAS = facial anomalies, growth restriction, CNS damage. Most common preventable cause of intellectual disability.
  • Tobacco — low birth weight, prematurity.
  • Thalidomide — limb malformations (1950s-60s, classic teratogen exam case).
  • Rubella — heart, eye, ear defects in 1st trimester.
  • Lead, mercury — CNS damage.
  • Zika — microcephaly.
TRAP: Embryonic period (weeks 2-8) is most vulnerable, not the fetal period. Organs are forming. By the fetal period, organs already exist, growth is the main task.

3. Piaget, Cognitive Development

4 stages

StageAgeAchievementsLimits
Sensorimotor0-2Object permanence (~8mo), goal-directed action, deferred imitation (end)No symbolic thought early on
Preoperational2-7Symbolic thought, language, pretend playEgocentrism, animism, centration, no conservation
Concrete Operational7-11Conservation, reversibility, classification, seriation, decentrationTied to concrete reality, abstract is shaky
Formal Operational11+Abstract, hypothetico-deductive, propositional logicAdolescent egocentrism (imaginary audience, personal fable)

Sensorimotor 6 substages (mini-list)

  1. Reflexive schemes (0-1mo)
  2. Primary circular reactions (1-4mo, on own body)
  3. Secondary circular reactions (4-8mo, on objects)
  4. Coordination of secondary schemes (8-12mo, intentional, object permanence, A-not-B error)
  5. Tertiary circular reactions (12-18mo, experimentation)
  6. Mental representation (18-24mo, deferred imitation, symbolic thought)

Key concepts

  • Schema — mental framework for understanding the world.
  • Assimilation — fitting new info into existing schema (a horse = a "doggie").
  • Accommodation — modifying schema for new info ("ok, that's a horse, not a doggie").
  • Equilibration — drive to balance assimilation + accommodation, engine of cognitive change.
  • Egocentrism — preoperational, can't take another's perspective. Three Mountains task.
  • Animism — preoperational, attributing life to objects.
  • Centration — focus on one feature at a time, blocks conservation.
  • Conservation — quantity stays same despite change in form (number, mass, volume). Order: number → mass → liquid → length → area → weight → volume (last, ~11).
TRAP: Object permanence (sensorimotor, ~8mo) ≠ Conservation (concrete operational, ~7yr). Different stages, different concepts.
TRAP: "Egocentrism" in dev psych ≠ narcissism. It's a cognitive limit, not a personality trait.

Critiques of Piaget

  • Underestimated infants (modern habituation studies show object permanence earlier).
  • Stages less discrete than he claimed (horizontal décalage = uneven within a stage).
  • Underplayed culture (Vygotsky's main attack).
  • Not everyone reaches formal operational, especially for unfamiliar content.

4. Vygotsky, Sociocultural

  • ZPD (Zone of Proximal Development) — gap between what child can do alone vs with help from a more knowledgeable other (MKO).
  • Scaffolding — temporary tailored support, withdrawn as competence grows. Bruner's term, applied to Vygotsky's framework.
  • Private speech — kids talk to themselves while problem-solving. Becomes inner speech, guides cognition. Piaget called this "egocentric speech" and dismissed it; Vygotsky said it's central.
  • Tools of thought — language is the master tool, internalized from social interaction. Higher mental functions are socially mediated.
  • Cultural specificity — cognitive development depends on the cultural tools available (counting systems, writing, schooling).
TRAP: "Scaffolding" = Bruner, not Vygotsky. Vygotsky's own terms are ZPD and MKO.
Piaget vs Vygotsky in one line: Piaget = child as lone scientist (development → learning). Vygotsky = child as apprentice (learning → development, mediated by culture).

5. Bronfenbrenner, Ecological Systems / Bioecological

Full standalone primer with PPCT details: notes/bronfenbrenner. Cheatsheet version below is exam-grade.

Core idea

  • Urie Bronfenbrenner (1917-2005). Development = ongoing interaction between the person and a set of nested environmental systems.
  • Ecological Systems Theory (1979) → renamed Bioecological Model (1994) with the PPCT mechanism.
  • Co-founded Head Start (1965).

The 5 systems (innermost → outermost)

SystemWhat it isExamples
MicrosystemDirect, face-to-face settings; bidirectionalFamily, school, peers, daycare
MesosystemInteractions between microsystems (a system of microsystems, not a setting itself)Parent-teacher meeting, home-school link, family-peer overlap
ExosystemSettings the child does not directly participate in but which still affect themParent's workplace, school board, mass media, parent's social network
MacrosystemCultural blueprint, the "envelope" around all other systemsIdeologies, customs, laws, religion, SES, cultural values
ChronosystemTime dimension. Added later (originally 4 systems)Divorce, parental death, pandemic, sociohistorical era

PPCT (1994 refinement, often asked)

  • Process — proximal processes, the engine of development. Regular, reciprocal interactions over extended time (parent-child play, reading together).
  • Person — individual traits in 3 sub-types:
    • Demand (visible: age, gender, appearance)
    • Resource (skills, intelligence, prior knowledge)
    • Force (temperament, motivation, persistence)
  • Context — the 5 nested systems above.
  • Time — micro-time (within an activity), meso-time (across days/weeks), macro-time (chronosystem level).

High-yield pointers

  • Bidirectionality is a microsystem feature, examiner trigger word.
  • Originally 4 systems, chronosystem added later.
  • Two phases of theory: Ecological Systems (1979) → Bioecological with PPCT (1994).
  • Mesosystem is not a setting, it's the link between two settings.
  • Proximal processes are the engine, context only modifies their effect.
TRAP 1: Exosystem ≠ no influence. Child isn't in it, but it shapes them via the microsystem (e.g. dad loses job → stress at home → child affected).
TRAP 2: Mass media affecting a child = exosystem, not macrosystem. Media is a setting, not a cultural blueprint.
TRAP 3: "Culture, religion, ideology, SES" = macrosystem. "Parent's workplace" = exosystem. "Sibling fight" = microsystem. "Parent-teacher meeting" = mesosystem.
If the stem mentions: nested environments, cultural context, parent's workplace, indirect influence → answer is Bronfenbrenner.

Adult attachment (Main, AAI)

  • Secure-Autonomous (corresponds to B)
  • Dismissing (corresponds to A)
  • Preoccupied (corresponds to C)
  • Unresolved / disorganized (corresponds to D)

Foundational attachment experiments

  • Harlow — wire vs cloth mother monkeys. Contact comfort > food. Demolished pure drive-reduction view of attachment.
  • Lorenz — imprinting in geese, classic critical period example.
  • Bowlby's WHO Report (1951) — "maternal deprivation hypothesis," institutional rearing harms development. Influenced post-WWII childcare policy.

11. Baumrind, Parenting Styles

High demandLow demand
High warmthAuthoritative (best outcomes)Permissive / Indulgent
Low warmthAuthoritarianNeglectful / Uninvolved
  • Authoritative — high warmth + high demand, clear limits, explanations. Best child outcomes (competence, self-regulation, school).
  • Authoritarian — high demand, low warmth, "because I said so." Linked to lower self-esteem, more anxiety.
  • Permissive — high warmth, few demands. Linked to impulsivity, lower achievement.
  • Neglectful — low on both (added by Maccoby & Martin, 4th style). Worst outcomes.
TRAP: Authoritative ≠ Authoritarian. Authoritative = warm + firm (good). Authoritarian = cold + harsh (bad). Words look similar, opposite outcomes.

12. Temperament, Thomas & Chess

  • Easy (~40%) — regular routines, positive mood, adapts to change.
  • Difficult (~10%) — irregular, intense reactions, slow to adapt.
  • Slow-to-warm-up (~15%) — low activity, mild reactions, gradual to adapt.
  • Remaining ~35% don't fit cleanly.
Goodness of fit (Thomas & Chess) — match between temperament and environmental demands predicts adjustment, not temperament alone.

15. Adolescence (compact)

  • Puberty — primary (gonads) + secondary (hair, voice) sex characteristics. Girls typically 1-2 yr earlier than boys.
  • Adolescent egocentrism (Elkind) — imaginary audience (everyone is watching) + personal fable (I am unique, immune to harm).
  • Identity — central task (Erikson stage 5); Marcia's 4 statuses.
  • Risk-taking — limbic system matures before prefrontal cortex (dual systems model).
  • Storm and stress (G. Stanley Hall) — older view, modern data shows most adolescents are not in chronic conflict.

16. Aging & Late Adulthood

  • Primary aging — biological (sensory decline, slower processing).
  • Secondary aging — disease, lifestyle (preventable).
  • Crystallized intelligence — preserved or improves with age (vocab, knowledge).
  • Fluid intelligence — declines (processing speed, novel problem-solving). Cattell-Horn-Carroll model.
  • Socioemotional selectivity theory (Carstensen) — older adults prioritize emotionally meaningful goals as time horizon shrinks.
  • Kübler-Ross 5 stages of dying — denial, anger, bargaining, depression, acceptance. Often misunderstood as universal/linear, she didn't claim that.

17. Top 15 MCQ Traps (Dev Psych)

  1. Object permanence (sensorimotor) ≠ Conservation (concrete operational).
  2. Critical period (rigid, biological) vs Sensitive period (flexible).
  3. Authoritative ≠ Authoritarian. One word, opposite outcomes.
  4. Disorganized attachment was Main & Solomon, not Ainsworth.
  5. "Scaffolding" is Bruner's term, applied to Vygotsky's ZPD.
  6. Bronfenbrenner exosystem = child not in it, but still affected (parent's workplace).
  7. Bronfenbrenner mesosystem = link between microsystems, not a setting itself.
  8. Chronosystem was added later, originally 4 systems.
  9. PPCT = Bioecological Model (1994), not the original 1979 theory.
  10. Gilligan added "ethics of care," didn't replace Kohlberg.
  11. Overregularization ("goed") is rule-learning, not regression.
  12. Embryonic period (weeks 2-8) is most teratogen-vulnerable, not fetal.
  13. FAS / FASD = most common preventable cause of intellectual disability.
  14. Egocentrism (Piaget) = cognitive limit, not a personality trait.
  15. Goodness of fit (Thomas & Chess) — outcome depends on temperament × environment match.

18. 30-Second Recall Script
