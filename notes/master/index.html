Percentiles from the normal curve: mean = 50th. ±1 SD covers 68% (so +1 SD ≈ 84th pct, −1 SD ≈ 16th). ±2 SD covers 95% (+2 SD ≈ 98th). ±3 SD ≈ 99.7%. Memorise 68-95-99.7.

You missed this: the approach that prefers "real relationship" + UPR over the term "therapeutic alliance" is Person-centred therapy. The "alliance" (bond/goals/tasks) is Bordin's construct, not a Rogerian one.

Psychodynamic self & object relations Kohut — you said you don't know any of this

Kohut's self psychology. The self develops through 3 selfobject needs: Mirroring (being admired/validated → healthy ambition), Idealizing (merging with a calm, strong other → ideals), Twinship/alter-ego (feeling alike/belonging). Failure of mirroring → grandiosity + craving admiration (narcissistic pathology). Failure of mirroring + idealizing → a fragmented, empty self (emptiness, abandonment fears, unstable relationships).
Object relations = the internalised mental representations of self and others and the relationship between them (Klein, Winnicott, Fairbairn, Mahler). Not the id/ego/superego, not defence mechanisms.

Learning: escape vs avoidance you guessed this

Escape learning = behaviour terminates an aversive stimulus that is already present (turn off a loud alarm). Avoidance learning = behaviour prevents the aversive stimulus from starting (leave before the alarm). Both are negative reinforcement.

Mania — first-line drugs you missed the SSRI trap

First-line for acute mania: Lithium · Valproate (Divalproex) · atypical antipsychotics (Olanzapine, Risperidone, Quetiapine, Aripiprazole). Carbamazepine is second-line.
NOT first-line: Fluoxetine and any SSRI/antidepressant — they can trigger a manic switch. Antidepressants are avoided in acute mania.

7. Abnormal Psychology

BPD vs NPD you missed this

Borderline (BPD)Narcissistic (NPD)
Pervasive instability of relationships, self-image, and affect; fear of abandonment; impulsivity; self-harm; chronic emptinessGrandiosity; need for excessive admiration; lack of empathy; sense of being special/entitled
The discriminator: pervasive instability of relationships and self-image is the BPD feature that is NOT defining of NPD. Both can show emptiness, but instability + abandonment terror = BPD.

Type D personality you didn't know this

Type D ("distressed") = negative affectivity + social inhibition. Predicts poor cardiac prognosis. Contrast: Type A = hostility/competitiveness/time-urgency (CHD risk); Type B = relaxed; Type C = suppressed emotion (cancer-linked, weaker evidence).

8. Social Psychology "more concepts like bystander"
ConceptWhat it is
Bystander effectPresence of others inhibits helping. Two drivers: diffusion of responsibility + pluralistic ignorance. (Latané & Darley, after Kitty Genovese, 1964)
Diffusion of responsibility"Someone else will do it" — responsibility splits across the group
Pluralistic ignoranceEveryone privately unsure, reads others' calm as "no emergency"
Social loafingPeople exert less effort in a group than alone (Ringelmann)
Social facilitationPresence of others improves easy/well-learned tasks, impairs hard ones
DeindividuationLoss of self-awareness/restraint in a group/anonymity (mobs)
GroupthinkDesire for harmony → bad decisions; suppressed dissent (Janis)
Group polarizationGroup discussion amplifies members' initial leanings
ConformityYielding to group pressure (Asch lines). Normative vs informational influence
ObedienceFollowing authority (Milgram shocks)
Cognitive dissonanceDiscomfort from conflicting attitudes/behaviour → attitude change (Festinger)
Fundamental attribution errorOver-attribute others' behaviour to disposition, under-weigh situation
Self-serving biasCredit self for success, blame situation for failure
Just-world hypothesisBelief people get what they deserve → victim blaming
9. Memory & Cognitive "summary of all memory types"
SystemWhat it holds
Sensory memory<1 sec raw input. Iconic (visual), echoic (auditory)
Short-term / Working memory~15–30 sec, 7±2 items. Baddeley: central executive + phonological loop + visuospatial sketchpad + episodic buffer
Long-term — Explicit (declarative)Conscious recall. Splits into ↓
  • EpisodicEvents with what / where / when context (your first day of college)
  • SemanticFacts, general knowledge, meanings (capital of France)
Long-term — Implicit (non-declarative)No conscious recall. Includes ↓
  • ProceduralSkills, "how to" (riding a bike)
  • PrimingExposure speeds later processing
  • ConditioningClassically conditioned associations
Prospective memoryRemembering to do something later
Quick map: Explicit = episodic + semantic (conscious). Implicit = procedural + priming (unconscious). "What/where/when of an event" → episodic.
