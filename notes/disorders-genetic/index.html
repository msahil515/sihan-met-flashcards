Genetic, Chromosomal & Metabolic Disorders

High-yield for MET 2026 + NIMHANS · Built 2026-05-16

Why this page. NIMHANS and MET both ask "which disorder is associated with which gene / chromosome / enzyme / behaviour." The MCQ traps are almost always trisomy number, sex chromosome count, inheritance pattern, or the one giveaway feature (musical ear, self-mutilation, happy puppet, etc.). Memorise the table, then the traps.

Three things to know per disorder: (1) genetic mechanism, (2) one physical or biochemical sign, (3) one behavioural / cognitive sign. That's the MCQ unit.

2. Chromosomal disorders — deeper dive

2.1 Down syndrome (Trisomy 21)

  • Most common chromosomal cause of ID. Incidence ~1 in 700 live births overall.
  • Mechanism (95%): maternal non-disjunction at meiosis I. Risk rises sharply with maternal age (1/1500 at 20, 1/100 at 40, 1/30 at 45).
  • Other 5%: Robertsonian translocation (14;21) — inheritable, recurrence risk much higher; mosaicism — milder phenotype.
  • Physical: brachycephaly, upslanting palpebral fissures, epicanthal folds, Brushfield spots on iris, flat nasal bridge, protruding tongue, single transverse palmar crease (simian), short fifth finger (clinodactyly), sandal-gap toe.
  • Medical: congenital heart defect (AV septal defect, ~50%), duodenal atresia, hypothyroidism, atlantoaxial instability, leukaemia risk.
  • Cognitive/behavioural: IQ commonly 30-70 (mild to moderate ID), sociable temperament, language < comprehension, mosaic Down syndrome milder. Early-onset Alzheimer's by 40s (extra APP gene on chr 21).
  • Screen: triple test (low AFP, high hCG, low estriol). Confirmed by karyotype.
TRAP: "Translocation Down" can be inherited from a carrier parent. Non-disjunction Down is not inherited and increases with maternal age. Know which.

2.2 Klinefelter (47,XXY)

  • Most common sex-chromosome aneuploidy (~1 in 600 males).
  • Tall stature, long limbs, small firm testes, gynecomastia, sparse body hair, infertility (azoospermia).
  • Cognitive: verbal IQ typically below performance IQ, language delay, reading difficulties; IQ often normal or borderline.
  • Behavioural: shy, immature, anxious; increased risk of depression and breast cancer.
  • Treatment: testosterone replacement at puberty.

2.3 Turner (45,X0)

  • Only viable monosomy in humans (~1 in 2,500 females).
  • Short stature, webbed neck, low posterior hairline, shield chest with widely spaced nipples, cubitus valgus, lymphoedema of hands/feet in newborn.
  • Streak ovaries → primary amenorrhoea, infertility; coarctation of aorta.
  • Cognitive: verbal IQ usually normal, visuospatial deficit (right hemisphere pattern), nonverbal learning disorder.
  • Behavioural: social immaturity, difficulty reading social cues; not generally ID.

2.4 Fragile X (FMR1, CGG repeat)

  • Most common inherited cause of ID (Down is most common overall but mostly sporadic).
  • X-linked; full mutation = >200 CGG repeats on FMR1 → silencing of FMRP protein.
  • Males more severely affected; females show variable, milder picture (X-inactivation).
  • Phenotype: long narrow face, prominent jaw and forehead, large protruding ears, post-pubertal macroorchidism, joint hyperlaxity.
  • Cognitive/behavioural: mild-to-severe ID, ADHD, hand-flapping, gaze aversion, autism features (~30% meet ASD criteria).
  • Premutation carriers (55-200 repeats) → FXTAS (tremor/ataxia) in older males, POI in females.
TRAP: "Most common inherited ID" = Fragile X. "Most common chromosomal ID" = Down. Don't swap these.

2.5 Williams (7q11.23 deletion)

  • Microdeletion includes elastin gene → supravalvular aortic stenosis, joint laxity, hoarse voice.
  • Elfin facies: broad forehead, periorbital fullness, short upturned nose, wide mouth, full lips, small chin.
  • Cognitive: mild-to-moderate ID with classic strength in verbal expressiveness, weakness in visuospatial.
  • Behavioural: hypersocial, overfriendly with strangers, "cocktail party" personality, anxiety, strong affinity for music.
  • Hypercalcaemia in infancy.

2.7 Cri-du-chat (5p−)

  • Deletion of short arm of chromosome 5.
  • Newborn cry resembles a cat's meow (laryngeal abnormality) — pathognomonic.
  • Microcephaly, hypertelorism (wide-set eyes), epicanthal folds, low-set ears, round "moon face".
  • Severe ID, motor and language delay.

2.8 22q11.2 deletion (DiGeorge / Velocardiofacial)

  • Most common microdeletion syndrome (~1 in 4,000).
  • CATCH-22: Cardiac (conotruncal), Abnormal facies, Thymic hypoplasia (T-cell immunodeficiency), Cleft palate, Hypocalcaemia (parathyroid hypoplasia), 22q11.
  • Mild-moderate ID, learning disability, nonverbal < verbal pattern.
  • Highest single-gene risk factor for schizophrenia known (~25× general population).

3. Inborn errors of metabolism (deeper)

3.1 PKU (Phenylketonuria) — exam favourite

  • Enzyme: Phenylalanine hydroxylase (PAH) deficiency. Autosomal recessive, chromosome 12.
  • Biochemistry: phenylalanine cannot convert to tyrosine → Phe accumulates, neurotoxic; tyrosine deficiency → low melanin (fair skin, blue eyes, blond hair) and low dopamine/NE.
  • Clinical: untreated → severe ID, seizures, microcephaly, eczema, musty/mousy body odour (phenylacetate in urine/sweat).
  • Screening: Guthrie test (heel-prick at 48-72 hrs of life, blood Phe by bacterial inhibition assay). Now mostly tandem mass spectrometry.
  • Treatment: low-phenylalanine diet (avoid aspartame, meat, dairy, eggs) for life; ideally lifelong. Maternal PKU — mother off diet during pregnancy → fetal damage even if fetus is heterozygote (Phe crosses placenta).
  • Outcome with treatment: normal IQ if started in first 3 weeks of life.
TRAP: PKU is the textbook example of preventable ID. The biochemistry (Phe → Tyr block) is the most common single-line MCQ stem. "Musty smell + fair child + ID" = PKU.

3.2 Tay-Sachs

  • Enzyme: Hexosaminidase A deficiency → GM2 ganglioside accumulates in neurons. Autosomal recessive, HEXA gene chr 15.
  • High carrier rate in Ashkenazi Jews (~1/30).
  • Normal at birth; regression starts at 3-6 months: loss of motor skills, exaggerated startle to sound, progressive paralysis, blindness.
  • Cherry-red spot on macula (pathognomonic when paired with neurodegeneration).
  • Death by 3-5 years. No effective treatment.

3.3 Lesch-Nyhan

  • Enzyme: HGPRT deficiency → cannot recycle purines → uric acid build-up. X-linked recessive (males).
  • Hyperuricaemia → gout, kidney stones in childhood.
  • Self-mutilation is the unique behavioural sign — biting lips, fingertips, head-banging. Compulsive, not psychotic; child is distressed by it.
  • Choreoathetosis, spasticity, ID.
TRAP: Self-mutilation in a male child + gout-like features = Lesch-Nyhan. Self-mutilation in a Rett girl = stereotyped hand-mouthing, different. Self-injury in autism is common but is not lip-biting in the classical Lesch-Nyhan pattern.

3.4 Galactosaemia

  • GALT enzyme deficiency. Autosomal recessive.
  • Newborn vomits / fails to thrive after milk; jaundice, hepatomegaly, cataracts, E. coli sepsis.
  • Untreated → ID, ovarian failure. Treat with lactose-free diet.

3.5 Maple syrup urine disease

  • BCKD (branched-chain α-keto acid dehydrogenase) deficiency.
  • Build-up of leucine, isoleucine, valine.
  • Sweet/maple-syrup smelling urine, neonatal lethargy, seizures, coma if untreated.

3.6 Wilson's disease

  • ATP7B mutation → copper accumulates in liver, brain (basal ganglia), cornea.
  • Kayser-Fleischer rings (copper in Descemet's membrane), hepatic dysfunction, parkinsonism, dysarthria.
  • Psychiatric: personality change, depression, psychosis — can mimic primary psychiatric illness.
  • Treat with penicillamine / zinc — psychiatric features reverse.

4. Other developmental disorders worth knowing

4.1 Rett syndrome

  • MECP2 gene, X-linked dominant. Almost exclusively in girls (lethal in males).
  • Normal development 6-18 months, then deceleration of head growth.
  • Stereotyped hand-wringing / midline hand movements after loss of purposeful hand use.
  • Loss of speech, gait apraxia, breathing irregularities, seizures.
  • Was in DSM-IV PDD section; removed from DSM-5 (now a medical condition with neurodevelopmental features, not a separate ASD subtype).

4.2 Fetal Alcohol Syndrome (FAS)

  • Most common preventable cause of ID (alongside iodine deficiency globally).
  • Facial: short palpebral fissures, smooth philtrum, thin upper lip, low nasal bridge.
  • Growth retardation, microcephaly, ADHD, learning disability.
  • Threshold dose unknown; first trimester exposure most damaging to face.

4.3 Congenital hypothyroidism (Cretinism)

  • Iodine deficiency or thyroid dysgenesis in fetal life.
  • Coarse features, large tongue, umbilical hernia, hypotonia, jaundice, lethargic feeding.
  • Untreated → severe ID, short stature. Preventable by neonatal screening + thyroxine.

4.4 Neurofibromatosis 1 (NF1)

  • NF1 gene, chromosome 17, autosomal dominant. High new-mutation rate.
  • ≥6 café-au-lait spots (>5 mm pre-puberty, >15 mm post), axillary/inguinal freckling, Lisch nodules (iris hamartomas), neurofibromas, optic glioma.
  • Learning disability in ~50%, ADHD overlap, normal IQ usually.

4.5 Tuberous sclerosis (TSC)

  • TSC1 (chr 9) or TSC2 (chr 16), autosomal dominant.
  • Ash-leaf hypopigmented macules, facial angiofibromas (adenoma sebaceum), shagreen patch, cortical tubers.
  • Infantile spasms / epilepsy, ID in ~50%, ASD in ~50%.

4.6 Huntington's disease

  • CAG trinucleotide repeat expansion on chromosome 4 (HTT gene), autosomal dominant. Anticipation (earlier onset in successive generations, esp. paternal transmission).
  • Onset 30-50 yrs. Chorea (involuntary jerky movements), caudate + putamen atrophy on imaging.
  • Subcortical dementia (slow processing, executive dysfunction, preserved language) — contrast with cortical dementia of Alzheimer's.
  • Psychiatric: depression, irritability, psychosis, very high suicide risk, may precede motor signs by years.

6. Twenty MCQ traps

  1. "Most common chromosomal cause of ID" → Down. "Most common inherited cause of ID" → Fragile X.
  2. Single palmar crease + flat face + early Alzheimer's → Down.
  3. Tall male, gynecomastia, small testes, infertility → Klinefelter (47,XXY).
  4. Short female, webbed neck, amenorrhoea → Turner (45,X0).
  5. Cat-like cry → Cri-du-chat (5p−).
  6. Elfin face + hypersocial + heart murmur → Williams (7q11 elastin).
  7. Hyperphagia + obesity + hypotonic infant → Prader-Willi (paternal 15q).
  8. Happy puppet, inappropriate laughter, ataxia → Angelman (maternal 15q).
  9. 22q11.2 deletion → CATCH-22 + highest schizophrenia risk.
  10. Normal girl → regression at 1 yr + hand-wringing → Rett (MECP2).
  11. Self-mutilation + gout in boy → Lesch-Nyhan (HGPRT, X-linked).
  12. Musty urine + fair child + ID → PKU (PAH deficiency).
  13. Cherry-red macula + Ashkenazi + regression → Tay-Sachs (HexA).
  14. Chorea + dementia + family hx → Huntington (CAG, AD).
  15. KF rings + tremor + liver → Wilson's (ATP7B, AR).
  16. Café-au-lait ≥6 + neurofibromas → NF1.
  17. Ash-leaf macules + infantile spasms → tuberous sclerosis.
  18. FAS triad → smooth philtrum, thin upper lip, short palpebral fissures.
  19. Down: 95% non-disjunction (maternal age), 5% translocation (inheritable). Get the percentages.
  20. Maternal PKU: mother off diet during pregnancy damages a heterozygote fetus. Tested in MCQs as "PKU genetics in pregnancy".
