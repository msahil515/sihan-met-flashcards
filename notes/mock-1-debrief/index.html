Biopsychology, 6 wrong

Pattern: you keep losing points on where structures are and which side does what. Memorise the locations, then the lesions follow.

m1_bio03Biopsych
The corticospinal tract decussates primarily at the:
a) Optic chiasm
b) Spinal cord at the level of entry
c) Pons  ← you picked
d) Pyramids of the medulla  ← correct
You: cCorrect: d
Why d. 85-90% of corticospinal fibres cross at the pyramidal decussation in the lower medulla, forming the lateral corticospinal tract. The remaining ~10% stay ipsilateral as the anterior corticospinal tract and cross only at the spinal segmental level.
Adjacent topics they will retest:
  • Optic chiasm (option a) crosses only the nasal retinal fibres so each cortex sees the contralateral visual field. Sectioning it = bitemporal hemianopia.
  • Sensory decussations: dorsal column / medial lemniscus (fine touch, proprioception) crosses at the medulla (internal arcuate fibres). Spinothalamic (pain, temp) crosses at the spinal cord level of entry, 1-2 segments up.
  • Brown-Sequard syndrome = hemisection of cord. Ipsilateral motor + dorsal column loss below the lesion, contralateral pain/temp loss starting 1-2 segments below.
  • UMN vs LMN signs: UMN lesion (above anterior horn) = spasticity, hyperreflexia, Babinski up. LMN = flaccid, atrophy, fasciculations, areflexia.
m1_bio05Biopsychguessed
Damage to the ventromedial hypothalamus typically produces:
a) Aphagia and weight loss  ← you picked
b) Insomnia
c) Hyperphagia and obesity  ← correct
d) Loss of thermoregulation
You: aCorrect: c
Why c. VMH = satiety centre. Lesion it and you lose the "stop eating" signal → hyperphagia + obesity (the classic Hetherington-Ranson rat). You flipped it: aphagia and weight loss come from the lateral hypothalamus (LH = hunger centre) being damaged.
Hypothalamus nuclei, lock these in:
  • VMH = satiety. Lesion → hyperphagia, obesity.
  • LH = hunger / arousal. Lesion → aphagia, lethargy.
  • Anterior hypothalamus = cooling (parasympathetic-leaning). Lesion → hyperthermia.
  • Posterior hypothalamus = heating. Lesion → poikilothermia (can't maintain temp).
  • SCN (suprachiasmatic) = circadian master clock, entrained by retinohypothalamic light input.
  • Supraoptic + paraventricular = ADH and oxytocin via the posterior pituitary.
  • Arcuate = dopamine to anterior pituitary, plus appetite (NPY/AgRP and POMC neurons).
  • Mammillary bodies = memory; damaged in Wernicke-Korsakoff (thiamine deficiency).
m1_bio06Biopsych
Which of the following is NOT a basal ganglia structure?
a) Caudate nucleus
b) Globus pallidus
c) Red nucleus  ← correct (NOT basal ganglia)
d) Subthalamic nucleus  ← you picked
You: dCorrect: c
Why c. The red nucleus is in the midbrain tegmentum (origin of the rubrospinal tract, motor coordination). It is not basal ganglia. Subthalamic nucleus IS basal ganglia, that's the trap you fell into.
Basal ganglia, full inventory:
  • Striatum = caudate + putamen. Input nucleus.
  • Globus pallidus (internal + external segments). Output via GPi.
  • Subthalamic nucleus (STN). Drives the indirect pathway.
  • Substantia nigra = pars compacta (dopamine to striatum, lost in Parkinson's) + pars reticulata (output, like GPi).
Lesion patterns to memorise:
  • Parkinson's = SNc dopamine loss → rest tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, postural instability.
  • Huntington's = caudate atrophy (medium spiny neurons) → chorea + cognitive decline.
  • STN lesion = hemiballismus (wild flinging movements of contralateral limbs).
  • Wilson's disease = copper deposition in putamen → movement + psychiatric signs.
m1_bio07Biopsych
The cones of the human retina are MOST concentrated in the:
a) Optic disc
b) Peripheral retina  ← you picked
c) Fovea centralis  ← correct
d) Macula but not the fovea
You: bCorrect: c
Why c. The fovea is almost exclusively cones, packed at maximum density, giving you sharpest acuity and colour. Rods dominate the periphery. The optic disc has neither = blind spot.
Vision quick reference:
  • Rods: ~120 million, peripheral, low light, no colour, slow, rhodopsin pigment.
  • Cones: ~6 million, foveal, bright light, colour (S/M/L = blue/green/red), fast.
  • Trichromatic theory (Young-Helmholtz) = receptor-level. Opponent-process theory (Hering) = ganglion-cell-level (red-green, blue-yellow, black-white).
  • Lateral inhibition via horizontal/amacrine cells → edge enhancement (Mach bands).
  • Visual pathway: retina → optic nerve → chiasm → optic tract → LGN of thalamus → V1 (Brodmann 17, calcarine sulcus).
  • Dorsal stream (where/how, parietal) vs ventral stream (what, temporal → fusiform face area for prosopagnosia).

m1_lang05Languageguessed
Choose the sentence with the correct subject-verb agreement:
a) Each of the participants were given a consent form.
b) Each of the participants are given a consent form.  ← you picked
c) Each of the participants have given a consent form.
d) Each of the participants was given a consent form.  ← correct
You: bCorrect: d
Why d. "Each" is singular, no matter what plural noun follows it in a prepositional phrase. So you need a singular verb: was given. The "of the participants" is a distractor.
Singular/plural traps to memorise (these come back every paper):
  • Each, every, either, neither, none, anyone, everyone, somebody, nobody = singular. Each of the patients was...
  • Either…or / Neither…nor: verb agrees with the nearer subject. Neither the therapist nor the clients were aware.
  • Collective nouns (team, family, committee, jury): singular if acting as a unit, plural if as individuals. The committee has decided vs The committee are divided.
  • Data, criteria, phenomena, media are technically plural (singular: datum, criterion, phenomenon, medium). Modern usage often treats data as singular but exams often want the plural form.
  • "A number of" = plural; "the number of" = singular. A number of patients were referred. The number of dropouts was high.
  • Distance, time, money as a unit = singular. Ten kilometres is a long walk.
  • Subjects joined by "and" = plural usually. Exception: when they refer to a single thing. Bread and butter is my breakfast.
m1_lang06Language
Identify the part of speech of the underlined word: 'The data were INCONCLUSIVE.'
a) Noun
b) Verb
c) Adverb  ← you picked
d) Adjective  ← correct
You: cCorrect: d
Why d. "Inconclusive" describes "the data" (a noun) → it's a predicate adjective, sitting after the linking verb "were." Adverbs modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs — not nouns. The "-ive" ending is an adjective tell.
Part-of-speech tells (suffix shortcuts):
  • Adjectives: -ive (decisive), -ous (anxious), -able/-ible (reliable), -ful (helpful), -less (careless), -al (clinical), -ic (manic), -ish (childish).
  • Adverbs: usually -ly (quickly, slowly). Exceptions: fast, hard, late, well, very, often, here, now, soon.
  • Nouns: -tion (cognition), -ment (assessment), -ness (illness), -ity (validity), -ism (behaviourism), -er/-or (examiner), -ist (psychologist).
  • Verbs: -ate (validate), -ify (clarify), -ise/-ize (categorise), -en (deepen).
  • Predicate adjectives follow linking verbs (be, seem, appear, feel, become, remain) and describe the subject. Don't confuse with adverbs.
m1_lang09Language
RC passage: "CBT rests on the premise that distorted thinking patterns sustain emotional distress. Identifying and modifying these patterns can therefore alleviate the distress, even when the original triggers cannot be changed. Empirical trials over five decades support its effectiveness across a range of disorders."

The central idea is:
a) CBT cures every psychological disorder
b) CBT works by changing distorted thoughts that maintain distress  ← correct
c) Triggers of distress are easy to remove  ← you picked
d) Empirical trials are unnecessary in psychotherapy
You: cCorrect: b
Why b. The passage's two-sentence thesis: distorted thoughts maintain distress → modifying them helps. Option c is the opposite of what the passage says (it explicitly notes triggers can't be changed). Option a overstates ("every"). Option d misrepresents (passage praises empirical trials).
RC main-idea rules to drill:
  • The main idea is the most general statement the passage supports, not a specific detail and not the opposite.
  • Eliminate options with extreme words: every, always, never, all, none, only, must — usually too strong.
  • Eliminate options that are true but specific (not the central idea, just one supporting point).
  • Eliminate options that contradict the passage even if they sound plausible.
  • The right answer often paraphrases the topic sentence using different words.

PTSD criteria, the full structure:
  • A. Exposure to actual or threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence (direct, witnessed, learning of close other, repeated indirect exposure).
  • B. Intrusion (≥1): memories, dreams, flashbacks, distress at cues, physiological reactivity.
  • C. Avoidance (≥1): of internal or external trauma reminders.
  • D. Negative alterations in cognitions / mood (≥2): amnesia for trauma, negative beliefs, distorted blame, persistent negative emotion, anhedonia, detachment, inability to feel positive.
  • E. Alterations in arousal / reactivity (≥2): irritability, recklessness, hypervigilance, exaggerated startle, concentration problems, sleep disturbance.
  • F. Duration >1 month. G. Functional impairment. H. Not due to substance / medical.
  • Specifiers: with dissociative symptoms (depersonalisation / derealisation); with delayed expression (full criteria not met until ≥6 months post-event).
  • PTSD in children <6: separate criteria set (developmentally appropriate, fewer required symptoms).
  • Acute stress disorder = 9 symptoms across the 5 categories of intrusion, mood, dissociation, avoidance, arousal, lasting 3 days to 1 month.
