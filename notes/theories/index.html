Theories — every named theory/model, its author, the one-line hook

Third of the four category sweeps. The bucket is "named theories/models." Grouped by domain so you see the rival theories side by side (that's how they're tested: "which theory says X?" or "who proposed it?"). Built 2026-05-24.

Effects Phenomena Theories Theorists Social Psych Cognitive Lookalikes
Two question shapes. (1) "Who gave [theory]?" → name the author. (2) "Which theory holds that [claim]?" → name the theory. Rival theories in one domain are each other's distractors (James-Lange vs Cannon-Bard, Piaget vs Vygotsky), so always learn the contrast, not the theory alone.

1. Learning theories

TheoryAuthorCore claim
Classical conditioningIvan PavlovNeutral stimulus paired with a reflex-evoking one comes to evoke the reflex.
Connectionism / Law of EffectEdward ThorndikeResponses followed by satisfaction are stamped in (trial-and-error, puzzle box).
Operant conditioningB. F. SkinnerBehaviour is shaped by its consequences (reinforcement/punishment).
Contiguity theoryEdwin GuthrieOne-trial learning by sheer stimulus–response pairing in time.
Insight learningWolfgang KöhlerSudden restructuring of a problem ("aha"), shown in chimps (Sultan).
Latent learning / cognitive mapsEdward TolmanLearning happens without reinforcement and shows up later (maze rats).
Social / observational learningAlbert BanduraWe learn by watching and modelling others (Bobo doll).
Rescorla–Wagner modelRescorla & WagnerConditioning depends on surprise / prediction error, not mere pairing.
Premack principleDavid PremackA high-frequency activity can reinforce a low-frequency one ("grandma's rule").
Conditions / hierarchy of learningRobert GagnéEight learning types from signal learning up to problem-solving; later five learned capabilities + nine instructional events.
Meaningful learning / advance organizersDavid AusubelNew material is learned by anchoring it to existing knowledge; pre-teach an advance organizer first. Reception, not discovery.
Discovery learningJerome BrunerLearn by exploring and figuring it out yourself; spiral curriculum; enactive → iconic → symbolic representation.
Two-factor (avoidance) theoryO. H. MowrerAvoidance learning = classical conditioning of fear + operant reinforcement from escaping it. Explains why phobias persist.

2. Motivation theories

TheoryAuthorCore claim
Hierarchy of needsAbraham MaslowNeeds climb from physiological → safety → love → esteem → self-actualisation.
ERG theoryClayton AlderferExistence, Relatedness, Growth (Maslow condensed; allows regression).
Two-factor (motivation-hygiene)Frederick HerzbergHygiene factors prevent dissatisfaction; motivators create satisfaction.
Drive-reductionClark HullBehaviour reduces physiological drives to restore homeostasis.
Arousal theory / Yerkes–Dodson lawYerkes & DodsonPerformance peaks at moderate arousal (inverted-U).
Self-determination theoryDeci & RyanIntrinsic motivation needs autonomy, competence, relatedness.
Expectancy theoryVictor VroomEffort = expectancy × instrumentality × valence.
Goal-setting theoryEdwin LockeSpecific, hard, accepted goals drive higher performance.
Need for achievement (nAch)McClelland; AtkinsonAchievement/affiliation/power needs; measured via TAT.
FlowMihaly CsikszentmihalyiTotal absorption when challenge matches skill.
Opponent-process theoryRichard SolomonEvery emotional state triggers an opposing after-reaction (addiction, thrill).
Theory X / Theory YDouglas McGregorManagers either assume workers dislike work and need control (X) or seek responsibility and self-direction (Y).
Incentive theory(behaviourist tradition)Behaviour is pulled by external rewards/goals, not just pushed by internal drives.

3. Emotion theories (order of events is the trap)

TheoryAuthorSequence
James–LangeJames & LangeStimulus → bodily arousal → emotion ("we're afraid because we tremble").
Cannon–BardCannon & BardStimulus → arousal and emotion simultaneously (thalamic).
Schachter–Singer two-factorSchachter & SingerArousal + cognitive label → emotion.
Cognitive appraisalRichard LazarusAppraisal of the event comes first; cognition precedes emotion.
Affective primacyRobert ZajoncEmotion can occur before and without cognition ("preferences need no inferences").
Facial-feedback hypothesisTomkins; EkmanFacial expressions feed back to intensify/trigger the felt emotion.
Basic emotionsPaul EkmanSix universal, cross-cultural facial emotions.
Wheel of emotionsRobert PlutchikEight primary emotions in opposing pairs, combining into others.
The classic swap: James-Lange = body first; Cannon-Bard = both at once; Schachter-Singer = arousal + label; Lazarus = appraisal first; Zajonc = feeling before thinking. Lazarus vs Zajonc is the famous "primacy of cognition vs affect" debate.

4. Personality theories

TheoryAuthorCore idea
PsychoanalyticSigmund FreudId/ego/superego; psychosexual stages; unconscious conflict & defences.
Analytical psychologyCarl JungCollective unconscious, archetypes, introversion/extraversion.
Individual psychologyAlfred AdlerInferiority/superiority, striving for superiority, style of life, birth order.
Trait (idiographic)Gordon AllportCardinal/central/secondary traits; functional autonomy.
16PF / factor traitRaymond Cattell16 source traits via factor analysis; fluid vs crystallised intelligence.
PEN modelHans EysenckPsychoticism, Extraversion, Neuroticism (biological arousal basis).
Big Five / Five-Factor (OCEAN)Costa & McCraeOpenness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism.
Need-press / personologyHenry MurrayNeeds interact with environmental "press"; gave the TAT.
Personal construct theoryGeorge Kelly"Man as scientist"; we build bipolar constructs to predict events.
Social-cognitiveAlbert BanduraReciprocal determinism; self-efficacy.
Locus of control / social learningJulian RotterBehaviour = expectancy × reinforcement value; internal vs external control.
Person–situation / CAPSWalter MischelBehaviour is situation-specific; cognitive-affective system; delay of gratification.
Self theory (humanistic)Carl RogersSelf-concept, congruence, unconditional positive regard, fully-functioning person.
Interpersonal theoryHarry Stack SullivanPersonality forms in relationships; the self-system (good/bad/not-me).
Somatotype theoryWilliam SheldonEndomorph/mesomorph/ectomorph body types map to temperament.
Neurotic needs / basic anxietyKaren HorneyChildhood basic anxiety drives 10 neurotic needs, grouped into moving toward / against / away from people. Critiqued Freud's penis envy with "womb envy".
Humanistic psychoanalysisErich FrommPersonality is shaped by the human need to escape isolation/freedom; character orientations (productive vs non-productive).
Psychodynamic–learning (frustration-aggression)Dollard & MillerRecast Freud in learning terms; habit hierarchy; the frustration–aggression hypothesis.

5. Developmental theories

TheoryAuthorCore idea
Cognitive developmentJean Piaget4 stages: sensorimotor, preoperational, concrete & formal operational.
SocioculturalLev VygotskyLearning is social; ZPD & scaffolding; language drives thought.
Psychosocial developmentErik Erikson8 lifespan stages, each a crisis (trust vs mistrust … integrity vs despair).
Psychosexual developmentSigmund FreudOral, anal, phallic, latency, genital stages.
Moral developmentLawrence KohlbergPre-conventional → conventional → post-conventional reasoning.
Ethic of careCarol GilliganCritiqued Kohlberg; women reason via care/relationships, not just justice.
Ecological systemsUrie BronfenbrennerMicro/meso/exo/macro/chrono systems; later PPCT model.
Attachment theoryJohn BowlbyInnate bonding system; secure base; internal working models.
Strange Situation / stylesMary AinsworthSecure, anxious-ambivalent, avoidant (Main later: disorganised).
Identity statusesJames MarciaDiffusion, foreclosure, moratorium, achievement (crisis × commitment).
Temperament typesThomas & ChessEasy, difficult, slow-to-warm-up; goodness-of-fit.
Maturation theoryArnold GesellDevelopment unfolds on a biological timetable (norms).
Piaget vs Vygotsky: Piaget = development drives learning, individual discovery, fixed stages. Vygotsky = learning drives development, social/cultural, ZPD. Erikson's stages (psychosocial) ≠ Freud's (psychosexual) — Erikson has 8 across the whole lifespan.

6. Social-psychology theories

TheoryAuthorCore idea
Cognitive dissonanceLeon FestingerTension from clashing cognitions drives attitude change.
Social comparisonLeon FestingerWe evaluate ourselves by comparing to others.
Attribution theoryFritz HeiderWe explain behaviour as internal (dispositional) or external (situational).
Covariation modelHarold KelleyAttribution uses consensus, consistency, distinctiveness.
Correspondent inferenceJones & DavisWe infer disposition from freely chosen, low-social-desirability acts.
Attribution of motivationBernard WeinerLocus × stability × controllability shapes emotion & expectancy.
Balance theoryFritz HeiderWe seek consistency among our liking/relationships (P-O-X triangles).
Social identity theoryTajfel & TurnerGroup membership shapes self-concept; in-group favouritism.
Realistic conflict theoryMuzafer SherifCompetition over scarce resources breeds intergroup hostility (Robbers Cave).
Elaboration Likelihood ModelPetty & CacioppoPersuasion via central (thoughtful) vs peripheral (cues) route.
Self-perception theoryDaryl BemWe infer our own attitudes by observing our behaviour.
Psychological reactanceJack BrehmThreatened freedom → wanting the forbidden thing more.
Social exchange theoryThibaut & KelleyRelationships weighed by rewards minus costs (comparison level).
Equity theoryJ. S. AdamsWe want a fair ratio of inputs to outcomes vs others.
Bystander effect / diffusion of responsibilityLatané & DarleyThe more witnesses present, the less likely any one helps; 5-step decision model (notice → interpret → take responsibility → know how → act).
Social impact theoryBibb LatanéInfluence of a group = strength × immediacy × number of sources.
Frustration–aggression hypothesisDollard et al. (rev. Berkowitz)Frustration breeds aggression; Berkowitz added that aggressive cues are needed.

7. Intelligence theories

TheoryAuthorCore idea
Two-factor (g + s)Charles SpearmanA general factor g plus task-specific factors; factor analysis.
Primary mental abilitiesLouis Thurstone7 independent abilities, no single g.
Fluid vs crystallised (Gf-Gc)Cattell & Horn (→ CHC)Fluid = reasoning power; crystallised = accumulated knowledge.
Structure of intellectJ. P. Guilford180 abilities (operations × content × products); divergent thinking.
Multiple intelligencesHoward Gardner8+ independent intelligences (linguistic, musical, bodily-kinesthetic…).
Triarchic theoryRobert SternbergAnalytical, creative, practical intelligence.
Hierarchical modelPhilip Vernong at top → verbal-educational & spatial-mechanical → specifics.
Emotional intelligenceSalovey & Mayer (popularised by Goleman)Perceiving, using, understanding, managing emotion.
PASS theoryDas, Naglieri & KirbyIntelligence = Planning, Attention, Simultaneous & Successive processing (built on Luria's brain functions).
Three-stratum theory (CHC)John Carrollg at the top, 8 broad abilities below, ~70 narrow ones; merged with Cattell-Horn into the CHC model behind modern IQ tests.

8. Memory & cognition models

ModelAuthorCore idea
Multi-store modelAtkinson & ShiffrinSensory → short-term → long-term, linked by attention & rehearsal.
Working memory modelBaddeley & HitchCentral executive + phonological loop + visuospatial sketchpad (+ episodic buffer).
Levels of processingCraik & LockhartMemory depends on depth of encoding, not a fixed store.
Episodic vs semantic memoryEndel TulvingMemory for events vs general knowledge (later: procedural, encoding specificity).
Dual-coding theoryAllan PaivioVerbal + visual codes; images are remembered better.
Spreading activationCollins & LoftusSemantic network where activating one concept primes related ones.
Reconstructive memory / schema theoryFrederic BartlettMemory isn't a recording; we rebuild it to fit our schemas (the "War of the Ghosts" study). Basis of eyewitness error.

9. Language-development theories

TheoryAuthorCore idea
Behaviourist (learning)B. F. SkinnerLanguage is learned via imitation + reinforcement.
Nativist / LAD / Universal GrammarNoam ChomskyInnate language-acquisition device; grammar is hard-wired (rebutted Skinner).
Critical period hypothesisEric LennebergLanguage must be acquired within an early window.
Interactionist / LASSJerome BrunerSocial interaction (language acquisition support system) scaffolds language.
Linguistic relativity (Sapir–Whorf)Sapir & WhorfLanguage shapes/constrains thought.
CognitiveJean PiagetLanguage follows cognitive development (thought before language).
The famous clash: Skinner (learned via reinforcement) vs Chomsky (innate LAD/UG). Chomsky's review of Skinner's Verbal Behavior launched the cognitive revolution.

10. Stress & abnormal-psychology models

ModelAuthorCore idea
General Adaptation SyndromeHans SelyeAlarm → resistance → exhaustion response to chronic stress.
Transactional model of stressLazarus & FolkmanStress = primary + secondary appraisal; problem- vs emotion-focused coping.
Diathesis–stress model—Disorder arises when a predisposition (diathesis) meets enough stress.
Biopsychosocial modelGeorge EngelIllness from interacting biological, psychological & social factors.
Learned helplessnessSeligman & MaierUncontrollable aversive events → giving up; model of depression.
Cognitive triadAaron BeckDepression = negative views of self, world & future; schemas.
ABC modelAlbert EllisActivating event → Belief → Consequence; irrational beliefs drive distress (REBT).
Hopelessness theoryAbramson, Metalsky & AlloyReformulated helplessness: a depressive attributional style.

11. Perception theories

TheoryAuthorCore idea
GestaltWertheimer, Koffka, KöhlerThe whole is organised by grouping laws (Prägnanz).
Direct / ecological perceptionJames GibsonBottom-up: the optic array provides enough info directly (affordances).
Constructivist perceptionRichard GregoryTop-down: perception is hypothesis-testing from prior knowledge.
Feature-integration theoryAnne TreismanFeatures detected in parallel; attention binds them into objects.
Signal detection theoryGreen & SwetsDetection = sensitivity (d′) + response criterion (bias), not a fixed threshold.

12. Career & counselling theories

TheoryAuthorCore idea
RIASEC / vocational typesJohn HollandMatch person type to work environment (Realistic, Investigative, Artistic, Social, Enterprising, Conventional).
Life-span, life-spaceDonald SuperCareer develops across life stages; self-concept implemented in work.
Developmental career choiceEli GinzbergOccupational choice as a developmental process (fantasy → tentative → realistic).
Social learning / happenstanceJohn KrumboltzCareer shaped by learning experiences and planned chance.
Needs theory of careerAnne RoeEarly parent-child climate shapes whether you pick person-oriented vs non-person-oriented careers.
Circumscription & compromiseLinda GottfredsonChildren narrow career options by gender, then prestige, then interests; compromise when reality bites.
