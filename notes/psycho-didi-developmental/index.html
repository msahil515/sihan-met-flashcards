Teratogens

Drugs / Alcohol

  • Alcohol: heart defects
  • Cocaine: vascular disruption
  • Valproate: ear problems
  • Vitamin A deficiency: Spina bifida

Infections

  • Rubella: microcephaly
  • Toxoplasma: hydrocephalus
  • Varicella: limb defects

Conditions

  • Diabetes: neural tube defects
Key. Match the screening method to its window: Fetoscopy (5 wk) → CVS (8 wk) → Amniocentesis (14 wk); Maternal Blood Analysis in the 2nd month checks for neural tube defect.

Congenital

Cerebral Palsy

  • Due to head trauma to mother and other problems during development
  • Motor deficits / epilepsy / intellectual disability
  • Classification (based on body parts affected): mono, hemi, di, quadri
  • Types: spastic, ataxic, mixed, athetoid

Fetal Alcohol Syndrome

  • Cause: having alcohol during pregnancy
  • Structural: microcephaly, irregular facial features, decreased muscle tone
  • Neurological: intellectual disability, epilepsy, poor eye-hand coordination
  • Functional: low IQ, developmental disability, learning disorders, language development delays

4 · Freud's psychosexual stages of development

Theory → theorist. Psychosexual stages of development were proposed by Sigmund Freud.
StageAgeErogenous zoneKey features
Oral stage0-2 yearsMouthTypes of oral fixation — oral incorporative: dependent, naïve, collecting and hoarding objects; oral biting: sarcastic, mean
Anal stage2-3 yearsAnusEgo develops. Fixations — anal retentive: rigid, strict, excessive organization, excessive cleanliness, OCD type, hoarding, miserly, stingy; anal expulsive: spendthrift, disorganized, messy
Phallic stage3-7 yearsGenitalsSuperego develops; Oedipus complex
Latency stage7-11 yearsNoneNo fixation; sexual energy is sublimated towards activities like sports, studies, same-gender friendships
Genital stage11 and aboveGenitalsHealthy adult development occurs here; interest in opposite sex develops
Trap. Don't confuse anal retentive (rigid, overly clean, hoarding, OCD-type) with anal expulsive (messy, disorganized, spendthrift) — they are opposite fixations of the same anal stage.

  • Greater emphasis on ego

Why it matters. Erik Erikson's development is discontinuous (stages) and governed by the epigenetic principle: each stage builds on the resolution of the one before it, with a specific basic strength (virtue) as the payoff.
Trap. Erikson stages are easily confused. Note the age boundaries: Latency (6-11) = Industry vs Inferiority maps onto Sigmund Freud's latency, but Erik Erikson adds three adult stages (Intimacy, Generativity, Integrity) that Freud's genital stage collapses into one.

6 · Piaget's stages of cognitive development

Theory → theorist. The four stages of cognitive development were given by Jean Piaget.
StageAgeKey learning / task
Sensorimotor stageBirth to 2 yearsChild learns object permanence; tested with the A-not-B task
Preoperational stage2-7 yearsLearns symbolic thought, animism; egocentrism tested with the Three Mountains task
Concrete operational stage7-11 yearsLearns conservation, reversibility, mathematical operations; conservation task
Formal operational stage11 and aboveLearns hypothetico-deductive reasoning and abstract reasoning; pendulum task

Sensorimotor: sequence of events

  1. Motor reflexes
  2. Primary circular reactions — e.g. putting everything in the mouth
  3. Secondary circular reactions — manipulation of objects
  4. Synthesis of secondary circular reactions
  5. Tertiary circular reactions — object permanence achieved here; goal-directed behaviour; mental representations; concept formation

Imitation involves:

  • Deferred imitation: repeating action after some time has passed
  • Inferred imitation: imitating only correct behaviour and not the incorrect one

Horizontal décalage

When a child is able to perform conservation of mass (or any other thing) but unable to do the conservation task for length or density (or anything else).

Trap: assimilation vs accommodation. In Jean Piaget's framework, assimilation fits new information into an existing schema, while accommodation changes the schema to fit new information. Do not swap them.
Trap: Piaget vs Lev Vygotsky. Piaget saw cognitive development as an individual, stage-based process driven from within the child. Vygotsky emphasized the social and cultural context (learning through interaction, scaffolding, the Zone of Proximal Development) driving development. Piaget = discovery within stages; Vygotsky = socially mediated learning.

8 · Kohlberg's theory of moral development

Theory → theorist. The three levels of morality were given by Lawrence Kohlberg.

He presented children, adolescents and young adults with stories that included moral dilemmas, such as the story of Heinz, in his longitudinal study. He studied a group of boys in the age group 10-16 for over 20 years.

LevelSubstages
Pre-conventional moralityPunishment avoidance; instrumental or reward
Conventional moralityGood boy/girl, social acceptance, good interpersonal relationship; social order / avoiding censure (Law and Order orientation)
Post-conventional moralitySocial contract and individual rights; universal ethical principles
Trap. Keep Piaget's moral stages (Heteronomous / Autonomous, ages 5-10) distinct from Lawrence Kohlberg's three levels (Pre-conventional / Conventional / Post-conventional). Kohlberg used the Heinz dilemma; Piaget did not.

Elkind's adolescent egocentrism

There are three types of adolescent egocentrism:

  • The imaginary audience — "everyone is looking at me"
  • The mythological fable — "that won't ever happen to me"
  • The personal fable — "nobody knows what it's like to be me"

10 · Theories of attachment

Mary Ainsworth's attachment styles

Theory → theorist. Attachment styles (measured via the Strange Situation) were given by Mary Ainsworth.
StyleMother leavesMother returns
SecureUpsetHappy
Anxious / avoidantIndifferentIndifferent
Ambivalent / resistantCries; upsetAngry
DisorganizedDazed and scaredDazed and scared
Trap. On reunion, secure babies are comforted (happy), avoidant babies stay indifferent, ambivalent/resistant babies are angry (seek but resist contact), and disorganized babies look dazed and scared. Watch the reunion column, not just the separation.

Contact Comfort Theory

Theory → theorist. Contact Comfort Theory was given by Harry Harlow and Margaret Harlow.
  • They raised monkeys with two artificial mothers — one a bare wire cylinder with a wooden head and an attached feeding bottle;
  • the other a cylinder with no bottle but covered with foam rubber and wrapped with terry cloth.
  • The infants much preferred contact with the comfortable cloth mother, even while feeding from the nourishing mother.

John Bowlby's ecological theory of attachment (1969)

Theory → theorist. The four phases of attachment were given by John Bowlby (1969).
PhaseAgeFeatures
Pre-attachment phaseBirth to 6 weeksRecognize their own mother's smell, voice and face
Attachment-in-the-making phase6 weeks to 6-8 monthsInfants respond differently to a familiar caregiver than to a stranger, but they still do not protest when separated from mother
Clear-cut attachment phase (monotropy)6-8 months to 18 months-2 yearsClear-cut attachment with 1 primary attachment figure
Formation of a reciprocal relationship18 months to 2 years and onRapid growth in representation and language permits toddlers to understand some of the factors that influence the parent's coming and going and to predict her return; children negotiate with the caregiver, using requests and persuasion to alter her goals
Key. Bowlby's term monotropy = the child's clear-cut, single primary attachment figure, emerging in the clear-cut attachment phase (6-8 months onward).
