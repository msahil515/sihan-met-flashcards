ICD — the full breakdown

International Classification of Diseases: who owns it, how it's built, ICD-10 vs ICD-11, the F-codes for mental disorders, and every place it differs from DSM. Built 2026-05-24. Read the boxes and tables; the prose is just glue.

One-line frame. The ICD is the WHO's global classification of all diseases, not just mental ones. Mental disorders sit in one chapter of it. DSM (American Psychiatric Association) only does mental disorders. So: ICD = whole-body, global, free, used for official health stats; DSM = mental-only, US, paywalled, used heavily in research. NIMHANS/India officially uses ICD.
Jump to: 1. What ICD is & who owns it 2. Version history 3. Where mental disorders live 4. ICD-10 F-codes (F00–F99) 5. ICD-11 & what changed 6. ICD vs DSM 7. Exam traps

1. What ICD is & who owns it

QuestionAnswer
Full nameInternational Statistical Classification of Diseases and Related Health Problems
Who publishes itWHO (World Health Organization). DSM is APA, the contrast they love.
ScopeAll diseases and health conditions, every body system. Mental & behavioural disorders are just one chapter.
PurposeGlobal standard for mortality & morbidity statistics, billing, epidemiology. It's the official tool for cause-of-death and disease reporting worldwide.
CostFree, multilingual. DSM is copyrighted and sold by APA.
Used where~194 WHO member states, including India. DSM dominates US clinical research.

Why "F"? No mnemonic meaning, it's just the letter assigned to the mental-disorders chapter (Chapter V) in ICD-10's alphanumeric scheme. If asked "F-codes refer to," answer mental and behavioural disorders.

Trap they set: In ICD-10, OCD and PTSD sit inside F40–F48 (neurotic/stress-related), NOT as standalone chapters. That's the opposite of DSM-5, which pulled OCD and trauma out into their own chapters. Know which manual the question is about.

5. ICD-11 — what actually changed

  • Coding format changed. Mental disorders moved from F-codes to alphanumeric 6-codes (chapter 06). E.g. schizophrenia = 6A20, bipolar type I = 6A60.
  • Complex PTSD added. ICD-11 has PTSD AND Complex PTSD (PTSD core + the three "disturbances in self-organization": affect dysregulation, negative self-concept, relationship disturbance). DSM-5 does not have Complex PTSD. High-yield difference.
  • Gaming disorder added as a formal diagnosis.
  • "Mental retardation" → "Disorders of intellectual development."
  • Gender incongruence moved out of the mental disorders chapter into a new sexual-health chapter (depathologised).
  • Schizophrenia subtypes removed (paranoid, catatonic, etc. dropped), matching DSM-5. Rated by symptom dimensions instead.
  • Bereavement → Prolonged grief disorder recognised.
  • Neurodevelopmental added to the chapter title; autism + ADHD grouped here.
  • Dimensional personality model: ICD-11 scrapped the categorical PD types and uses severity + five trait domains (negative affectivity, detachment, dissociality, disinhibition, anankastia) with one retained pattern, borderline.

NIMHANS angle: India officially codes on ICD. Expect "which body publishes ICD" (WHO), "ICD chapter for mental disorders" (V / F-codes in ICD-10), and an ICD-vs-DSM difference (Complex PTSD, free-vs-paid, global-vs-US, guidelines-vs-criteria).

7. Exam traps, fast

WHO vs APA. ICD = WHO. DSM = APA. Swapping these is the #1 setup.
F = mental. "F-codes" → mental & behavioural disorders (ICD-10 Chapter V). Don't overthink the letter.
OCD/PTSD placement. ICD-10 buries them in F40–F48 (neurotic/stress). DSM-5 gave each its own chapter. ICD-11 also broke them out more.
Complex PTSD is ICD-11 only. Not in DSM-5.
First mental-disorders edition = ICD-6 (1949).
Multiaxial was DSM-IV, not ICD. ICD has never been multiaxial; DSM-5 abolished its 5 axes.
That's ICD end to end. If you remember nothing else: WHO owns it, it covers all disease, mental disorders are Chapter V/F-codes in ICD-10 and Chapter 06/6-codes in ICD-11, ICD-11 added Complex PTSD + gaming disorder and depathologised gender incongruence, and India codes on ICD.
