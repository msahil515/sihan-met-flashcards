1. Abnormal Psychology

1.1 The 4 Ds (definition of abnormality)

  • Deviance — statistical or social norm violation
  • Distress — subjective suffering
  • Dysfunction — impairs daily life / role functioning
  • Danger — risk to self or others
TRAP: Deviance alone ≠ abnormal. A genius IQ is statistically deviant but not pathological. Always need distress or dysfunction with it.

1.4 Mental Status Examination (MSE), order matters

ABS-MTPCIJ: Appearance, Behavior, Speech, Mood/affect, Thought (form + content), Perception, Cognition, Insight, Judgment.

  • Mood = subjective ("how do you feel"). Affect = observed (range, congruence).
  • Thought form = how thoughts flow (loose, tangential, flight of ideas, circumstantial). Thought content = what (delusions, suicidal, obsessions).
  • Insight = awareness of illness. Judgment = decision-making in real situations.

1.9 Suicide risk, SAD PERSONS

Sex (male), Age (<19 or >45), Depression, Previous attempt, Ethanol/drugs, Rational thinking loss, Social supports lacking, Organized plan, No spouse, Sickness.

TRAP: Strongest single predictor of completed suicide = previous attempt. Most common method globally = hanging. Highest-risk diagnosis = MDD with comorbid substance use.

1.10 Schizophrenia (Schneider, Bleuler, DSM-5)

  • Bleuler's 4 As: Associations (loose), Affect (flat), Ambivalence, Autism (withdrawal).
  • Schneider's First-Rank Symptoms: thought insertion/withdrawal/broadcasting, audible thoughts, voices commenting/arguing, made impulses/actions, somatic passivity, delusional perception. Suggestive but not diagnostic alone.
  • Positive symptoms: hallucinations, delusions, disorganized speech, disorganized behavior.
  • Negative symptoms: flat affect, alogia, avolition, anhedonia, asociality.
  • DSM-5 criteria: ≥ 2 of (delusions, hallucinations, disorganized speech, grossly disorganized/catatonic behavior, negative symptoms), at least one from first three. Active phase ≥ 1 month, total disturbance ≥ 6 months.
DisorderDuration
Brief Psychotic Disorder1 day to < 1 month
Schizophreniform1 month to < 6 months
Schizophrenia≥ 6 months
SchizoaffectiveMood episode + psychosis, but ≥ 2 wks of psychosis without mood symptoms
Delusional Disorder≥ 1 month delusions only, no other psychotic symptoms
TRAP: Kraepelin coined dementia praecox. Bleuler renamed it schizophrenia (split mind, not split personality). Don't confuse with DID.

1.11 Dissociative disorders

  • Dissociative Amnesia — gaps in autobiographical memory, often after trauma. Specifier: with dissociative fugue.
  • DID (Dissociative Identity Disorder) — ≥ 2 distinct identities, recurrent gaps in recall. Strongly linked to severe early trauma.
  • Depersonalization / Derealization Disorder — feeling unreal/detached from self or surroundings, reality testing intact.

1.12 Somatic symptom and related (DSM-5 renamed from somatoform)

  • Somatic Symptom Disorder — distressing somatic symptoms + excessive thoughts/behaviors about them.
  • Illness Anxiety Disorder — preoccupation with having illness, few/no somatic symptoms (replaces hypochondriasis).
  • Conversion Disorder (Functional Neurological Symptom) — neuro symptoms without medical explanation. La belle indifférence is a soft sign.
  • Factitious Disorder — intentional faking for sick role. Munchausen = on self; Munchausen by proxy = on another.
  • Malingering — faking for external gain (money, avoiding work). NOT a mental disorder, V-code.
TRAP: Conversion + factitious + malingering are easy to mix. Conversion = unconscious, no gain. Factitious = conscious symptom production, sick role gain. Malingering = conscious, external gain.

1.14 Substance use disorder (DSM-5)

≥ 2 of 11 criteria in 12 months. Severity: 2-3 mild, 4-5 moderate, ≥ 6 severe. DSM-5 collapsed abuse + dependence into one spectrum (no more "abuse vs dependence").

1.15 Childhood / neurodevelopmental disorders

  • IDD (Intellectual Developmental Disorder) — onset in dev period, deficits in intellectual + adaptive functioning. Severity by adaptive function, not just IQ.
  • ASD — social communication deficits + restricted/repetitive behaviors. DSM-5 merged Asperger's, PDD-NOS, autistic disorder into one spectrum.
  • ADHD — inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity ≥ 6 months, onset before 12, ≥ 2 settings.
  • Specific Learning Disorder — reading (dyslexia), writing (dysgraphia), math (dyscalculia).
  • Tic disorders — Tourette's = motor + vocal tics ≥ 1 yr, onset before 18.

2. Developmental Psychology

2.1 Principles of development

  • Cephalocaudal — head to toe
  • Proximodistal — center to periphery
  • Continuity vs discontinuity (gradual vs stages)
  • Nature vs nurture (genes vs environment, now interactionist consensus)
  • Critical periods (rigid window) vs sensitive periods (preferred but flexible window)

2.2 Piaget, 4 stages

StageAgeAchievement
Sensorimotor0-2Object permanence (~8 mo), goal-directed action
Preoperational2-7Symbolic thought, egocentrism, animism, lacks conservation
Concrete Operational7-11Conservation, reversibility, classification, seriation
Formal Operational11+Abstract, hypothetico-deductive reasoning
Sensorimotor 6 substages: Reflexes (0-1mo) → Primary circular reactions (1-4mo, on body) → Secondary CR (4-8mo, on objects) → Coordination of secondary (8-12mo, intentional, object permanence) → Tertiary CR (12-18mo, experimentation) → Mental representation (18-24mo, deferred imitation).
TRAP: Conservation ≠ object permanence. Object permanence = sensorimotor (~8mo). Conservation = concrete operational (~7yr). Easy mix-up.

2.3 Piaget key concepts

  • Schema — mental framework
  • Assimilation — fitting new info into existing schema
  • Accommodation — modifying schema for new info
  • Equilibration — drive to balance assimilation + accommodation
  • Egocentrism — preoperational, can't take others' view (Three Mountains task)
  • Animism — preoperational, attributing life to objects
  • Centration — focus on one feature, blocks conservation

2.4 Vygotsky, sociocultural

  • ZPD (Zone of Proximal Development) — gap between what child can do alone vs with help
  • Scaffolding — temporary support from more knowledgeable other (MKO)
  • Private speech — self-talk that becomes inner speech, guides thinking
  • Tools of thought — language is the master tool, internalized from social interaction
TRAP: Scaffolding term is Bruner's, applied to Vygotsky's ZPD. Vygotsky himself talked about MKO and ZPD, didn't use "scaffolding" word.

2.12 Bronfenbrenner's ecological systems

Full exam-grade primer: Bronfenbrenner, Ecological Systems Theory.

  • Microsystem — direct, face-to-face settings (family, school, peers). Bidirectional influence.
  • Mesosystem — interactions between microsystems (parent-teacher meetings, home-school link). Not a setting itself.
  • Exosystem — settings the child isn't in but which still affect them (parent's workplace, school board, mass media).
  • Macrosystem — cultural blueprint (ideology, laws, religion, SES, customs).
  • Chronosystem — the time dimension (divorce, pandemic, sociohistorical era). Added later, originally 4 systems.

1979 Ecological Systems Theory → 1994 Bioecological Model with PPCT: Proximal Processes (engine of development, regular reciprocal interactions over time), Person (demand / resource / force traits), Context (the 5 systems), Time (micro / meso / macro time).

TRAP: Exosystem ≠ no influence. Child isn't in it, but it shapes them via the microsystem (e.g. dad loses job → stress at home).
If the stem mentions: nested environments, cultural context, parent's workplace affecting child, indirect influence → answer is Bronfenbrenner.
