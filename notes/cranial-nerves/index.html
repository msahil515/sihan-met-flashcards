Mnemonics

Name mnemonic (the classic):
"On Old Olympus' Towering Tops, A Finn And German Viewed Some Hops"
→ Olfactory, Optic, Oculomotor, Trochlear, Trigeminal, Abducens, Facial, Auditory (vestibulocochlear), Glossopharyngeal, Vagus, Spinal accessory, Hypoglossal.
Type mnemonic (Sensory / Motor / Both):
"Some Say Marry Money, But My Brother Says Big Brains Matter More"
→ S, S, M, M, B, M, B, S, B, B, M, M (I–XII).
Brainstem exit level:
Midbrain: III, IV. Pons: V, VI, VII, VIII. Medulla: IX, X, XI, XII.
(I + II are forebrain; everything else exits brainstem top-to-bottom in number order.)

Brainstem nuclei map

LevelCN nuclei present
MidbrainIII (oculomotor + Edinger-Westphal), IV (trochlear)

MedullaIX (nucleus ambiguus motor + inferior salivatory + solitary, taste/visceral), X (dorsal motor + ambiguus + solitary), XI (nucleus ambiguus + spinal accessory nucleus C1–C5), XII (hypoglossal)

Sensory nuclei sit lateral, motor nuclei sit medial. Solitary nucleus (taste + visceral afferent) gets VII, IX, X.

High-yield deficits to memorise

  • CN III palsy — ptosis + "down and out" eye + fixed dilated pupil. Compressive cause (PCom aneurysm, uncal herniation) hits parasympathetic fibres first → pupil involved. Ischaemic cause (diabetes) often pupil-sparing.
  • CN IV palsy — vertical diplopia, worse looking down (reading, stairs). Compensatory head tilt away from lesion (Bielschowsky sign).
  • CN VI palsy — inability to abduct, horizontal diplopia worse on gaze to lesion side. First CN to fail in raised ICP — false localising sign.
  • Bell's palsy (CN VII, LMN) — whole half-face including forehead, taste loss anterior ⅔ tongue, hyperacusis, dry eye. UMN (stroke) spares forehead because upper face has bilateral cortical input.
  • Acoustic neuroma (CN VIII) — unilateral sensorineural hearing loss + tinnitus + vertigo. At cerebellopontine angle so later involves V and VII as it grows.
  • Bulbar palsy — LMN lesion of IX, X, XI, XII (and sometimes V, VII). Flaccid, fasciculating tongue, nasal speech, dysphagia. Cause: MND (PMA variant), Guillain-Barré, brainstem stroke.
  • Pseudobulbar palsy — UMN lesion of same CNs. Spastic tongue (can't protrude), brisk jaw jerk, emotional lability, "Donald Duck" speech. Cause: bilateral strokes, MS, MND (UMN variant).
  • CN XII lesion — tongue deviates TOWARD lesion (LMN) — "the lick goes to the sick." UMN lesion deviates AWAY.
  • CN X / vagus lesion — uvula deviates AWAY from lesion. Hoarse voice if recurrent laryngeal involved.
  • Jugular foramen syndrome (Vernet) — IX, X, XI together (they all exit the jugular foramen). Cause: glomus tumour, base-of-skull fracture.

High-yield MCQ traps

  • "Which CN bypasses the thalamus?" — Olfactory (I). Every other sensory modality relays through thalamus.
  • "Which CN exits dorsally from the brainstem?" — Trochlear (IV). Also the only one to decussate before exit.
  • "First CN affected in raised ICP?" — Abducens (VI), longest intracranial course.
  • "Which CN is most often a false localising sign?" — VI (raised ICP) and III (uncal herniation).
  • "Bell's palsy spares the forehead?" — NO. Bell's is LMN, whole half-face. Forehead-sparing = UMN = stroke.
  • "Tongue deviates which way in CN XII lesion?" — TOWARD the lesion (LMN). The weak side can't push the tongue out so the strong side pushes it across.
  • "Uvula deviates which way in CN X lesion?" — AWAY from lesion. Opposite of tongue. Same logic: strong side pulls uvula across.
  • "Gag reflex — afferent vs efferent?" — Afferent IX, efferent X. (Both, but knowing which limb is which is the trap.)
  • "Smallest CN?" — Trochlear (IV).
  • "Largest CN?" — Trigeminal (V).
  • "CN with longest intracranial course?" — Abducens (VI).
  • "Which CN carries taste from anterior ⅔ tongue?" — Facial (VII) via chorda tympani. Posterior ⅓ = IX. Epiglottis = X.
  • "Acoustic neuroma associated with?" — NF2 (bilateral), CPA lesion, CN V/VII signs as it expands.
  • "Argyll Robertson pupil" — accommodates but does NOT react to light. Neurosyphilis classic. "Prostitute's pupil — accommodates but doesn't react."
  • "Marcus Gunn pupil (afferent pupillary defect)" — CN II lesion. Swinging flashlight: affected eye dilates when light moves to it.

Bedside testing (NIMHANS clinical viva)

CNHow to test
ISmell coffee/cloves each nostril separately. (Often skipped in routine exam.)
IISnellen chart, fields by confrontation, fundoscopy, pupillary light reflex (afferent), colour vision (Ishihara).
III, IV, VI"H-pattern" eye movements, pupils (light + accommodation), look for ptosis, diplopia.
VLight touch + pinprick V1/V2/V3 each side. Corneal reflex (afferent V, efferent VII). Clench teeth (masseter bulk). Jaw jerk.
VIIRaise eyebrows, screw eyes shut tight, show teeth, puff cheeks. Watch for forehead sparing.
VIIIWhisper test or finger-rub each ear. Rinne (tuning fork mastoid vs air) + Weber (forehead, lateralises to affected ear in conductive loss, away in sensorineural).
IX, XSay "ahh" — uvula should rise midline. Gag reflex (touch posterior pharynx). Voice quality (hoarse = X).
XIShrug shoulders against resistance (trapezius). Turn head against resistance (SCM — turns to opposite side).
XIIProtrude tongue — note deviation, fasciculations, atrophy. Push against cheek from inside.
One-line revision trick. The eye-movement trio (III, IV, VI) is tested together as an H-pattern, the lower-CN bulbar group (IX, X, XII) is tested together with "ahh" + tongue + voice, and VII gets its own block (eyebrows + eyes shut + teeth + cheeks). If you can run those three sub-exams from memory, you've covered 8 of the 12 CNs.
