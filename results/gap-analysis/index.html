A. Option-reading slips: five rules

9 misses where a rule would have struck your pick without knowing the content: JSS Q46, Q76, Q80, Q82, Q87 and RML Q24, Q32, Q47, Q82. These are the cheapest marks on this page.

A1. A famous surname with the wrong first name, or a word that doesn't exist, is a plant. Real names: Wilhelm Wundt, Erich Fromm, Donald Winnicott, Gerald Klerman. You took “William Wundt” for functionalism (JSS Q46), even though you'd got IHBAS Q11 right five days earlier: functionalism = James. You also took “Richmond Fromm” (JSS Q80), “Charles Winnicot” (JSS Q25) and “Lexphasia”, which isn't a term (RML Q47). Strike these first, then choose among what's left.
A2. EXCEPT / NOT / “would not”: mark every option true or false, then pick the false one. The false one usually carries an absolute or an odd word. RML Q24: “crisis-related bereavement should always be considered traumatic grief” was the false one. RML Q32: case management aims at functioning, not the “cure of a primary condition”. RML Q82: “all groups have an analytic base” is the odd jargon. Same rule on JSS Q88, where analysis of transference is Freud's, not Adler's.
A3. A combo option (“both a and b”, “(a) and (c)”) is only as true as its weaker half. JSS Q76: a and b were the same wrong idea said twice (the rational method), so “both” was doubly wrong. The MMPI is empirical criterion keying. RML Q90: you took “(a) and (c)”, but (c) isn't true of all three people, so the combo is dead even though (a) is right.
A4. If you can defend two single options and “All of the above” is there, take it. JSS Q87: anxiety, unfulfilled wishes and personality are all named causes of helicopter parenting.
A5. Mirror terms get flipped under pressure. Before you tap, say the definition to yourself for any pair that is the same idea reversed: inductive/deductive (JSS Q82), escape/avoidance, positive/negative reinforcement, primary/secondary appraisal, proactive/retroactive interference.

Bereavement and case management (RML Q24, Q32)

Bereavement. Normal grief is a process that runs its course and doesn't need treatment. Complicated (prolonged) grief does need intervention: DSM-5-TR's prolonged grief disorder needs at least 12 months since the death in adults (6 months in children). Counselling can be one-to-one or in groups. Crisis-related bereavement is not always traumatic grief, and that “always” made it the EXCEPT answer. Models: Kübler-Ross's five stages (1969: denial, anger, bargaining, depression, acceptance); Worden's four tasks of mourning (accept the loss, work through the pain, adjust to a world without the person, find an enduring connection while moving on); Bowlby's phases.

Case management links a person with chronic illness to services and keeps the care going. Its goals are functioning, skills, empowerment, environmental accommodations and continuity of care. Cure of the primary condition is not one of them.
