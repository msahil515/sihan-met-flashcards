The RCI Syllabus, and the six entrances

RCI curriculum · IHBAS · NIMHANS · CIP Ranchi · RML · DIMHANS · JSS  ·  built 4 Aug 2026

You asked for the RCI M.Phil Clinical Psychology syllabus for IHBAS, NIMHANS and the rest. There is no single answer to that, because two different documents both get called "the syllabus" and only one of them changes your prep. (1) The course syllabus is written centrally by the Rehabilitation Council of India and is the same at every approved centre in the country — six papers, fixed marks, fixed clinical hours. It tells you what you will study once you are in. It is quoted here off RCI's own two PDFs, verbatim. (2) The entrance syllabus is written by each institute separately, and they diverge hard — that is exactly the RML-vs-IHBAS thing you noticed. That is the one worth your time, and it is laid out side by side below, all six colleges, counted off real papers rather than described.

Short version, before anything else. One syllabus covers 90–100% of every one of these papers, so you are not preparing six subjects, you are preparing one and then patching a small residue per college. The residue is small and named: RML's social-work / case-management strand (~12 of 100), NIMHANS' thin English-and-reasoning tail (~3), and CET-166's Section A of general awareness and reasoning. Everything else is the same eight areas in different proportions. The single biggest structural fact on this page is not a topic at all: the new RCI MA rules make the interview worth 30%, up from nothing at IHBAS and NIMHANS today.

Overlap and gaps — the actual answer

The overlap. Eight areas — abnormal, general/cognitive, biopsych, clinical/therapy, stats and research methods, social, developmental, assessment — account for 100% of the IHBAS paper, 97% of NIMHANS, 100% of CIP (its Personality 8 is part of the same core) and 90% of RML 2020. Those same eight are eight of JSS's nine named subjects. So a single body of preparation is worth 90 to 100 marks out of every 100 on every college on this page. That is the whole finding: you are preparing one subject, not six.

The gaps are small, specific and worth naming precisely, because each one is cheap to close once you know it exists:

The one real divergence inside the shared core. It is not "how much clinical", which is near-identical everywhere (39 per 100 on IHBAS against 36 on RML 2020). It is which half of the clinical block carries it. CIP 2020 put 25 into abnormal and 2 into therapy. NIMHANS puts 18 into therapy, the most of any of them. IHBAS splits it 17/15. So the movable part is psychotherapy and the therapy schools — who founded what, named in full. That is the one sub-area whose weight genuinely triples or collapses depending on which college's hall you are in, which is the argument for preparing abnormal and therapy as a single unit and never betting on last year's split.

The RCI course syllabus OFFICIAL

Rehabilitation Council of India · quoted off RCI's own guideline PDFs

This is the part that is genuinely centralised. RCI writes one curriculum and every approved centre — IHBAS, NIMHANS, CIP, RML, DIMHANS, JSS, all of them — teaches it. The current live document is the MA in Clinical Psychology (RCI Regulated Programme), effective 2025-26, which replaces the M.Phil. Both documents are covered here because the M.Phil version is still running in the centres that have not converted yet.

"With the implementation of NEP 2020 there has been marked shift in higher education including professional education. One of the changes mandated by UGC is to discontinue all M.Phil. programmes including M.Phil. in Clinical Psychology. It has therefore, necessitated the present programme in place of M.Phil. Clinical Psychology. It is being nomenclated as 'M.A. (Clinical Psychology) 2 year programme, RCI regulated'." — RCI, MA Clinical Psychology Guidelines & Syllabus, 2025, preface

The six papers

Two years, three theory papers a year, plus practicals, case submissions and a thesis. Every paper is 3 hours, 70 marks final assessment + 30 internal = 100.

Year 1 · Paper I — Psychosocial Foundations of Behavior and Psychopathology

3 hr · 70 + 30 = 100 marks · 12 units

  1. Introduction: scope of clinical psychology, the profession and practice
  2. Mental health and illness: care past and present, stigma and attitude
  3. Epidemiology: Indian studies, socio-cultural correlates
  4. Self and relationships: self-concept, self-image, self-regulation
  5. Family influences: early deprivation, trauma, neglect and abuse, attachment
  6. Societal influences: discrimination by race, gender, ethnicity; social class
  7. Disability: definition, classification, psychosocial models
  8. Rehabilitation: approaches and interventions
  9. Rights issue: UNCRPD, empowerment, rights of the disabled
  10. Introduction to psychopathology: normality and abnormality
  11. Psychological theories: psychodynamic, behavioural, cognitive, humanistic
  12. Indian thoughts: concepts of mental health and illness, nosology, taxonomy

Year 1 · Paper II — Psychiatry

3 hr · 70 + 30 = 100 marks · 6 units (was 11 in the M.Phil)

  1. Psychoses and neurotic disorders: schizophrenia, affective, delusional
  2. Disorders of personality and behaviour
  3. Organic mental disorders: dementia, delirium
  4. Behavioural, emotional and developmental disorders of childhood
  5. Neurobiology of mental disorders and current therapeutics
  6. Liaison psychiatry and subspecialties

Year 1 · Paper III — Statistics and Research Methodology

3 hr · 70 + 30 = 100 marks · 6 units (was 12)

  1. Introduction: hypothesis testing, significance, sample
  2. Epidemiological studies: rates, prevalence, incidence
  3. Analysis of variance: fixed, random and mixed effects models
  4. Multivariate analysis: multiple regression, logistic regression, PLS
  5. Qualitative research: methods and analysis
  6. AI: machine learning, neural networks for prediction, and applications

Year 2 · Paper I — Biological Determinants of Behavior and Neuropsychology

3 hr · 70 + 30 = 100 marks · two blocks

  1. Anatomical subdivisions of the brain, surface and interior structures
  2. Biochemistry of the brain, medical genetics
  3. Neurobiology of sensory-motor systems
  4. Psychopharmacology: principles, sites of drug action
  5. Neuropsychology: structure-function, frontal and temporal lobe syndromes
  6. Parietal and occipital lobe syndromes
  7. Neuropsychological assessment: principles, approaches, indications
  8. Neuropsychological rehabilitation

Year 2 · Paper II — Behavioral Medicine

3 hr · 70 + 30 = 100 marks · 7 units

  1. Introduction: psycho-behavioural influence on neuroendocrine systems
  2. Central nervous system: disturbance in major CNS conditions
  3. Cardiovascular system: personality, lifestyle, health practice
  4. Respiratory system
  5. Genitourinary / renal / reproductive: sexual and psychosocial issues
  6. Gastrointestinal system and dermatology
  7. Oncology and pain

Year 2 · Paper III — Psychotherapy and Counseling

3 hr · 70 + 30 = 100 marks · 7 units

  1. Introduction: training of therapists, ethical and legal issues, rights
  2. Affective psychotherapies: psychodynamic, historical and empirical status
  3. Behaviour therapies: indications and empirical status
  4. Cognitive therapies: REBT and the rest, indications and empirical status
  5. Systemic and physiological therapies: family therapy and others
  6. Counselling and psychoeducation: behavioural, cognitive, humanistic
  7. Psychotherapy research: treatment effects, comparators

Pass mark is 50% in each theory paper, each practical and viva, each submission and the thesis — separately, not in aggregate. 75%+ overall is a distinction, 60–75% is First Class, and the merit class is only awarded if both parts are cleared in the first attempt. No more than three attempts at either part. Only 300 of the 1,200 marks in the whole degree are the classic Year-2 theory papers. Half the degree is practicals, case records and a thesis.

The clinical work that is actually mandated

This is the bit that makes the course what it is, and it is a fixed floor, not a suggestion. By the end of the two years:

Of the therapy hours, no fewer than 50 hours each in: therapies with children; individual therapies with adults; family/marital/group/sex therapy; and psychological or neuropsychological rehabilitation. A supervised logbook has to be produced to the examiners. And the reason the seat counts are so brutally small is in the same document: a centre needs a minimum turnover of 250 cases a month for an intake of four candidates, and one extra seat per additional 50 cases a month. That is why IHBAS has 11 seats and CIP has 21 — the ceiling is the hospital's caseload, not the classroom.

The 70/30 rule is the thing to watch, and it has not landed everywhere yet. RCI's MA document prescribes 70% written and 30% interview. Right now only RML's CET-166 actually matches it. IHBAS and NIMHANS still select on the written paper alone with no interview, CIP runs 100 CBT against a 50-mark interview, JSS adds a 10-mark viva. Those are the institutes' own current published patterns and they govern the exam you will actually sit — but as each converts its M.Phil to the RCI MA, the interview is contractually supposed to become worth 30% of the decision. On a paper where you are scoring 91, a 30% interview is a bigger swing than any topic on this page. Check the interview weightage on every notification before you sit it, and treat the therapy-style profile and dissertation prep as scoring prep, not decoration.

What makes NIMHANS different from the other five. Two things, and only two. First, therapy is the top area at 18 — on CIP the same area was 2. If you prepare for CIP and walk into NIMHANS you have under-prepared the single biggest block. Second, it is one of only two papers here with negative marking, and it is also the one with no interview, so the written score is the entire decision and wrong answers cost you. That combination makes NIMHANS the paper where guessing discipline is worth the most marks. Everything else about it is the standard core.

RML, Delhi — and which paper is which

ABVIMS & Dr. Ram Manohar Lohia Hospital · degree awarded through GGSIPU · two separate RCI clinical trainings that get confused constantly

RML runs two programmes and they are not the same exam. The legacy M.Phil CET is 100 MCQs × 4 marks, 120 minutes, no negative, 90% CET + 10% interview, ~13 seats. CET-166 is the new RCI MA — reported as ~70 marks split into a Section A of general awareness, logical reasoning and research aptitude (~20) plus a Section B of subject psychology (~50), with 70% test + 30% interview. The 2020 paper you sat is the M.Phil one. It is a fair content rehearsal for CET-166 and an unreliable format rehearsal.

Published syllabus GAP

GGSIPU publishes no numbered CET-166 syllabus. What is confirmed is the area list: general psychology, clinical psychology, child/developmental psychology, research methods, assessment and tests, counselling and psychotherapy, social, personality, biopsychology, Indian psychology. The depth comes from how the RML/IPU clinical papers have actually behaved, and all six real RML papers (2020 through 2025) are live as mocks on this site with per-distractor explanations.

How the RML paper behaves FROM PAST PAPERS

  • Clinical and therapy are factual and founder-driven. Who developed which therapy, plus the acronyms themselves — REBT, DBT, ACT, EMDR, IPT, MI. The paper rewards the name, not the concept.
  • Abnormal goes deliberately beyond DSM. In-depth, factual, and fond of disorders current DSM has dropped. A DSM-only revision misses exactly what they fish for; lean on ICD and the classic eponyms.
  • Indian psychology, both ends of the timeline. The classical frameworks and the modern history — first departments, RCI, the Acts. Revising only the Vedantic side is the standard trap.
  • The social-work and case-management strand. 10 of 100 on the 2020 paper, plus roughly 2 more inside the Social block. RML only — no other college on this page asks it.
