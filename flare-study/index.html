Studying during a flare-up

Start with one familiar concept at a time, in whichever format feels easiest today.

These are study-format suggestions to try, not a treatment or a proven best format for fibromyalgia. There is no clinical evidence here that cards, audio, or any other format is best. Fibromyalgia can involve fatigue and problems with concentration or memory; sensitivity to noise or light can also happen. Audio is always optional. NIAMS: Fibromyalgia

A small card can be enough

Try one short card on a topic you already recognize. Keep the main part simple:

  • Definition: one clear sentence.
  • Example: one concrete example.
  • Optional: a quick comparison with a confusable idea, or one recall question with its answer visible.

Begin with familiar topics. If it feels comfortable, you could briefly read the card aloud or listen to audio while keeping the same text available as a transcript. Skip audio if sound is uncomfortable. Audio files may not be available for every card.

Example card · learning

Positive and negative reinforcement

Definition: Both positive and negative reinforcement increase a behavior. Positive reinforcement adds a valued outcome; negative reinforcement removes an unpleasant condition.

One example

A car’s seatbelt alarm stops when you buckle up. If this increases future buckling, it is negative reinforcement. The sound being removed alone does not establish reinforcement.

Optional comparison

Positive adds something valued; negative removes something unpleasant. In both cases, the behavior increases.

Optional recall, with answer visible

Question: Does negative reinforcement decrease behavior?
Answer: No. Reinforcement increases behavior; “negative” means something unpleasant is removed.

Choose your stopping point

After one card, stop and rest, or continue if it feels comfortable. There is no fixed study quota. Skip recall if it takes too much effort. There is no need for timed mock exams or streaks. Longer or difficult new learning can wait for a time when you have more capacity; that is a pacing option, not a promise that symptoms will improve or go away.

The existing Concept Viva cards are one optional source of familiar concepts. Card length may vary. This page is a standalone guide; no flare-up study mode has been built.

Sources and scope
