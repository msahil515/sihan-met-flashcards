PYQ fact-check: everything that changed

26 Nov 2026. The 9 papers checked after you said to keep going, plus RML 2025, 2024, 2022, 2021 and 2020, IHBAS 2024 and JEMAS(PG) 2025, 2024, 2023 and 2022 (added later), all in one place. Each paper: 4 or 5 readers went through every explanation and every trap, and every fix was checked against the source (the original paper, the textbook the question was copied from, or the official key). Everything is already in the Tests app and on the site; no new APK needed.

Key changes (your score on these can move)

  • RINPAS 2019 Q27: a to c. "Most psychologists believe that aggression is" is Morris and Maisto's own sentence: "largely a learned behavior". So a learned response, not an innate response to frustration.
  • RINPAS 2019 Q78: b to a. The heredity question is Lahey's neuroscience perspective word for word, and Lahey's own practice test keys "neuroscience". Biogenic is the abnormal psych word for biological causes, not a perspective.
  • GMCH 2024 Q81: d to b (now follows GMCH's official key). All four options are Pavlov's own words, so there is no clean answer; the mock goes with GMCH's irradiation and the Key note says so.
  • RML 2024 Q91: c to b (added later on 26 Nov). "The visual field covered by each human eye" is about 160 degrees: 60 towards the nose, 100 to 107 towards the temple. 180 degrees is the figure for both eyes together, so GGSIPU's printed key was wrong.
  • IMHANS 2021 Key notes corrected, keys the same. LBS's revised key did not switch Q15 and Q72 to a. It accepts two answers (Q15 a or c, Q72 a or b, Q51 b or c), so the mock's answers were accepted too. 2021 is now 74 of 75 against LBS.

Every other paper: no key changes. GMCH 2023 and 2025 matched 100 of 100 on a blind re-solve; the IMHANS keys already follow the official LBS keys, so only the explanations were checked.

RINPAS 2019

2 key changes (above) + 4 explanation fixes · Open the mock

  • Q4: in Pavlov's experimental neurosis the ellipse was made rounder and rounder towards the circle, not a circle turned into an ellipse.
  • Q10: response coming back after a new stimulus = dishabituation; after a rest = spontaneous recovery (the card had them mixed). Same fix in the PYQ diagnostic quiz.
  • Q28: Adler's first master motive was the aggression drive (1908); will to power came after it, then striving for superiority.
  • Q96: in Vaillant's ladder denial is a psychotic-level defence; the immature ones are projection, acting out and so on.

GMCH 2025

No key changes · 6 explanation fixes · Open the mock

  • Q18: self-actualisation isn't Maslow's own word. Kurt Goldstein used it first, Rogers used it too (1959); Maslow made it famous.
  • Q35: the 1951 Gestalt Therapy book is by Perls, Hefferline and Goodman. Laura Perls co-founded the approach but isn't an author.
  • Q53: Motivational Interviewing has a 4th edition (2023). Same fix in IHBAS Mock 2 Q46.
  • Q76: Binet's 1905 scale had no total score; mental level came with the 1908 scale and was later called mental age.
  • Q89: Beck's 1967 list is arbitrary inference, selective abstraction, overgeneralisation, magnification/minimisation and inexact labelling. Personalisation and all-or-nothing thinking come in his 1976 book.
  • Q98: Yerkes-Dodson 1908: the middle shock was best only on the medium and hard tasks; on the easy task learning kept getting faster with stronger shock.

IMHANS 2025

Keys follow LBS · 12 explanation fixes · Open the mock

  • Q1: same Ivey slip: paraphrasing was not in his 1968 study, it came into his model later.
  • Q7 and Q38: Rogers' 1957 paper lists six necessary and sufficient conditions. The three the therapist brings (congruence, unconditional positive regard, empathy) are the core conditions.
  • Q12: Broca's patient Tan (1861) was the first strong evidence that language is localised. The left-hemisphere part came in 1865, after more cases. Same card in IHBAS Mock 1 Q59.
  • Q23: stressors lasting minutes boost some immunity, but real-life brief stressors like exams suppress it (Segerstrom and Miller 2004). Trap c: the stress-hormone result on new neurons is Gould 1992 in rats; her 1997 study was social stress in tree shrews.
  • Q29: DSM-5-TR codes malingering as Z76.5. V codes were ICD-9 and are gone.
  • Q31: Linehan's 1991 trial counted acts of parasuicide, not self-harm attempts.
  • Q33: Bouchard 1990 linked about 70% of IQ variance to genes. Twins reared apart were about as alike as twins reared together on personality, interests and attitudes, not on IQ.
  • Q36: Holland's Self-Directed Search came out in 1970, not 1971. Same fix in the PYQ diagnostic quiz.
  • Q46: the relative refractory period = potassium channels still open plus some sodium channels still recovering. The card had left the sodium part out. Same card in IHBAS Mock 1 Q14.
  • Q59: Motivational Interviewing's 4th edition (2023) spirit is partnership, acceptance, compassion and empowerment. Evocation was the 2013 word.
  • Q72: the current WAIS-5 (2024) has five main indexes (Perceptual Reasoning split into Visual Spatial and Fluid Reasoning). Trap a: mental age came with the 1908 Binet-Simon scale, not 1905.

  • Q20 trap a: good continuation (also called continuity) is a standard Gestalt term. The card said it wasn't.
  • Q25: la belle indifference was first used by Freud (Elisabeth von R., 1895), who credited it to Charcot; Janet described the indifference but never used the term (Stone et al. 2006). GGSIPU's key says Janet, so the key stays c and the card now tells you both. Trap a no longer says Freud didn't use it.
  • Q31: 13 to 16 hours is when imprinting peaks in mallard ducklings (Hess), not "the first 13 to 16 hours" of Lorenz's window. Same card in SGT PYQ Q61.
  • Q35 traps: Heinz is the man in Kohlberg's moral dilemma, not an attention theorist. Ebbinghaus did note that his own span was about seven syllables; the magical number and chunking are Miller's.
  • Q36: the paper prints "McCray", the real authors are McCrary and Hunter (Science, 1953). Trap b: some books credit Ebbinghaus (1902) with first reporting the serial position effect, so b is wrong only by this paper's key.

  • Q53: the card argued for visual illusion against its own key. The question is copied word for word from Biswas's forensic medicine book, which uses this exact rail-track example for universal or permanent illusions. Key a holds, card and trap c now say why.
  • Q62: a hypnic jerk is the muscle twitch as you fall asleep; the falling feeling often comes with it. Same card in St John's Mock 1 Q94.
  • Q79: corpus callosotomy is still used for severe epilepsy (mainly drop attacks), not only in the past. Same card in JSS Mock 2 Q32.
  • Q85: the word Volkerpsychologie came from Lazarus and Steinthal, Wundt credits them himself. He built it into a full programme, so the key stays Wundt. Same card in Diagnostic 2 Q1.
  • Q87 trap c: a valid test does have to be reliable; c is wrong because of "by definition". The old trap contradicted the card's own explanation.
  • Q88: Gage was hurt in 1848; Harlow wrote up the personality change in 1868, and the damage was mostly to the left frontal lobe. "Spares motor abilities" now says prefrontal, since the frontal lobe holds the motor cortex. Same card in SGT PYQ Q57.
  • Q91: re-keyed to 160 degrees (see Key changes). The card now gives the real numbers: one eye about 160 side to side and about 135 to 150 up and down, both eyes about 200 with about 120 of overlap.
  • Q96: bulimia has no binge-purge type. Binge-eating/purging is a type of anorexia, the one with more impulse problems. Same card in SGT PYQ Q58.

RML 2022 (GGSIPU code 157), added 27 Nov

Keys follow GGSIPU's key off the exam-portal sheet, Q89 re-keyed (pineal) · 14 cards fixed · Open the mock

  • Q10: Jones and Smith named fetal alcohol syndrome in 1973, but Lemoine's team in France had described it first, in 1968. Same card in St John's Mock 2 Q87.
  • Q11: persistent complex bereavement was a proposed condition in DSM-5. In DSM-5-TR (2022) it became prolonged grief disorder, a full diagnosis. NSSI is still a proposed condition, so key d stays.
  • Q22: the card tagged Vygotsky's "tasks society sets adolescents" with Havighurst (1948). Vygotsky died in 1934, the idea is his own.
  • Q23: "educable" was the old school category, not an AAMD level. AAMD used mild, moderate, severe and profound.
  • Q30: Pavlov never showed taste aversion. Garcia, Kimeldorf and Koelling did (1955, with radiation sickness), then Garcia and Koelling (1966).
  • Q36 trap a: signal detection theory drops the fixed threshold. It uses hits and false alarms (d-prime and criterion).
  • Q40: antidepressants don't usually cause tardive dyskinesia, but DSM-5 counts amoxapine (sold as an antidepressant) with the dopamine blockers that can. Same card in SGT PYQ Q43.
  • Q44: losing all of childhood is localized amnesia (one set period, can run for years), not selective. Selective means some events of a period are remembered and some aren't. Same card in St John's Mock 1 Q89.
  • Q55 and Q70: the five groups of projective tests (association, construction, completion, choice or ordering, expression) are Lindzey's (1959), not Frank's. Frank coined the word projective. Q55 also: the projective Draw-a-Person is Machover's (1949); Goodenough's 1926 Draw-a-Man was an intelligence test. Same cards in JSS Mock 2 Q2 and Q11.
  • Q59: bigger samples don't shrink error variance. They raise power through the between-groups side of F. Same card in St John's Mock 2 Q72.
  • Q67: the WISC-V Full Scale IQ comes from 7 subtests, not from the 5 index scores. Same card in St John's Mock 1 Q38.
  • Q82: Burke, Arkowitz and Menchola (2003) did review smoking, but MI didn't work for it there (it did for alcohol, drugs, and diet and exercise). Key d (OCD) stays.
  • Q97: Gesell, not Gessell.
