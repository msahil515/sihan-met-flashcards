1. Is 364 the ×4 number?

Yes. 364 is the conversion, not a screen number, and the conversion was done right.

364 ÷ 4 = 91 exactly — a whole number of questions, which is what a real ×4 conversion always produces. Our engine cannot print 364: it scores out of 100 and the largest number it can ever show is 100.00. So 91 right, 9 not right, and on the real 2020 scale that is 364 / 400.
The check that proves the method was right, not just the number. If you had multiplied the engine score by 4 instead of the raw correct count, you would not have landed on 364. The engine charges −0.25 a wrong answer, so a 91-right run reads somewhere between 88.75 and 91.00 out of 100 depending on how many of the nine were attempted-and-wrong versus left blank. Multiply those by 4 and you get 355 to 364 — and only the top of that range, the one that comes from multiplying the raw correct count, is divisible by 4. 364 is. The number is clean.
Why the −0.25 vanishes on the real paper. RML 2020 was 100 questions × 4 marks = 400, no negative marking. Every question is worth 4 whether you leave it blank or get it wrong — both pay zero. So on the real scale marks = correct × 4, full stop, and the wrong-versus-blank split does not change your 364 at all. It only matters for the coaching point: a blank is strictly worse than a guess on this paper. Nine blanks handed back 36 marks; nine guesses at chance would have brought roughly 2 of them back, for free.
