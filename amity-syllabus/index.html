Amity University Jaipur — MA Clinical Psychology

Full topic-by-topic syllabus, every paper broken down across both years. Transcribed from the official Amity (AIBAS) MA Psychology — Clinical/Counselling syllabus, batch 2021–23. Built 2026-06-02.

This is the program you're aiming at. It's a 2-year, 4-semester MA run by the Amity Institute of Behavioural & Allied Sciences (AIBAS). The first year is shared core (history, cognition, research, stats, biopsych, social, development, Indian psychology). The specialisation splits in Year 2: you pick the Clinical Psychology track (Semester III taught papers + Semester IV all field work). The taught clinical content — psychopathology, psychotherapy, assessment, biopsych — overlaps your MET/NIMHANS prep almost exactly, so what you're drilling now carries straight over.

Duration 2 years / 4 semesters Total credits ~118 (31 + 32 + 35 + 20) Each taught paper 3 credits (L2 T1) Classification DSM-V & ICD-10 Specialisation Clinical or Counselling

Year 1 · Semester I (31 credits)

Four core papers + Practicum I + a 9-credit basket of domain electives (Cognitive Psychology, Neurological Basis of Behaviour, Term Paper, Seminar) + French/German/Spanish/Chinese, Communication & Behavioural Science.

History & Schools of Psychology Core

MCP 101
I. Introduction: History of psychology; psychology as a science.
II. Structuralism: subject matter of psychology; methods of studying human behaviour.
III. Functionalism: subject matter; methods of studying behaviour.
IV. Associationism: Thorndikian associationism; Watsonian behaviorism.
V. Phenomenology & Gestalt: classical psychoanalysts — Freud, Adler, Jung; continuity theory.

Personality Theories Core

MCP 102 / 105
I. Introduction: nature of personality theory; grouping among theories; perspectives.
II. Dispositional: type & trait — Sheldon, Kretschmer, Allport, Cattell, Eysenck, Kobasa; alternative Five-Factor model.
III. Psychoanalytic: Freud (topographic, structural, instincts, defense mechanisms); Adler (superiority striving, birth order); Jung (collective unconscious); Erikson (ego, stages); Sullivan (personifications).
IV. Humanistic & phenomenological: Maslow's hierarchy; Rogers' person-centred; May's existential-analytic tradition.
V. Behavioural / cognitive: Skinner's radical behaviourism; Bandura's social-cognitive theory; Rotter's expectancy model; Kelly's personal constructs.

Research Methodology Core

MCP 103
I. Basics & ethics: meaning, purpose, dimensions, objectives, types & approaches of research; methods vs methodology; ethical problems and principles.
II. Components & process: nature of data; defining a research problem; criteria of a good problem; hypothesis — meaning, types, formulation, testing.
III. Research traditions: reviewing literature; parametric vs non-parametric characteristics; applications of psychological testing.
IV. Sampling: meaning, types, procedures, sample size, merits & limitations.
V. Methods & report writing: selecting statistical methods; interpreting data; writing the research report.

Psychological Measurement & Statistics Core

MCP 104
I. Introduction: history of testing; nature & significance of measurement; assessment vs measurement; levels of measurement.
II. Test construction: classification & characteristics of tests; steps to develop a test; ethics; item analysis (difficulty, discrimination, IRT).
III. Standardization: validity, reliability, methods of estimating each; test norms and their development.
IV. Descriptive statistics: central tendency & variability; correlation — product-moment, point-biserial, phi, biserial, tetrachoric, Spearman.
V. Inferential statistics: probability & normal curve; levels of significance; Type I & II errors; one- vs two-tailed; parametric & non-parametric tests; t-tests (independent, dependent); one-way / two-way ANOVA & F-test; MANOVA; post-hoc.

Practicum I Core lab

MCP 120 · perform & write any 6
Psychological Well-Being · Emotional Intelligence Scale · Beck Depression Inventory · Suicide-risk questionnaire · Drug Use (AUDIT) · 16 PF · Sentence Completion Test · Eysenck Personality Questionnaire · Adjustment Scale · Family/Home Environment Scale.

Cognitive Psychology Domain elective

MCP 105/102
I. Introduction & sensation: origin, methods & current status of cognitive psychology; sensation — meaning, types, sensation vs cognition.
II. Attention & perception: determinants, types & theories of attention; perception types, cues, theories (pattern recognition); illusions, delusions, hallucinations.
III. Learning, memory & forgetting: learning types & theories; memory types, models, retrieval; theories of forgetting.
IV. Language, thinking, problem solving: concept formation; language structure, comprehension & production; convergent/divergent, creative/critical thinking; problem-solving methods & hindrances.
V. Intelligence, reasoning, decision making: intelligence nature & theories; creativity; inductive/deductive reasoning, syllogisms; decision models, algorithms, heuristics.

Neurological Basis of Behaviour Domain elective

MCP 106
I. Biopsychology: nature, scope, ethics, divisions; methods of studying the brain (ablation, recording, stimulation, neurochemical); brain & spinal cord; CNS vs PNS.
II. Neural communication: neuron structure, types, conduction; synaptic transmission; neurotransmitters; supporting cells; blood-brain barrier.
III. Senses: cell structure, mitosis/meiosis; eye, tongue, nose; RNA/DNA; cerebral cortex & parallel processing; imaging — PET, CT, fMRI, ERP.
IV. Evolutionary perspectives: principles of evolution; reflexes, instincts; environmental influences; current research & controversies.
V. Neural mechanisms: brain & cognition (intelligence, memory, learning); endocrine system & hormones; neurological disorders — tumors, seizures, Parkinson's, Huntington's, Alzheimer's, MS; chromosomal & hereditary determinants.

Plus NTCC electives: Term Paper (MCP 130) and Seminar (MCP 145) — secondary-data review & presentation, ~3000 words each.

Year 1 · Semester II (32 credits)

Four core papers + Practicum II + Summer Internship + a 9-credit elective basket (Life Skills, Experimental Designs, Advanced Counselling Skills, Review Article) + Open Elective + language/communication.

Advanced Social Psychology Core

MCP 201
I. Introduction: nature & scope; contemporary, cultural & cross-cultural psychology; traditional perspectives; research methods.
II. Social influence: social facilitation, loafing; conformity, compliance, obedience; social power; reactance; social perception; attitude; aggression; prosocial behaviour.
III. Intergroup relations: group dynamics; leadership style & effectiveness; theories of intergroup relations; conflict & resolution.
IV. Applications in the real world: societal development in counselling; cyber-world issues; gender, poverty, marginalisation, social suffering; wellbeing in diverse contexts.
V. Applied social psychology: stereotyping, prejudice, discrimination & countering them; health, environment, law; personal space, crowding, territoriality.

Psychometrics Core

MCP 202
I. Psychological tests: meaning of assessment & psychometrics; history; core characteristics; classification; steps to develop a test; ethics.
II. Item analysis & test construction: item difficulty, discrimination, IRT; rational, empirical & factor-analytic test construction.
III. Standardization: reliability & validity and their types; methods of estimation; test norms.
IV. Qualitative methods: choosing a method; theory-to-text & text-to-theory; grounded theory; triangulation.
V. Non-parametric tests: nature & assumptions; distribution-free statistics; chi-square, contingency coefficient, median & sign test, Friedman; stats software.

Human Development in Social Context Core

MCP 203
I. Theoretical frames: psychoanalytic (Freud, Mahler, Winnicott, Kakar); individual-constructivist (Piaget, Kohlberg); social-constructivist (Vygotsky, Valsiner); life-cycle (Erikson) & life-span (Baltes); dynamic-systems & transpersonal theories.
II. Early & middle childhood: images of "the child"; childhood in India; multiple childhoods; physical, cognitive, socio-emotional, moral development; child policy (Anganwadi, RTE, child labour, mid-day meal).
III. Adolescence: issues & crises; does adolescence exist in India; cognition, moral & socio-emotional development; identity formation; negative identity, totalism; juvenile policy.
IV. Adulthood & old age: images & constructs in India; post-formal thought; mid-life crisis; intergenerational relations; policy on work, marriage, elder care.

Indian Approaches to Psychology Core

MCP 204
High-yield for you. MET weights Indian psychology heavily — this whole paper is the source material for the guna / yoga / consciousness questions that have been costing you.
I. Scope & methods: psychology in the Indian tradition; sources; research methods — experimental, phenomenological.
II. Centrality of consciousness: Advaita metaphysics; Buddhist phenomenology; four planes of consciousness; consciousness in Sāṁkhya-Yoga.
III. Mind–body complex: Vedic, Sāṁkhya-Yoga, Advaita Vedānta & Buddhist conceptions of mind.
IV. Self & personality: theories of the Self in Indian thought; Anattā & denial of self in Buddhism; self in Vedānta & Sāṁkhya-Yoga; personality in the Bhagavad Gītā & Āyurveda; psychometric studies of Guṇa (sattva, rajas, tamas).
V. Cognition & emotion: Śaṅkara on cognition & knowledge; Bharata on emotions & aesthetic moods; Rasa theory; Karma Yoga as means to liberation.

Practicum II Core lab

MCP 220 · perform & write any 6
Transactional Analysis · DBDA · Interest Inventory · MBTI · NEO-PI · Bender Gestalt Test · Stroop Test · Tower of London · PGI Memory Scale · Wisconsin Card Sorting.

Domain electives this semester: Life Skills (MCP 207), Experimental Designs in Behavioural Research (MCP 205 — ANOVA foundations, single-factor & factorial experiments, randomized complete block designs), Advanced Counselling Skills (MCP 206), Review Article (MCP 240). Plus the evaluated Summer Internship (72 hrs, marked in Sem III).

Year 2 · Semester III — Clinical Specialisation (35 credits)

This is where you choose Clinical. Semester III is the taught half of the specialisation: four clinical papers below, plus Summer-Internship evaluation, Practicum III, an Open Elective and a 9-credit domain-elective basket (Community Psychology, Behavioural Problems of Children & Adolescents, Guided Counselling, Scientific Research Paper).

Introduction to Clinical Psychology Clinical

MCP 301
I. Foundations: early & recent history; nature of the discipline; developing a professional identity — education, training, employment settings, sub-specialisations.
II. Psychodynamic approach: four psychoanalytic psychologies — drive, ego, object-relations, self-psychology; psychological defenses, regression, true/false self.
III. Other major approaches: behavioural & cognitive-behavioural; humanistic; existential; family systems; biological; bio-psycho-social integration.
IV. Clinical assessment: rationale & planning; clinical interviewing; intellectual/educational, personality/interpersonal & behavioural/psycho-diagnostic applications.

Psychopathology Clinical

MCP 302
Maps almost 1:1 onto your abnormal-psych prep. DSM-V & ICD-10 are the classificatory systems.
I. Classification & models: systems of classification; basic features of DSM-V & ICD-10 — similarities, differences, critical evaluation; major theoretical models.
II. Mood, anxiety, somatoform & behavioural syndromes: depression & bipolar; phobia, panic, OCD, PTSD, adjustment disorder; dissociative, somatoform & other neurotic disorders; eating & sleep disorders.
III. Psychotic spectrum: schizophrenia & its spectrum; delusional, brief & shared psychotic disorders; schizoaffective; culture-specific manifestations & organic overlay.
IV. Disorders of infancy, childhood & adolescence: specific developmental disorders of scholastic skills; pervasive developmental disorders; behavioural & emotional disorders; disorders of social functioning.

Psychotherapy Clinical

MCP 303
I. Foundations: becoming a psychotherapist — training & supervision; stages of therapy; modes (individual, group, couples, family); psychotherapy research; controversial issues.
II. Psychodynamic therapies: psychoanalytic; brief analytic; object-relations; interpersonal approaches.
III. Humanistic & transpersonal: client-centred; existential; Gestalt; transpersonal therapies.
IV. Behavioural & CBT: behavioural therapy; cognitive therapy (Beck); rational emotive behaviour therapy (Ellis).

Clinical Psychology: A Positive Psychology Approach Clinical

MCP 304
I. Introduction: positive psychology & counselling; salutogenic vs pathogenic models; Indian & Western traditions.
II. Positive emotional & cognitive states: principles of pleasure; positive emotions & health; emotional intelligence; optimism, hope, self-efficacy; wisdom, courage, faith; flow & spirituality.
III. Subjective well-being & quality of life: the fully functioning individual; life satisfaction, happiness, meaning in life.
IV. Positive relationships: mindfulness; positive traits & coping; love, compassion, forgiveness, altruism, gratitude, empathy; family/marital application.
V. Applications: ageing, health, work, mental health, stress management, communities.

Practicum III (MCP 320): behavioural assessment of children, Adolescent Coping Scale, Social Support, Body Image, Abstinence Self-Efficacy, Sociometry, 16 PF, TAT/CAT, depression screening, Learning-Disabilities assessment, DISC-IV. Domain electives: Community Psychology (MCP 309), Behavioural Problems of Children & Adolescents (MCP 310 — incl. learning disabilities: dyslexia, dysgraphia, dyscalculia, dyspraxia), Guided Counselling (MCP 375), Scientific Research Paper (MCP 365).

If you pick the Counselling track instead — show the 4 Sem-III papers

Counselling specialisation Counselling

Methods & Approaches in Counselling (MCP 305): psychodynamic, behaviouristic, humanistic, existential & Indian spiritual/yogic approaches; dynamic methods; cognitive/non-directive/directive; psychodrama; group, peer & co-counselling.
Assessment & Research in Counselling (MCP 306): psychological measurement; test development; research methods; counselling in India.
Areas & Related Disciplines of Counselling (MCP 307): educational, vocational, health, paediatric, cross-cultural, rehabilitation, crisis/trauma, substance-abuse & psychiatric counselling; links to social work, community & rehab psych, TA, NLP.
Counselling for Diverse & Vulnerable Population (MCP 308): children, adolescents, elderly, women & minority populations — risk factors, effects, interventions.

Year 2 · Semester IV — Clinical Field Work (20 credits)

All practical. No taught papers — Semester IV is supervised field work and the dissertation. This is the placement-heavy half the program is built around.

Clinical specialisation — field work Clinical

Conduction of Supervised Workshops (MCP 481, 4 cr): 96 hrs; attend & run workshops, conceptualise client concerns, write reports.
Internship in Clinical Setting (MCP 482, 5 cr): 120 hrs; block placement in hospitals / therapeutic centres; patient logbook.
Internship in NGO / Rehabilitation Centre (MCP 483, 5 cr): 120 hrs; block placement; client logbook.
Dissertation (MCP 455, 6 cr): empirical research thesis — literature review, design, data collection, analysis, discussion.

Counselling track mirrors this: Supervised Workshops (MCP 484), Internship in Community Setting (MCP 485), Internship in NGO/Rehab (MCP 486), Dissertation (MCP 455).

Legend & what this means for you

Core shared first-year + labs Clinical the track you want Counselling the alternative split
  • Year 1 is all transferable. Cognition, biopsych/neuro, research methodology, stats, social, development and Indian psychology are exactly your MET/NIMHANS sections — you're already revising this material.
  • Indian Approaches (Sem II) is the formal home of the guna / yoga / consciousness content that's been the soft spot in your mocks. Worth over-preparing.
  • The clinical depth lands in Year 2: Psychopathology (DSM-V/ICD-10), Psychotherapy (psychodynamic → CBT/REBT), Introduction to Clinical Psychology and a positive-psychology paper, then a full semester of supervised placements + thesis.
  • RCI note: this MA is the recognised feeder. The licence to practise as a "Clinical Psychologist" comes from the 2-year M.Phil in Clinical Psychology stacked on top — Amity runs both (AIBAS ran Rajasthan's first RCI-licensed M.Phil from 2011). MA → M.Phil → RCI registration.
