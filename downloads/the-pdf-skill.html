1 · The move, in three steps

There is no setup before this and no cleanup after. The entire thing is three steps, and you only do the first two. The third happens on its own.

1

Send the PDF

Attach the PDF in the chat. Past paper, a chapter, a syllabus, a notes dump, a screenshot of a question, anything. It doesn't have to be clean. Scanned, rotated, badly OCR'd, half-highlighted: all fine. It reads it as-is.

2

Say where it goes and what it should become

One sentence. "Make this a note in the app." "Turn this into a mock." "Add this to the tracker." You don't have to be precise; if you just say "put this somewhere useful," it picks. But the more you say, the closer it lands. Section 3 has the exact lines.

3

It builds it and pushes it live (you do nothing)

It reads the PDF, writes the note or builds the mock to full depth, puts it in the right folder, and publishes it to the site. A minute or two later it sends you the link. You open it on your phone. That's the end of it.

The key thing to trust: you are not asking for a draft you then have to edit. You are asking for the finished, live thing. The reply you get back is a URL, not homework.

2 · Where a PDF can land (the four places)

A PDF can become one of four things. You don't memorise this, you just need to know it exists so you know what to ask for. When in doubt, ask for notes; you can always say "now also make a mock from it" after.

You wantIt becomesLands in
To study / revise the contentA note in the Notes-for-Exam book library, written to depth, searchable, works offline.The Notes app
To practise on it / test yourselfA self-contained mock: real questions, scored, with a full explanation under every one.Tests
To track an admission / newsA row in the admissions tracker or a card on the news board (dates, fees, seats, links).Tracker / News
To keep a copy that works offlineFolded into the offline pack so it's there on a flight or with no signal.Offline pack

All four are the same move under the hood. The only thing that changes is the one sentence you say in step 2.

3 · Exactly what to say (copy these)

Send the PDF, then send one of these. Plain language. You can change the words; these just work.

To turn it into notes

Notes"Turn this PDF into notes and put it in the app. Write it to full depth, don't just summarise it."

If it's one chapter of a known book, say so: "This is the Pinel chapter on the brain, add it to the notes app at proper depth." Naming the book helps it match the look and depth of the others.

To turn it into a mock / test

Mock"Make a mock out of this. Use the real questions from the paper, score it, and put a full explanation under every question."

If the PDF is a real past paper with an answer key, say that, because it changes how carefully it's handled: "This is a real past paper, the answers are marked in bold. Build it verbatim, don't invent questions, and flag anything where the key looks wrong."

To add it to the tracker

Tracker"This is the admission notice for [college]. Add it to the tracker with the dates, fees, seats and the link."

When you're not sure

Let it pick"Here's a PDF. Read it and put it wherever it's most useful on the site, then tell me what you did and where it is."
One PDF, both things. You can ask for notes and a mock from the same PDF: "Make notes from this and also build a mock from it." Or come back later: "remember that paper I sent, now make a mock from it too."

4 · What "to depth" means

This is the part that makes it worth doing at all, so it's worth knowing what you're actually getting. "To depth" is the default, you don't have to ask for it. It means:

  • For a note: it doesn't paste the PDF text back. It rewrites the content as proper exam notes, explains the concepts, keeps the figures and diagrams where they matter, and writes it so you can actually revise from it, not just re-read it.
  • For a mock: every question gets a real explanation underneath, not just "the answer is C." It explains why C is right and why each wrong option is the trap it is. So a mock you got wrong teaches you, instead of just scoring you.
  • For a real past paper: it pulls the questions exactly as written, uses the official answer key, and tells you honestly if a key looks debatable instead of quietly papering over it.
You can always push for more. If a note feels thin, say "go deeper on this one, it's too shallow." If you want the explanations richer, say "add the trap under each wrong option." Depth is a dial you can turn after the fact.

5 · After it's live: checking it, and fixing it

You get a link. Open it. If something's off, you don't fix it yourself, you just say what's wrong in plain words and it redoes that part. Examples of exactly what to say:

What you noticeWhat you say
A note is too shallow"That note's too thin, go deeper, explain it properly."
A question's answer looks wrong"Question 14 in that mock, I think the answer is wrong, check it against the key."
It put it in the wrong place"Move that into the Notes app instead, it shouldn't be a mock."
The page won't load / looks old"That page is showing the old version, force it to update."
You want it gone"Take that one down, I don't need it."
If a fresh page looks stale on your phone: the app caches things to work offline, so a brand-new page can show the old version for a minute. Pull to refresh, or hit the refresh (↻) button. If it's still stuck, just say "force-update that page" and it'll bump the cache so it reloads clean.

6 · Small things that make the result better

None of these are required. The move works without them. They just make it land closer the first time, so you do fewer "redo this" rounds.

  • Name the source. "This is the IHBAS 2024 paper" beats "here's a PDF." It knows your papers and matches the style of the ones already up.
  • Say if it's verbatim. For a real paper, "build it exactly, don't invent questions" tells it to reproduce, not rewrite. Important for past papers.
  • Say which exam it's for. "This is for the Amity Noida MA" vs "for NIMHANS" changes how it's framed and where it files it.
  • Send the answer key too, if you have it. Even a screenshot of the bold answers. It'll use the real key instead of working out answers itself.
  • One PDF per message is cleanest. If you dump five at once, say what each is, or just send them one at a time and say "same as before" for each.

7 · What you never have to do

This is the reassurance part. The whole point of the skill is that the boring machinery is invisible. You never touch any of this:

You never...Because...
Write or run PythonIt writes and runs any script itself, you never see it.
Touch git, GitHub, or "push" anythingPublishing live is part of the move. It does it.
Set anything up or install anythingIt's already set up. You're just feeding it PDFs.
Open a code editor or edit HTMLYou describe what's wrong in words. It edits the code.
Format the PDF or clean it firstMessy, scanned, rotated, half-OCR'd: it handles it.
Wait around or babysit itYou send and walk away. It pings you the link when it's live.

If you ever find yourself about to do one of these, stop, you're doing its job. Say what you want in a sentence instead.
